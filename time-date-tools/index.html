What these time and date tools cover

Time questions come in a few shapes: how long between two dates, what date falls a number of days away, what time it is in another city right now, what a meeting time means in another zone, how long until an event, and how long something took. These five tools cover each shape — date arithmetic with real calendar rules, time zone conversion with the offset in force on the day you enter, live clocks for major cities, a countdown that ticks in your browser tab, and a stopwatch with laps and milliseconds.

All of it happens in your browser. The clocks and timers use your device clock, and the date and time zone maths uses the same IANA time zone database your operating system uses — so daylight saving is handled automatically and nothing you enter is uploaded or stored anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Do the clocks use my device time?

Yes. The World Clock, Countdown Timer and Stopwatch all run on your device clock, so they are as accurate as the clock on your own device.

Are leap years handled?

Yes. The Date Calculator uses the real calendar, so a span covering 29 February counts the extra day, and the century rules (1900 not a leap year, 2000 a leap year) are applied correctly.

How does the Time Zone Converter handle daylight saving?

It calculates the offset for the exact date you enter, using the same IANA time zone database your operating system uses — so a meeting booked three months away is checked against the rules in force on that day, not today's offset.

Is anything I enter stored?

No. Every tool on this page runs in your browser; nothing you enter is uploaded or stored.

Do these tools work on my phone?

Yes — every time and date tool works in a mobile browser, with no app to install.
