What this video tool covers

Compression is the most common video job there is: a phone recording too big for an attachment, a screen capture too heavy for an upload form, a clip that takes forever to send in a chat. The Video Compressor handles that one job properly — it decodes your file in the browser, re-encodes it at a lower bitrate with the quality preset and resolution you pick, carries the audio track over, and downloads the smaller file as a WebM video.

It works with the formats browsers can play: MP4 (H.264), WebM and MOV in most browsers. The download is a WebM file because browsers record WebM, not MP4 — WebM plays in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and VLC, and any video app can convert it to MP4 afterwards if that format is needed. Compression takes about as long as the video itself, because the video is re-encoded in real time, and the progress bar shows exactly where it is.

Frequently asked questions

What video formats can I compress?

Any format your browser can play and decode: MP4 (H.264), WebM and MOV in most browsers. If a file will not load, the browser cannot decode that format or codec.

Why is the compressed file a WebM?

Browsers record WebM, not MP4 — there is no MP4 recorder in Chrome, Edge or Firefox. The compressor downloads a WebM file, which plays in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and VLC; convert it to MP4 afterwards in any video app if you need that format.

How long does compression take?

About as long as the video itself, plus a few seconds. The compressor re-encodes your video in real time, so a two-minute video takes about two minutes.

Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. Every tool on this page runs in your browser; your file is decoded, re-encoded and downloaded on your own device, and nothing is uploaded or stored.

Do the video tools work on my phone?

Yes — the video compressor works in a mobile browser, with no app to install. Compression takes about as long as the video itself, so a short clip compresses fastest on a phone.
