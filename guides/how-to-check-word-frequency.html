How to Check Word Frequency

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 17 November 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026

Word frequency shows which words you use most — and how often. Paste any text into a frequency counter and you get a sorted table of every word, its count and its share of the total. It takes ten seconds, and it tells you what a text is really about, which words you repeat too much, and whether your writing stays on topic.

What word frequency is

Word frequency is simply a count: how many times each word appears in a piece of text. A frequency table lists the words from most to least used, usually with a percentage showing each word's share of the total. The most frequent words in almost any English text are small function words — the, and, of, to — which is why a good counter lets you hide them and look at the words that carry meaning.

The idea is older than computers. Linguists have counted words by hand for centuries, and the first computer concordances in the 1950s did the same job with punched cards. What changed is speed: a browser tool counts thousands of words instantly, and the table it produces is the same kind of evidence a researcher would once have spent days compiling.

Why writers and editors count words

The most common reason is repetition. Every writer has favourite words — really, just, very, actually, important — and they creep into a draft unnoticed. A frequency table makes them visible: if really appears forty times in a two-thousand-word essay, it stands out at the top of the list, and cutting most of them improves the writing immediately.

The second reason is topic. The most frequent content words in a text are usually its subject, so a quick frequency check shows whether a piece stays on topic or drifts. Teachers use it to check essays, editors use it before publishing, and SEO writers use it to check that a page mentions its topic clearly without stuffing the same phrase into every paragraph.

Check word frequency in four steps

  1. Copy your text. Select the text you want to analyse — an essay, a blog post, a chapter, a report — and copy it. You can paste from a document, a web page or an email; formatting does not matter, because only the words are counted.
  2. Paste it into the counter. Open the Word Frequency Counter and paste your text into the box. There is no upload and no sign-up — the counting happens in your browser, so your text never leaves your device.
  3. Set the options. Leave Ignore case on so Word and word count together. Tick Ignore common words to hide the, and, of and similar filler words, leaving the table full of the words that carry meaning.
  4. Read the table. The words are sorted from most to least frequent, with a count and a percentage for each. Look at the top ten: they should match what the text is about. Copy the table to paste it into a spreadsheet if you want to keep it.

Reading the numbers honestly

A high count is not automatically a problem. A recipe repeats its ingredients, a technical report repeats its key terms, and a story repeats its characters — that is the text doing its job. The question is whether the repeated words are the right ones. If the top of the table is full of filler words and hedges, the writing needs tightening. If it is full of topic words, the text is focused.

Percentages help here. A single word taking more than three or four percent of a long text is worth a look, and a phrase repeated in every paragraph usually deserves a synonym or a restructure. But there is no magic number: a short text will always have higher percentages than a long one, because there are fewer words to share the total. Compare within the text, not against a rule.

Other ways to check word frequency

A word processor can help in a basic way: most have a find function that counts occurrences of one word at a time, which works if you only want to check a single term. Spreadsheets can count words with formulas, but setting that up takes longer than the analysis itself. Dedicated text-analysis software does far more — collocations, readability scores, comparisons between texts — but it is overkill for a quick check.

For a one-off look at a single text, the browser counter is the fastest route: paste, click, read the table. Use heavier software only when you need to compare many documents or track frequencies over time.

Questions people ask

What is a good word frequency for a keyword?

There is no fixed target. The keyword should appear clearly among the top content words, but forcing it into every paragraph reads badly and search engines understand topics through context, not just repetition.

Why are the, and and of always at the top?

They are the most common words in English, so they top almost every frequency table. Tick Ignore common words to hide them and see the meaningful words instead.

Does the counter treat Word and word as the same word?

Yes, when Ignore case is on, which is the default. Untick it if capitalisation matters to your analysis.

Can I check word frequency in other languages?

Yes, the counting works for any alphabet. The Ignore common words list is English only, so leave that option off for other languages.
