How to make an image square

Square is the shape the internet keeps coming back to: posts, profile pictures, thumbnails and product grids all favour it. Here is how to make an image square, whether to crop or pad, and how to keep the result looking like it was always square.

Why square still matters

Instagram posts, profile pictures, marketplace thumbnails, app icons and product grids all favour square images. A square displays identically everywhere, with no awkward cropping by the platform — which is why making the square yourself beats letting an app guess.

The classic problem is a landscape photo uploaded to a square slot: the platform crops the left and right edges, often cutting off the subject. Making the square yourself means you decide what stays — crop deliberately, or pad so that nothing is lost at all.

Crop or pad: which to choose

Crop when the edges do not matter: a landscape where the subject sits in the middle, a group photo with space at the sides, a screenshot with wide margins. Cropping keeps the image full-bleed — no borders, no filler — and the result looks like it was always square.

Pad when the edges matter: a product photo where the item touches the frame, a diagram or poster where cutting would remove information, a tall portrait where cropping would cut off a head or feet. Padding adds space instead of removing picture, and a colour matched to the photo's background looks intentional. Transparent padding is the cleanest option for graphics: the square has see-through sides, so it sits on any background.

How to make an image square

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the Square Image Maker: drop the image in, choose crop or pad, pick a padding colour if you pad, and download. Transparent padding is available for PNG and WebP.
  2. On Windows, with Photos. Open the image in the Photos app, click Edit, then Crop, and choose the square aspect ratio. Drag the crop box to frame the subject, then save a copy.
  3. On a Mac, with Preview. Open the image in Preview, choose the Rectangular Selection tool, hold Shift while dragging to force a square selection, then choose Tools, then Crop. Save as a new file to keep the original.
  4. Check the result at the size it will be used. A square that looks right at full size can feel tight when displayed small. If the subject touches the edges, pad instead of cropping — or crop a little less.

Square for marketplaces and listings

Marketplaces are where square pays off most. Etsy, eBay and Amazon all display product photos in square or near-square thumbnails, and a landscape photo gets cropped by the platform — often cutting off the product itself. A square upload displays whole, fills the thumbnail, and looks consistent next to other listings.

Padding is the safer choice for products: it keeps the whole item visible and centres it in the frame, which is exactly how a product photo should look. A white or transparent pad matches most marketplace backgrounds, and the square file uploads without any further cropping. For a shop with dozens of products, square padded photos give the whole catalogue a consistent, professional look.

Square images and quality

Cropping to square removes pixels, so the result is smaller than the original — a 4000×3000 photo becomes 3000×3000. That is still plenty for screens, and the file gets smaller too. Padding keeps every original pixel and adds new ones, so the file is slightly larger and the dimensions grow to the longer side.

One detail to watch: padding with a colour that does not match looks like a mistake. White padding on a dark photo draws the eye to the border; a colour sampled from the photo's own edge blends in. If you cannot match the colour, transparent padding (PNG) is the safer choice — the page behind the image provides the background.

One more detail: the size of the square matters as much as its shape. A 1000×1000 square is the common minimum for marketplace listings, and most platforms accept up to 5000×5000. If your original is smaller than 1000 on its long side, enlarge it first with the Image Enlarger and then square it — the order keeps the final image sharp.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between crop and pad?

Crop trims the longer edges away, so the square is cut from the middle of your photo. Pad keeps the whole photo and adds space on the shorter sides, so nothing is cut off.

Why did my transparent padding turn white?

JPEG cannot store transparency. If you tick transparent padding and download as JPEG, the padding is filled with your chosen colour instead. Use PNG or WebP for transparency.

My photo is already square — what happens?

Nothing changes: cropping and padding a square image both return the same picture. The tool is only useful for rectangles.
