How to Use a Stopwatch Online

An online stopwatch does exactly what a physical one does: it counts up from zero, records laps, and stops when you tell it to. The difference is that it lives in your browser — no app, no download, no sign-up. This guide shows how to use it and how to get accurate times from it.

The controls

The stopwatch has three controls: Start and Pause on one button, Lap, and Reset. The display shows minutes, seconds and milliseconds, and hours appear automatically once the stopwatch passes 60 minutes. Laps are listed below the display with their total time and their split — the time since the previous lap.

Timing uses the browser's high-resolution clock rather than counting screen updates, so the stopwatch keeps accurate time even if the tab is busy or you switch to another tab. The display refreshes about 30 times a second, which is why the milliseconds look smooth without flickering.

What laps are for

A lap records the time at a moment you choose, without stopping the stopwatch. Runners use laps for each circuit, swimmers for each length, and cooks for each stage of a recipe. Each lap shows two numbers: the total time since the stopwatch started, and the split — the time since the previous lap — so you can compare segments directly.

Laps stay on screen until you press Reset, so you can read them back at the end of a session. There is no limit on the number of laps, and no need to write anything down — the table keeps every lap in order, with the fastest and slowest splits easy to spot.

Use the stopwatch in four steps

  1. Open the stopwatch. Open the Stopwatch — it starts at 00:00.000, ready to go. There is nothing to install and nothing to set up.
  2. Click Start. The clock starts counting up in minutes, seconds and milliseconds. Click the same button again to pause, and again to resume from the same time.
  3. Click Lap to record a split. At any moment, click Lap to record the time without stopping the stopwatch. Each lap shows its total time and its split since the previous lap.
  4. Click Reset when you are done. Reset clears the time and all laps, ready for the next session. Read the lap table before resetting if you want to keep the times.

Stopwatch vs timer

A stopwatch counts up from zero; a timer counts down to zero. They sound interchangeable, but they suit different jobs. Use a stopwatch when you want to measure how long something takes — a run, a task, a meeting. Use a timer when you want to stop after a set time — a Pomodoro session, a cooking step, a break. The two tools answer opposite questions: how long did that take, and how long is left.

This site has both. The Pomodoro Timer counts down in focused 25-minute sessions with breaks, which suits deep work; this stopwatch counts up with laps, which suits measuring. If you are timing a workout, use the stopwatch and record a lap per set. If you are trying to focus for half an hour without distractions, use the Pomodoro Timer instead.

Getting accurate times

The stopwatch is accurate enough for workouts, cooking, study sessions and everyday timing. The underlying clock is precise to a fraction of a millisecond, and the display updates about 30 times a second, so what you see is never more than a few hundredths of a second behind the true time.

Reaction time is the real limit. A human finger on a Start or Lap button is accurate to roughly a tenth of a second at best, and a browser tab is not a certified timing device. For anything where hundredths of a second decide the result, use dedicated timing equipment — for everything else, this stopwatch is more than accurate enough.

For the most accurate starts, use the keyboard where you can: the Start button can be triggered with the keyboard once it has focus, which removes the small delay of moving a mouse or a finger to the screen. The difference is only a fraction of a second, but for short timings it is the largest source of error you can control.

Questions people ask

How do I use an online stopwatch?

Click Start to begin timing, Pause to stop the clock, Lap to record a time without stopping, and Reset to clear everything and start over.

What is a lap?

A lap records the time at a moment you choose, without stopping the stopwatch. Each lap shows the total time and the split since the previous lap.

Does it keep running if I switch tabs?

Yes, the timing keeps running, because it is based on the clock rather than on screen updates. Closing the tab stops it.

How accurate is it?

It uses the browser's high-resolution clock, precise to a fraction of a millisecond. Reaction time on the buttons is the real limit, at roughly a tenth of a second.
