How to convert HEIC to JPG on Mac

Your Mac is the one computer that opens HEIC photos without a fuss — and yet the form, the Windows computer or the website you are sending the photo to still wants a JPG. The good news: the Mac already has three built-in ways to convert, and none of them needs an app.

Why your Mac still needs JPG sometimes

Macs have understood HEIC natively since macOS High Sierra, so Preview and Photos open iPhone photos with no extra software and no complaints. That makes the Mac the easiest place in the world to look at a HEIC photo — and it hides the problem until the moment you try to send it somewhere else.

Windows computers, many application forms, marketplace listings and plenty of websites still refuse HEIC outright, or show it as a blank file. So the photo that opens perfectly on your Mac still has to become a JPG before it leaves it. The conversion itself is simple, and because it is built into macOS, you do not need to download a converter, create an account or upload your photos to a stranger's server to do it.

Method 1: use Preview (best for one photo, or a few)

Preview is the Mac's built-in image viewer, and it is the most precise way to convert a single photo, because you choose the exact quality yourself.

  1. Open the photo in Preview. Double-click the HEIC file — Preview is the default, so it should open straight away.
  2. Choose File, then Export. A save dialog appears with a format menu.
  3. Pick JPEG as the format. Set the quality slider to around 90–92% for a good balance of size and sharpness.
  4. Choose where to save it and click Save. Preview creates a new JPG file and leaves your original HEIC untouched.

Preview can also convert several photos at once: select the files in Finder, open them all in Preview, select the thumbnails in the sidebar, then choose File, then Export Selected Images, and pick JPEG. Every selected photo exports as a JPG in one go.

Method 2: use Finder Quick Actions (fastest for a batch)

For a folder full of photos, Quick Actions is the quickest built-in route — no app opens at all.

  1. Select the HEIC files in Finder. Click one, then Shift-click or Command-click the rest.
  2. Right-click the selection. In the menu that appears, choose Quick Actions, then Convert Image.
  3. Pick JPEG and a size. Choose JPEG as the format, and keep the image size as Actual Size unless you also want the photos smaller.
  4. Convert. The Mac creates a JPG copy of every selected photo in the same folder, and the originals stay exactly where they were.

This method appeared in macOS Mojave, so it works on any reasonably recent Mac. If you do not see Convert Image in the Quick Actions menu, your macOS is older — use Preview instead, or the browser method below.

Method 3: export from the Photos app

If your photos live in the Photos app rather than in a folder, export them from there. Select the photos, choose File, then Export, then Export Photos. In the dialog, set the photo kind to JPEG and the quality to High, then choose a folder. The Photos app writes JPG copies and leaves your library untouched — nothing in your library changes, and the HEIC originals stay in Photos for the future.

This is the method to use when the photos are already organised in albums. One thing to watch: exporting from Photos re-encodes the photo, so exporting the same photo several times, each time from a previous export, slowly loses quality. Always export from the original, and keep the original.

Method 4: convert in the browser (best for a very large batch)

When there are dozens of photos, the browser method is the least fiddly. Open our HEIC to JPG tool in any browser on your Mac, pick all the photos at once, choose JPG and download everything as one ZIP file. Nothing is installed and nothing is uploaded — the conversion runs on your Mac. This is also the simplest answer if you ever need to explain the same job to a Windows user: our guide How to Convert HEIC to JPG on Windows 11 covers the PC side, and the browser method works identically there.

What about quality, Live Photos and originals?

Converting to JPG does lose a little quality on paper — HEIC compresses more efficiently, so the same photo as a JPG is usually a bigger file, and JPG is a lossy format. In practice, at around 92% quality the difference is not visible in normal viewing: the pixels are the same, only the file encoding changes. Preview's quality slider and the Photos app's High setting both land in that range. What you should not do is convert the same photo back and forth several times, because every re-encode discards a little more detail.

Live Photos need one extra thought. A Live Photo is a still image plus a short video clip, and converting it to JPG keeps the still frame — the motion part does not survive as a JPG. If the movement matters, keep the original Live Photo as well, and send the JPG only where a still image is what is asked for. And whatever method you use, keep the original HEIC until the converted file is confirmed working: conversion should add a usable copy, never destroy the only one you have.

Questions people ask

Does Mac open HEIC files?

Yes. Macs have opened HEIC files natively since macOS High Sierra, so Preview and Photos display them with no extra software. The problem is not opening the photo on your Mac — it is sending it somewhere else. Windows computers, many forms and plenty of websites still refuse HEIC, so the photo usually needs to be a JPG before it leaves your Mac.

How do I convert HEIC to JPG on Mac without installing anything?

Open the photo in Preview, choose File, then Export, pick JPEG as the format and set the quality slider. For several photos at once, right-click them in Finder, choose Quick Actions, then Convert Image, and pick JPEG — the Mac converts the whole selection without opening any app.

Can I convert many HEIC files to JPG at once on Mac?

Yes. In Finder, select all the HEIC files, right-click, choose Quick Actions, then Convert Image, and pick JPEG — every selected file converts at once. Preview can also export several selected images together. For a very large batch, a browser converter converts them all at once and downloads the results as one ZIP file.

Will converting HEIC to JPG reduce quality?

A little, but not visibly at a good quality setting. HEIC compresses more efficiently than JPG, so the same photo as a JPG is usually a bigger file, and JPG is a lossy format. At around 92% quality the difference is not visible in normal viewing — the pixels are the same, only the file encoding changes.
