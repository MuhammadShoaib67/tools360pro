How to crop an image into a circle

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 17 November 2026 · Updated 17 November 2026

Almost every profile picture on the internet is a circle — and almost every app crops it for you, badly. Cropping the circle yourself means you choose what is inside it. Here is how to crop an image into a circle, how to get the framing right, and why the file must be a PNG.

Where circle photos are used

Messaging apps, social profiles, comment sections, team pages and email avatars all display photos inside a round frame. Uploading a square photo works — the app crops it for you — but the app chooses the crop, and it often cuts off the top of a head or an important detail at the edge.

Cropping the circle yourself means you choose what is inside it. Centre the face, leave a little room around the head, and the result looks deliberate instead of accidental. The same applies to logos and icons shown in round frames on websites and app stores.

Getting the crop right

The circle is cut from the centre of your image, and its size is a percentage of the shorter side. At 100% the circle uses the full width of a portrait photo or the full height of a landscape one. Shrink it to zoom in — useful when the face is small in the frame and you want it to fill the circle.

Leave breathing room. A face that touches the edge of the circle looks cramped once it is displayed small, and a little space around the head reads as confidence rather than a mistake. If the subject is not centred in the original photo, crop the image first so the subject sits in the middle, then circle-crop the result.

How to crop an image into a circle

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the Circle Crop tool: drop the image in, set the circle size, and download. The file is always PNG, so the corners stay transparent.
  2. In PowerPoint. Insert the picture, select it, then choose Picture Format, then Crop, then Crop to Shape, then Oval. Drag the crop handles to a square first so the oval is a true circle, then right-click and save the picture.
  3. With GIMP (free, Windows/Mac/Linux). Open the image, choose the Ellipse Select tool, hold Shift while dragging to force a circle, then Select, then Invert, then Edit, then Clear. Export as PNG to keep the transparency.
  4. Check the result on the background it will sit on. A circle with transparent corners takes the background of wherever it is placed. Preview it on a dark background as well as a light one — a white edge that looked fine on white may show on dark.

Circle crops on websites

If the circle is for a website you control, there is a simpler option: no crop at all. A square image with the CSS rule border-radius: 50% displays as a perfect circle in every browser, and the corners are hidden by the page rather than cut from the file. That keeps the original square intact for other uses, and the circle is always perfectly centred.

The trade-off is control. CSS crops from the centre of the image, so a face that sits off-centre will be cut — and on platforms you do not control, like social profiles, there is no CSS to help. For those, a real circle crop in PNG is the answer: you choose the framing, and the file itself is a circle wherever it goes.

PNG transparency, explained

A circle has corners, and the corners have to be something: transparent, in this case. PNG is the format that stores transparency, which is why circle crops are always saved as PNG — a JPG would fill the corners with white, and the “circle” would show as a white square on any coloured background.

Some apps show a transparent PNG with a checkerboard pattern in their preview; that is just how transparency is displayed, and the file itself is correct. When the image is placed on a page or in a profile, the page's own background shows through the corners, and the photo reads as a clean circle.

Questions people ask

Why is the download always PNG?

Only PNG (and WebP) can store transparency. A JPG would fill the circle's corners with white. PNG keeps the corners transparent, so the photo reads as a clean circle on any background.

My preview shows a checkerboard — is that in the file?

No. The checkerboard is how viewers display transparency; it is not part of the image. On a real page, the background shows through the corners instead.

What size should a profile picture be?

Most platforms display profile pictures at 200–500 pixels, but accept much larger uploads. A circle of 500–1000 pixels is a safe choice: sharp on every screen without a heavy file.
