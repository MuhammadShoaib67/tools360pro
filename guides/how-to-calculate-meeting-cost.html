How to Calculate Meeting Cost

A meeting costs whatever the people in it are paid for the time they spend there. Multiply the attendees by the average hourly rate by the duration, and you have the price — usually far higher than anyone expected. This guide shows the formula, works through examples, and explains how to use the number to run fewer, shorter, cheaper meetings.

The meeting cost formula

The maths is simple: total cost equals the number of attendees times the average hourly rate times the duration in hours. Eight people at 50 an hour for one hour is 8 times 50 times 1, which is 400. For a 90-minute meeting, the duration is 1.5 hours, so the same group costs 600. For a 30-minute meeting, it is 0.5 hours, so the same group costs 200.

The formula works for any meeting, from a two-person call to an all-hands gathering. The only inputs are people, rate and time — and all three are usually known before the meeting starts, which is what makes the calculation useful. You can price a meeting before it happens, not just after.

What hourly rate to use

The only judgement call is the hourly rate. A plain salary rate gives a conservative figure: divide an annual salary by the number of working hours in a year. A loaded rate adds benefits, office costs and other overheads, and is often 1.5 to 2 times the plain rate. Neither is wrong — they answer different questions, and the loaded rate is closer to what the meeting really costs the organisation.

When attendees earn different amounts, use the average across the group. For a rough estimate that is accurate enough; for an exact figure, calculate each group separately and add the totals. Either way, the result is only as honest as the rate you enter — a low rate makes every meeting look cheap.

Calculate a meeting cost in four steps

  1. Count the attendees. Count everyone invited, including people who only attend for part of the meeting — if they are there for half of it, count them as half a person.
  2. Work out the average hourly rate. Use a plain salary rate for a conservative figure, or a loaded rate including benefits and overheads for a fuller one. Average across the group if pay varies.
  3. Enter the duration. Convert minutes to hours: 30 minutes is 0.5 hours, 90 minutes is 1.5 hours. Then open the Meeting Cost Calculator and enter the three numbers.
  4. Read the total. The calculator shows the total cost, the cost per person and the cost per minute. For a recurring meeting, multiply the total by the number of times it repeats.

What the number leaves out

The formula counts the time people spend in the meeting itself. The true cost is higher, because meetings also cost preparation time, travel time, and the minutes it takes to refocus on real work afterwards. Research on attention consistently finds that returning to a task after an interruption takes time — and a meeting is the largest interruption in most working days.

Treat the calculated cost as a floor, not a ceiling. If the floor already looks expensive, the real price is worse. That is the point of the exercise: the number is not meant to be exact, it is meant to be visible. An invisible cost is never questioned; a visible one is.

How to cut the cost

The number is most useful before a meeting is scheduled, not after. A 30-minute meeting with four people costs a quarter of a 60-minute meeting with eight — same topic, very different price. Cutting the attendee list to the people who actually need to decide, and cutting the default hour to 30 minutes, are the two cheapest savings in any organisation.

It also settles whether a meeting should exist at all. If the answer could be an email, a shared document or a five-minute call, the meeting is the expensive option. Calculate the cost, compare it with the decision the meeting is supposed to produce, and let the number argue for you.

Questions people ask

What is the formula for meeting cost?

Multiply the number of attendees by the average hourly rate by the duration in hours. Five people at 40 an hour for one hour costs 200.

Should I use salary or a loaded rate?

A plain salary rate gives a conservative figure. A loaded rate adds benefits and overheads and is often 1.5 to 2 times the salary rate. Pick the one that matches your question.

How much does a recurring meeting cost?

Calculate one meeting and multiply by the number of times it repeats. A weekly one-hour meeting costs 52 times the single figure over a year.

Does the calculator count preparation time?

No. It counts the time in the meeting itself. Preparation and refocus time are real costs too, so the true cost is higher.
