How to Check a Page for Basic SEO

Before you publish a page, spend five minutes checking the basics: is the title the right length, does the description sell the click, is there exactly one H1, does the keyword appear where it should? These small checks catch the mistakes that quietly cost rankings. This guide walks through a basic SEO check you can run on any page or draft.

The five things every page needs

A unique title of around 60 characters, with the primary keyword near the front. A unique meta description of 150 to 160 characters that sells the click. Exactly one H1 that matches the title's topic. The keyword in the title, the H1 and the early body text. And enough content to answer the query — thin pages rarely rank for anything competitive.

Those five checks take five minutes and catch the majority of on-page mistakes: the page with no title at all, the description copied from another page, the draft with three H1s, the article that never mentions its own keyword. They are the floor, not the ceiling — but a page that fails them is not ready to publish.

What a basic check does not cover

A checklist cannot judge whether the writing is good, whether the page matches what the searcher actually wants, or whether the page is technically sound. Crawlability, page speed, mobile rendering, internal links and backlinks all sit outside a basic on-page check, and any one of them can hold a page back even when the title and H1 are perfect.

Think of this as a pre-publish sanity check, not an audit. It confirms the page is not making avoidable mistakes. The deeper checks — Search Console data, speed tests, a full crawl — come after publishing, and they answer different questions.

Run the basic SEO check in five steps

  1. Paste your draft into the checker. Open the SEO Draft Checker and paste your title, meta description, headings and body text, plus your target keyword. The checker runs the whole list at once and shows a pass or fail for each item.
  2. Fix the title and description lengths. A title over 60 characters or a description outside 150 to 160 characters needs a rewrite, not a trim — cutting words off the end usually removes the benefit. Rewrite the sentence to fit the space.
  3. Check the H1. Exactly one H1 per page. If there are two, demote the second to an H2. If there are none, add one that states the page's topic in the words a searcher would use.
  4. Check keyword placement. The keyword should appear in the title, the description, the H1 and the body. If it is missing from the body entirely, the page probably does not answer the query it is targeting — that is a content problem, not a keyword problem.
  5. Check the word count and readability. Thin content rarely ranks for competitive queries, and long unbroken paragraphs with no headings make a page hard to scan. Break the text into sections with descriptive H2s, and keep sentences short enough to read at a glance.

After the basic check: the next five checks

Once the basics pass, check the page on a phone — most search traffic is mobile, and a page that is hard to use on a small screen loses readers no matter how good the title is. Check the page speed with PageSpeed Insights. Check that the page is linked from somewhere sensible on your site, and that it links out to related pages. Check that images have alt text. And confirm the page returns a 200 status and is listed in your sitemap.

Tools for a deeper check

Google Search Console shows whether the page is indexed and which queries bring it traffic. PageSpeed Insights scores speed and gives fixes. Screaming Frog's free tier crawls up to 500 URLs and finds broken links, duplicate titles and missing descriptions across a whole site. And your browser's own DevTools show the mobile view, console errors and the rendered page exactly as a visitor sees it.

Questions people ask

What is the most important on-page SEO check?

The title. It is the strongest on-page ranking signal and the first thing a searcher sees. A unique, keyword-led title under 60 characters is the single highest-value check.

How many H1 tags should a page have?

Exactly one. Modern HTML allows more, but one H1 per page keeps the page's topic unambiguous for search engines and screen readers.

How many words should a page have for SEO?

There is no magic number. Pages should be as long as the query needs: a tool page can rank at 800 words, a guide may need 1,500. Content that is thin for its topic rarely ranks.

Does keyword density matter?

Not as a target number. Repeating a keyword to hit a percentage reads badly and can look like stuffing. Use the keyword naturally where it belongs: title, H1, first paragraph, and a few times in the body.
