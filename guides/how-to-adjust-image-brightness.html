How to adjust image brightness

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 17 November 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026

A photo taken against a bright window, a dinner shot in a dim restaurant, a sunset that fooled the camera's meter — brightness is the first fix for all of them. Here is how to adjust image brightness without washing the picture out, and how to tell when you have gone too far.

Why photos come out too dark

Every phone camera guesses the exposure when you press the shutter, and the guess is often wrong in mixed light. A bright background makes the camera darken the whole scene, turning your subject into a silhouette. A dim room makes it hesitate the other way. The result is a photo where the subject hides in shadow while the background looks fine — or the reverse.

Brightness rescues these shots by scaling every pixel's value up or down. It works best on photos that are only a stop or two off: a modest lift of 120–140% brings back faces and scenery that looked lost. It cannot recover detail that was never captured — a fully black shadow stays black — but most dark photos have more information in them than they appear to.

How much brightness is too much

Watch the bright parts of the photo as you adjust. Pixels that were already bright hit pure white and lose all detail: a pale sky becomes a flat sheet, a white shirt loses its folds. When the brightest areas start to go featureless, you have gone too far — pull back until the detail returns.

The same applies in reverse. Darkening a washed-out photo restores depth, but push it too far and the shadows crush to black. Small moves are usually enough: most fixes land between 80% and 130%. If a photo needs more than that, the exposure was badly off, and brightness plus a contrast tweak will look better than brightness alone.

How to adjust image brightness

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the Image Brightness Adjuster: drop the image in, drag the slider, and watch the live preview. 100% is the original; right brightens, left darkens.
  2. On Windows, with Photos. Open the image in the Photos app, click Edit, then Adjustments, and drag the Brightness slider. Photos applies the change live, and you can save a copy to keep the original.
  3. On a Mac, with Photos. Open the image in Photos, click Edit, then Adjust, then Light, and drag the Brightness slider. The same panel holds Exposure and Contrast, so you can balance all three in one place.
  4. Check the result on a different screen if you can. Brightness looks different on a bright phone screen and a dim laptop. If the photo is for sharing, a quick check on another device catches over-brightening before anyone else sees it.

Brightness for scans and documents

Scans are the other place brightness earns its keep. A document scanned on a flatbed often comes out with a grey background instead of white, because the scanner's light was not perfectly even. A modest brightness lift — 120% to 140% — pushes the background toward white and makes the text look cleaner, especially for documents that will be printed or shared as PDFs.

The limit is the same as with photos: too much brightness eats the lightest marks. Pencil handwriting, faint stamps and light pencil shading vanish first, so check the lightest part of the document as you drag. For documents, brightening to clean white is a genuine improvement; brightening past it destroys information.

Brightness plus contrast

Brightness alone can wash a photo out, because lifting the shadows moves them closer to the highlights. If your brightened photo looks flat, follow up with a small contrast lift: it restores the separation between light and dark that makes a photo feel three-dimensional.

Work in that order — brightness first, contrast second. Set the overall lightness, then set the punch. Each adjustment does less work that way, and the result looks like a well-exposed photo rather than an edited one. And remember the file format: download as PNG if you plan to adjust the same photo again later — JPG re-compresses on every save, and repeated edits accumulate small losses.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between brightness and exposure?

Exposure is a camera setting — how much light hit the sensor. Brightness is an edit applied afterwards, scaling every pixel evenly. They feel similar, but brightness cannot recover detail the camera never captured.

Can brightness fix a completely black photo?

No. Brightness scales the detail that is already there. Pixels that are fully black contain no information, so they stay black however far you drag the slider.

What brightness value should I use?

Most fixes land between 80% and 130%. Watch the brightest areas as you drag — when they start losing detail, pull back. Small moves almost always look better than big ones.
