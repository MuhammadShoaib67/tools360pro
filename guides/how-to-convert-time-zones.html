How to Convert Time Zones

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 18 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026

A meeting at 3pm in London is 10am in New York, 7pm in Dubai and 8pm in Karachi — except when it is not, because daylight saving moves the goalposts twice a year. Converting time zones by adding a fixed number of hours works until the clocks change. This guide shows how to convert a time properly, for the exact date it happens on.

What a time zone conversion actually does

Every time is a moment, and a time zone is a label for how that moment is written on a local clock. Converting a time means taking one moment and writing it the way a different city's clocks would show it. The moment does not change; only the label does. That is why a conversion always needs a date as well as a time — the label a zone uses depends on the date, because of daylight saving.

The tool shows the converted time together with both zones' UTC offsets and the difference between them. A UTC offset is the gap between a zone's local time and Coordinated Universal Time: Karachi is always UTC+05:00, five hours ahead of UTC, while London is UTC+00:00 in winter and UTC+01:00 in summer. Seeing both offsets makes the answer checkable at a glance — if the offsets differ by five hours, the times differ by five hours.

Why the gap between two cities changes

Daylight saving is the reason. Many zones move their clocks forward by an hour in spring and back in autumn, but they do it on different dates. The United States changes on the second Sunday in March and the first Sunday in November; the United Kingdom and most of Europe change on the last Sunday in March and the last Sunday in October. Between those dates the gap between the two is one hour smaller than usual.

New York and London show it clearly. In winter they are 5 hours apart: noon in New York is 5pm in London. For the three weeks in March after the United States has changed its clocks but the United Kingdom has not, they are only 4 hours apart: noon in New York is 4pm in London. Once London changes too, the gap is 5 hours again. The same pair of cities, three different gaps in one month — which is why a fixed “London is five hours ahead” rule fails.

The southern hemisphere is reversed. Sydney's summer is December to February, so its clocks go forward around October and back around April — the opposite half of the year from London and New York. And many zones never change at all: Karachi, Dubai, Tokyo, Singapore and Mumbai keep the same offset all year, so a conversion involving them is the same in every season.

Convert a time between zones in three steps

  1. Choose the starting zone. Open the Time Zone Converter and pick the zone your starting time is in from the “From” dropdown — or leave Local time, which uses your own zone.
  2. Pick the date and time. Enter the date and time you want to convert. It is read as wall-clock time in the starting zone, so a meeting at 3pm London time goes in as 3pm with London selected.
  3. Choose the target zone and read the result. The converted time appears with both UTC offsets and the difference between the zones. Use Swap to reverse the zones, or “Use current time” to reset the input to now.

Scheduling across zones without mistakes

Always state the zone with the time. “3pm” on its own is a guess; “3pm London time” is a fact. When you send a meeting time to someone in another zone, give them the time in their zone too — a one-line conversion in the message saves a missed meeting. Calendar invites do this automatically: the invite carries the zone, and each person's calendar shows the time in their own zone.

Check the date, not just the time. A conversion can land on a different day: a 10pm meeting in Tokyo is 6am the same day in Los Angeles in summer (5am in winter, when Los Angeles is on standard time), and an evening call from Sydney can be morning the same day in London. The tool shows the full date in both zones, so a day change is visible rather than assumed. And when a meeting falls near a daylight-saving change, convert it for the exact date it happens on — a gap you memorised last month may already be wrong.

Half-hour zones

Not every zone is a whole number of hours from UTC. Mumbai is UTC+05:30, half an hour ahead of Karachi at UTC+05:00 — so noon in Karachi is 12:30pm in Mumbai, not 1pm. These half-hour gaps are the easiest to get wrong by hand, because a rounded guess is always off by thirty minutes, and a thirty-minute error is exactly the size that makes someone miss the start of a call. The converter handles them the same way as every other zone: the offset is part of the calculation, not an adjustment added afterwards.

The honest limits

The tool uses your browser's built-in time-zone database — the same database your device uses to show the time. It is kept current by your browser and operating system, and it carries the daylight-saving rules for each zone, including past and future changes. Governments occasionally change the rules, for example by abolishing daylight saving, and an out-of-date browser may miss a very recent change — so for a meeting that truly cannot move, a second check the day before is cheap insurance.

The converter works with the 30 zones in its list, covering the cities most people need — London, New York, Los Angeles, Dubai, Karachi, Mumbai, Singapore, Tokyo, Sydney and the rest. If a zone you need is not listed, convert via UTC: convert your time to UTC first, then from UTC to the zone you want. And the tool converts one time at a time — it is a converter, not a meeting scheduler, so it will not find a slot that suits everyone.

Questions people ask

How do I convert a time to another time zone?

Choose the starting zone, pick the date and time, then choose the target zone. The result shows the converted time, both UTC offsets and the difference between the zones.

Does it handle daylight saving time?

Yes. The conversion uses the rules for the exact date you pick, so a March date and a July date between the same two cities can give different gaps.

Why is the time difference different in summer?

Because many zones move their clocks forward in summer and back in winter, and they do it on different dates. New York and London are 5 hours apart in winter but only 4 hours apart for a few weeks in March.

What is a UTC offset?

The difference between a zone's local time and Coordinated Universal Time. Karachi is UTC+05:00, five hours ahead of UTC; New York is UTC−05:00 in winter, five hours behind.
