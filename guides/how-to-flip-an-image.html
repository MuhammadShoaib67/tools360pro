How to flip an image

A selfie that reads backwards, a logo facing the wrong way, a design where the subject looks off the page — flipping mirrors the picture and fixes all three. Here is how to flip an image, which direction to choose, and the one side effect to check before you share the result.

Why selfies look mirrored

A front camera shows you a mirror while you frame the shot, because a non-mirrored preview feels wrong — you would move your hand the wrong way. Some phones then save the photo exactly as you saw it, mirrored. Others save the un-mirrored version. Which one yours does depends on the phone and its settings, and it is easy not to notice until someone points out that the text on your shirt reads backwards.

The fix is a horizontal flip: left becomes right, right becomes left, and the photo matches how the world actually sees you. Faces are not symmetrical, so a mirrored face can look subtly unfamiliar even to its owner — flipping it back restores the face everyone else recognises.

Horizontal, vertical, or both

Horizontal flipping swaps left and right. It is the one you want for selfies, for text that reads backwards, and for a subject that should face into a layout instead of off the edge of the page.

Vertical flipping swaps top and bottom. It is mostly a creative choice — reflections, abstract images, a fun twist on a familiar photo. Flipping both ways at once turns the image upside down, which is the same result as a 180° rotation. If that is what you want, either route gets you there.

How to flip an image

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the Image Flipper: drop the image in, choose horizontal, vertical or both, and download. The flip applies instantly and your original file is never changed.
  2. On Windows, with Paint. Open the image in Paint, click Rotate, then choose Flip horizontal or Flip vertical. Save as a new file if you want to keep the original — Paint overwrites when you save.
  3. On a Mac, with Preview. Open the image in Preview, then choose Tools, then Flip Horizontal or Flip Vertical. Preview has both options in the Tools menu, and the change applies immediately.
  4. Check any text in the picture. Flipping mirrors text too. Read the preview before sharing: if a sign, a logo or a shirt now reads backwards, the flip was the wrong direction — or the text was already mirrored and the flip fixed it.

When flipping is the wrong fix

Not every wrong-way-up photo needs a flip. If the picture is simply upside down, a 180° rotation is the cleaner fix — flipping both ways gives the same result, but rotation is the more obvious tool for the job. And if a photo looks right on your phone but mirrored on a website, the problem may be the orientation flag again, not the pixels: rotate the pixels once with the Image Rotator and the mirror effect disappears everywhere.

The other case is text. A sign photographed in a mirror reads backwards in the photo because it was backwards in real life — flipping it makes the sign read correctly, but it also flips everything else in the scene. That is usually the right trade for a photo where the sign is the subject, and the wrong trade where the sign is incidental. Decide what the photo is about, then choose.

Flipping for design and transfers

Designers flip photos so the subject faces the content: a person looking left belongs on the right side of a layout, looking in — not on the left, looking off the page. It is a small change with an outsized effect on how balanced a page feels.

Iron-on transfers are the practical case. Text printed for a T-shirt transfer must be mirrored, or it reads backwards on the shirt — so flip the whole design before printing. The same applies to any print viewed through glass or from the back: flip first, print second, and check the result against a mirror before committing. A quick check before you print: hold the flipped design up to a mirror — what you see in the mirror is what the transfer will look like on the shirt.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between flipping and rotating?

Flipping mirrors the picture — horizontal flip swaps left and right, vertical swaps top and bottom. Rotating turns it around its centre. Flipping both ways at once equals a 180° rotation.

Will flipping make text read backwards?

Yes — flipping mirrors everything, including text. That is exactly why it fixes mirrored selfies, and exactly why you should check the preview before sharing or printing.

Does flipping reduce image quality?

No. Flipping only rearranges pixels. Quality only changes if you save as JPG, which re-compresses the image — choose PNG for a pixel-perfect result.
