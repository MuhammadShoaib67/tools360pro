How to Calculate Your Sleep Cycle

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 17 November 2026 · Updated 17 November 2026

Sleep runs in cycles of about 90 minutes, and waking between cycles feels far easier than waking in the middle of one. Count back or forward from the time you need to wake or sleep, in whole 90-minute cycles, and you get the times that leave you rested. This guide shows how the maths works and how to use it.

What a sleep cycle is

Sleep is not one long stretch of unconsciousness. It moves through stages — light sleep, deep sleep, and dreaming REM sleep — in cycles that last about 90 minutes on average. A full night of sleep is four to six of these cycles in a row, and the stages repeat in roughly the same order each time.

Ninety minutes is an average, not a rule. Cycles vary between people and across the night — early cycles have more deep sleep, later cycles have more dreaming — so the times this guide gives are guides, not exact predictions. They are close enough to be useful, and far better than guessing.

Counting in 90-minute cycles

The maths is simple. Take the time you need to wake up, subtract 15 minutes for falling asleep, then subtract whole 90-minute cycles. To wake at 07:00, five cycles back is 07:00 minus 15 minutes minus 7.5 hours, which is 23:15. Six cycles back is 21:45. Those are the two bedtimes that give you a full number of cycles.

Going the other way works the same: take your bedtime, add 15 minutes, then add whole cycles. Going to bed at 23:00, five cycles forward is 06:45 and six cycles is 08:15. The calculator does both directions and lists all four options, with the five- and six-cycle times marked as recommended.

Calculate your sleep cycle in four steps

  1. Decide which time is fixed. If you must wake at a set time, start from the wake-up time. If your bedtime is fixed, start from the bedtime. One of the two is always the anchor.
  2. Allow 15 minutes to fall asleep. Most people take around 15 minutes to drift off, so the cycles start 15 minutes after the bedtime you enter. If you fall asleep faster or slower, shift the times by a few minutes.
  3. Count in 90-minute cycles. Open the Sleep Cycle Calculator, choose the direction, enter the time, and read the list of bedtimes or wake times for 3, 4, 5 and 6 cycles.
  4. Pick the 5- or 6-cycle option. That is 7.5 to 9 hours of sleep, the range most adults need. Four cycles is a workable minimum for a night or two; three is an emergency, not a plan.

Why waking mid-cycle feels worse

Waking up in the middle of a deep-sleep stage causes sleep inertia: that heavy, groggy feeling that can take half an hour to shake off. Waking at the end of a cycle, when sleep is light, feels much easier — even if you slept fewer hours in total. That is why six hours of sleep can feel better than seven: six hours is four full cycles, while seven hours interrupts the fifth.

The calculator works with this. It counts back or forward in whole 90-minute cycles from the time you enter, and adds 15 minutes for falling asleep. The result is a set of times that land between cycles instead of inside one — the times when waking is easiest.

Naps and regular times

Naps follow the same logic. A 20-minute nap stays in light sleep, so you wake easily; a 90-minute nap is one full cycle, so you also wake easily. A 45-minute nap is the worst of both, because it wakes you from deep sleep. If you nap, keep it short or make it a full cycle — the middle ground is where the grogginess lives.

Keep the times regular. Going to bed and waking at roughly the same times each day trains your body to complete its cycles on schedule, which matters more than any single night's calculation. And remember this is a timing guide, not medical advice — if you regularly struggle to sleep, or feel tired no matter how long you sleep, speak to a doctor.

Questions people ask

How long is a sleep cycle?

About 90 minutes on average, though cycles vary between people and across the night.

How many sleep cycles do I need?

Most adults need 5 to 6 cycles a night, which is 7.5 to 9 hours of sleep.

Why add 15 minutes for falling asleep?

Most people take around 15 minutes to fall asleep, so the cycles start 15 minutes after the bedtime you enter.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is a timing guide based on average cycle lengths. If you have persistent trouble sleeping, speak to a doctor.
