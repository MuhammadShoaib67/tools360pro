How to convert WebP to JPG

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 17 November 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026

The most common reason to convert WebP to JPG is a form that says 'JPG only'. JPG is still the format the whole world accepts — every upload form, every printer, every old app. The price is transparency: JPG cannot store it, so transparent areas become white. Here is how to convert, and how to handle the transparency question.

Why JPG, still

Accepted everywhere, for thirty years

JPG has been the default photo format for thirty years, and that history is its strength. Every upload form, ID portal, marketplace, printer and email client accepts it. Every phone, camera and computer made in the last three decades opens it. When a destination lists the formats it takes, JPG is always on the list — WebP, for all its technical advantages, often is not.

A conversion of necessity, not preference

That is why WebP to JPG is usually a conversion of necessity rather than preference: the picture is fine as a WebP, but the place it needs to go will not take it. A downloaded product photo, a screenshot saved by a browser, an image from a modern website — all commonly WebP, all commonly needing to become JPG before a form or an old app will touch them.

The transparency trade

Transparent pixels become a white box

JPG has no alpha channel, so any transparent pixels in the WebP are placed on a white background when the JPG is created. For photos this makes no difference at all — photos have no transparent pixels. For logos, icons and cutouts it matters a great deal: a logo that floated cleanly on any background will now sit inside a white box.

Transparency means PNG, not JPG

If the picture relies on transparency, do not convert to JPG. Convert to PNG instead — WebP to PNG keeps the transparency and is accepted everywhere JPG is, at the cost of a larger file. Choose JPG when the picture is a photo or a screenshot with no transparency to lose, and the destination demands the JPG format.

How to convert WebP to JPG

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the WebP to JPG Converter: drop one or more WebP images onto the box, set the quality (92% is a good default), press Convert, and download each JPG or grab them all as a ZIP. The conversion runs on your own device, so the pictures are never uploaded anywhere.
  2. With GIMP, the free desktop editor. Open the WebP in GIMP, choose File, then Export As, and change the filename to end in .jpg. GIMP shows a quality slider so you can balance file size against sharpness, and it is the best choice when you are about to edit the picture anyway.
  3. On a Mac, with Preview. On macOS 11 (Big Sur) and later, Preview opens WebP files. Open the image, choose File, then Export, pick JPEG as the format, and drag the quality slider. On older macOS versions Preview cannot open WebP, so use the browser converter or GIMP instead.
  4. Check the result, especially the edges. Open the converted JPG and look at the edges of the subject, particularly if it was a cutout or a logo. If a white box has appeared where transparency used to be, convert to PNG instead — the WebP original is still there, untouched.

Picking the right target format

The choice between JPG and PNG comes down to one question: does the picture need transparency? If yes, PNG is the only answer of the two. If no, JPG is the smaller file and the safer bet for forms, because some upload systems accept JPG and not PNG. And if the picture is staying on the web — a blog, a store, a portfolio — the honest answer is often to keep the WebP, which is smaller than both.

Whatever you choose, keep the original WebP. It is the smallest version of the picture and the best source for any future conversion. Converting from the WebP each time beats converting from an already-converted JPG, which loses a little quality with every generation.

Questions people ask

Why is my converted JPG on a white background?

JPG cannot store transparency, so transparent areas of the WebP are filled with white during conversion. If the white background ruins a logo or cutout, convert the same WebP to PNG instead — PNG keeps transparency.

What quality setting should I use?

92% is a good default for most pictures. Photos look fine at 85% and above; screenshots with small text deserve 95%. If a form has a strict file-size limit, lower the quality until the file fits, and check the result at full size before submitting.

Can I convert WebP to JPG on my phone?

Yes — the browser converter works on phones exactly as it does on a computer. Open the tool in your phone's browser, pick the WebP from your files, convert, and download the JPG. No app to install, and the picture never leaves your device.
