How to Calculate Subscription Costs

A subscription's monthly price is the smallest number attached to it. Multiply by 12 for the yearly cost and by 60 for five years, and the picture changes completely. This guide shows how to calculate what subscriptions really cost, how to compare monthly and yearly billing, and how to audit the subscriptions you already pay for.

The subscription maths

The yearly cost of a subscription is the monthly price times 12. Ten a month is 120 a year; 15 a month is 180 a year. The five-year cost is the monthly price times 60: that same 10 subscription costs 600 over five years. The maths is trivial, which is exactly why it is worth doing — the numbers are small enough to ignore and large enough to matter.

Several subscriptions multiply. Three services at 10 a month each come to 360 a year, which is a holiday, a new phone, or several months of groceries, depending on where you live. Most households have more subscriptions than they can name from memory, which is the first sign that the total is bigger than expected.

Monthly vs yearly billing

Many services offer a yearly plan priced below twelve monthly payments — the saving is the service's way of buying your commitment for a year. A yearly plan at 100 against a monthly price of 10 saves 20 a year, which is real money, but only if you will still use the service in twelve months. Enter both prices and the calculator shows the exact saving.

Yearly billing is not always cheaper. Some yearly plans cost exactly twelve months with no discount, and some charge a fee for cancelling early. The calculator tells you plainly when yearly billing saves nothing — or when it actually costs more — so you never switch on an assumption.

Calculate subscription costs in four steps

  1. List your subscriptions. Check your bank statements for recurring payments — streaming, music, cloud storage, apps, magazines, gym memberships. Most people find at least one they forgot about.
  2. Note the monthly price of each one. If a service bills yearly, divide the yearly price by 12 to get the monthly equivalent, so every subscription is on the same footing.
  3. Calculate the totals. Open the Subscription Cost Calculator, enter a monthly price, and read the yearly and 5-year totals. Repeat for each subscription.
  4. Add the yearly totals together. The sum is what your subscriptions cost each year. Compare it with what you expected — the gap is usually the interesting part.

The yearly subscription audit

Once a year, list every subscription you pay for and write the monthly price next to each one. Add them up, multiply by twelve, and look at the total. Then cancel what you do not use, and downgrade what you use rarely. A family plan shared properly is cheaper per person than separate accounts; a free tier is often enough for an app you open once a month.

The audit takes twenty minutes and usually pays for itself many times over. The subscriptions to look for first are the ones you signed up for a single purpose — a show, a trip, a project — and never cancelled. They are the most common waste, and the easiest to cut.

Free trials and forgotten subscriptions

Free trials are the other trap. A trial that converts to a paid plan after seven or fourteen days is easy to forget, and the first charge is often the moment you realise the trial ended. If you start a trial, set a reminder for the day before it converts, and cancel the moment you decide you do not want it — the service will usually let you keep the trial until it expires.

Forgotten subscriptions survive because the amounts are small. A charge of 5 or 10 a month does not stand out on a bank statement, and the service has no reason to remind you. The defence is the audit: check your statements once a year, and cancel anything you cannot remember using in the last month.

A shared subscription is worth checking too. Many services offer family or household plans that cost less per person than separate accounts — but only if everyone actually uses them. A family plan with one active user is more expensive than a single account, so count the real users before you upgrade.

Questions people ask

How do I calculate the yearly cost of a subscription?

Multiply the monthly price by 12. A 10 monthly subscription costs 120 a year.

Is yearly billing always cheaper?

Usually, but not always. Some yearly plans cost exactly twelve months with no discount. Enter both prices to see the real saving.

How do I add up several subscriptions?

Calculate each one and add the yearly totals together. Three subscriptions at 10 a month each come to 360 a year.

What if the price goes up?

Treat the 5-year figure as a minimum based on today's price. Re-check once a year.
