How to convert HEIC to JPG on Windows 10

You copy photos from an iPhone to a Windows 10 PC, double-click one — and the Photos app shows an error instead of the picture. The files are HEIC, Apple's default photo format, and Windows 10 cannot open them out of the box. Here are four honest ways to convert HEIC to JPG on Windows 10: in the browser with nothing to install, with Microsoft's own codecs, with a free plugin, or by letting the iPhone do the converting when it hands the photos over.

Why Windows 10 cannot open HEIC photos

Since iOS 11, iPhones save photos as HEIC by default. The format is genuinely good — it stores the same picture in roughly half the space of a JPG — but it needs a special decoder, and Microsoft never shipped one with Windows 10. The Photos app therefore cannot read the file at all, and File Explorer shows a blank icon instead of a preview.

One thing has changed since the early guides about this problem: Windows 10 reached end of support on 14 October 2025. Microsoft no longer ships feature updates or fixes for it — a consumer Extended Security Updates programme covers security fixes only, until 12 October 2027. That means no HEIC support is coming to Windows 10, so the fix is a converter or a codec you add yourself, not a Windows Update to wait for. This is a Windows problem, not a broken photo: the same file opens perfectly on the iPhone that took it, and on a Mac. Our general guide HEIC Photos Won't Open? How to Convert Them on Any Device covers the same problem on every device; this guide is the Windows 10 version, step by step. On Windows 11 the steps are nearly identical — see our Windows 11 guide for that version.

Option 1: convert in the browser (fastest, nothing to install)

If you need the photos now — for a form, an email, a marketplace listing — converting them is faster than fixing Windows. The browser method needs no install, no account and no payment, and it works on any Windows 10 PC, including a borrowed or office computer where you cannot install software. It is also the only option here that works when the PC is not yours to change.

  1. Open the HEIC to JPG tool. Go to the HEIC to JPG Converter — it opens straight in your browser.
  2. Add your photos. Drop the HEIC files onto the box, or click it to browse your Pictures folder. You can add several at once.
  3. Choose JPG or PNG. Pick JPG for sharing, forms and uploads — it is the universal format. Pick PNG if you plan to edit the picture further, because PNG is lossless and loses nothing in the conversion.
  4. Download the results. Save the converted files next to the originals, then open one in the Photos app to confirm it displays properly before you delete anything.

Because the conversion runs in your browser, the photos never leave your device — which is worth knowing when the pictures are personal. Family photos converted on a random upload site are copies on somebody else's server; here, there is no server involved at all.

Option 2: install Microsoft's codecs from the Store

If you want Windows 10 itself to open HEIC files — thumbnails in Explorer, double-click to view — install Microsoft's two extensions from the Microsoft Store. Search the Store for HEIF Image Extensions and install it; it is free. Then search for HEVC Video Extensions and install that too: it is the part that actually decodes the photo data, and Microsoft lists it as a paid item at $0.99.

After both are installed, close and reopen the Photos app. HEIC files should now open, and Explorer should show thumbnails. Two honest caveats: first, the codecs only let you view HEIC files — if a website, a form or an old program still rejects the format, you will need to convert the file to JPG anyway. Second, some PCs from big manufacturers already ship with these extensions installed, so if your Photos app already opens HEIC, you have nothing to fix. One more Windows 10 note: the Store still works on Windows 10, but with support ended, do not expect these extensions to gain new features — they do what they do today.

Option 3: a free plugin that adds HEIC to Windows itself

If you would rather not pay $0.99 for Microsoft's HEVC extension, CopyTrans HEIC for Windows is a free plugin for personal use that teaches Windows 10 to see HEIC files. Once installed, File Explorer shows HEIC thumbnails, and you can open the photos in the classic Windows Photo Viewer. It also adds a right-click menu item, "Convert to JPEG with CopyTrans", which converts selected photos to JPG in the same folder — up to 100 images at a time — without opening any program.

The honest limits: it only handles HEIC files from iPhones, it gives you no control over the output quality, and a converted JPG is roughly twice the size of the original HEIC — so keep the originals until you have checked the results. It also works with Windows Photo Viewer rather than the newer Photos app, so you may need to right-click a photo, choose Open with, and pick Windows Photo Viewer the first time. Download it only from the official CopyTrans website — free codec downloads from anywhere else are a classic place to pick up unwanted software.

Option 4: let the iPhone convert the photos for you

If the photos keep arriving as HEIC because they come from your own iPhone, the cleanest long-term fix is on the phone, not the PC — and it costs nothing. On the iPhone, open Settings > Photos and scroll to Transfer to Mac or PC. Select Automatic. From then on, the phone converts HEIC photos to JPG by itself whenever you copy them to a Windows PC over a USB cable — you keep the space-saving HEIC files on the phone, and the PC receives JPGs it can open.

For new photos, you can go one step further: Settings > Camera > Formats, and switch from High Efficiency to Most Compatible. From then on the phone saves new photos as JPG, and every Windows PC opens them without any extra step. The trade-off is real: JPG files are roughly twice the size of HEIC, so the phone's storage fills faster and transfers take longer. If storage is tight, keep High Efficiency switched on and rely on the Automatic transfer setting instead — it solves the problem for every future transfer without costing a megabyte of phone storage. Note also that changing either setting only affects photos taken or transferred afterwards; the HEIC files already on your PC still need converting, with Option 1.

JPG or PNG — which should you pick on Windows 10?

For almost everything on Windows 10, JPG is the right answer: every program opens it, every form accepts it, every website takes it, and the files are small. The quality loss from one HEIC-to-JPG conversion is invisible in normal viewing — you would need to zoom deep into fine detail, side by side with the original, to find it.

Choose PNG in exactly two cases: you plan to edit the photo heavily afterwards (cropping, colour work, repeated saving), or the picture is a screenshot or graphic with sharp text and flat colours, where JPG's compression can smudge edges. PNG files are much larger, so do not pick it by default. And if the converted JPG is still too large for a form, Compress Image shrinks it further without changing how big it appears on screen.

Batch tips for a whole camera roll

A holiday's worth of photos is a batch job, not a one-by-one job. Convert in groups rather than all at once, so a mistake is easy to spot; keep the original HEIC files until you have opened several converted JPGs and confirmed they look right; and give the converted files clear names — Windows saves them with the same name as the original except for the extension, so a folder will show photo.heic next to photo.jpg, which is tidy enough on its own. Once the JPGs check out, the HEIC originals can be archived or deleted — but only after the check, never before.

Questions people ask

Why won't my Windows 10 Photos app open HEIC files?

Windows 10 ships without the codec that reads HEIC images. The Photos app can only open the format once the HEIF Image Extensions and the HEVC Video Extensions are installed from the Microsoft Store, so a fresh Windows 10 PC shows an error instead of the photo. Converting the files to JPG removes the problem entirely, on any PC — and with Windows 10 support ended, no update will add the codec for you.

Is converting HEIC to JPG free on Windows 10?

Converting in the browser is free: our HEIC to JPG tool runs with no install, no account and no payment. The Microsoft Store route is only partly free — the HEIF Image Extensions are free, but the HEVC Video Extensions that actually decode the photos are a paid item, listed at $0.99 in the Store. CopyTrans HEIC is a free alternative for personal use, with a 100-image limit per batch.

Does converting HEIC to JPG lose quality?

Very slightly, and almost never visibly. HEIC and JPG are both lossy formats, so converting re-encodes the picture and a tiny amount of fine detail is lost — at high quality settings the difference is invisible in normal viewing and printing. If you plan to edit the photo heavily afterwards, convert to PNG instead: PNG is lossless, so nothing is lost in that step.

Can I convert HEIC to JPG on Windows 10 without installing anything?

Yes. A browser-based converter does the whole job with no install: open the HEIC to JPG tool, drop in your photos, choose JPG or PNG, and download the results. The conversion runs on your own device, so even personal family photos are never uploaded anywhere.
