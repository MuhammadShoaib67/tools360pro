How to add text to an image

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 17 November 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026

Text on a picture travels with it: a caption drawn on a photo survives every share, repost and download, while a caption underneath is lost the moment the image moves. Here is how to add text to an image so it reads clearly — and the tools that do it fastest.

Why text belongs on the picture

Without words, a photo loses its context

A photo shared without its caption loses its context. Nobody knows where it was taken, what it shows, or why it matters. The same photo with a few words drawn on it — a place, a date, a price, a joke — keeps its meaning wherever it goes. That is why sellers put prices on product photos, teachers label diagrams, and families date their holiday pictures.

Text can point: labels, titles, warnings

Text also does work a caption cannot: it can point. A label on a screenshot shows exactly which button to press; a title on a thumbnail is read before any description; a warning on a photo is seen before the photo is shared. The words become part of the image, and part of what the image communicates.

Readable text: contrast, size, position

Contrast first: an outline keeps text readable

The first rule of text on a photo is contrast. White text vanishes on a bright sky; black text disappears into shadow. The reliable fix is an outline: text drawn with a dark edge stays readable on both light and dark areas of the same photo, which is why this site's tool adds one automatically. White text with a dark outline is the safest choice for almost any picture.

Size and position for small screens

The second rule is size. Text that looks comfortable at full size becomes unreadable when the image is shrunk to a thumbnail — and most images are viewed small. If the picture will be seen on a phone, go bigger than feels necessary. The third rule is position: put text over the plainest part of the photo — a sky, a wall, an empty corner — not over faces or busy detail. A busy background fights the letters; a plain one lets them speak.

Fonts: what works where

For captions, clarity beats personality: Arial, Verdana and Trebuchet MS are designed to read well at small sizes on screens. For formal images — certificates, invitations, title slides — Georgia and Times New Roman carry more weight. For memes and posters, Impact is the expected voice: heavy, condensed, impossible to ignore.

Whatever the font, one style per image is enough. Two fonts fighting on one picture look like a ransom note. And keep the words short: a line of text is read at a glance, a paragraph is skipped. If you need more than a line, the text probably belongs in a caption underneath, not on the picture.

How to add text to an image

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the Add Text to Image tool: drop the image in, type your text, pick a font, size, colour and position, and download. The text is drawn with a subtle outline so it stays readable.
  2. In Canva, for designed posts. Upload the photo to a Canva design, click Text, and add a heading or text box. Canva offers hundreds of fonts and lets you drag the text anywhere — it suits posts where the text is part of a larger design.
  3. On Windows, with Paint. Open the image in Paint, click the Text tool (the “A” icon), draw a box where the text goes, and type. Choose the font and size from the toolbar, then save a copy — Paint overwrites the original when you save.
  4. On a Mac, with Preview. Open the image in Preview, click the markup toolbar, then the Text button, and type in the box that appears. Drag the box to position it, and use the style button to change the font, size and colour.

Text vs watermark vs meme

Three kinds of text go on images, and they want different tools. A caption is meant to be read — one clear line, the job this guide covers. A watermark is meant to claim ownership without hiding the picture: faint, repeated, impossible to crop out — use the Watermark Image tool for that. A meme is a joke in a fixed format: bold white Impact text, top and bottom, heavy outline — the Meme Generator does it in one step.

One caution applies to all three: text baked into an image cannot be edited or removed afterwards. Always work on a copy, and keep the original — if you spot a typo after downloading, you will want the clean file to start over from.

Questions people ask

How do I make text readable on a busy photo?

Use white text with a dark outline, make it larger than you think you need, and place it over the plainest part of the photo — a sky, a wall or an empty corner — rather than over faces or detail.

Can I add more than one line?

With the browser tool here, add one line, download, and open the result again to add the next — each pass adds a line, so you can build a title and a subtitle. Canva lets you add many text boxes at once.

Which font is best for captions?

Arial and Verdana are the clearest for captions at small sizes. Georgia suits formal images, and Impact is the classic meme font. Try a couple in the preview before downloading.
