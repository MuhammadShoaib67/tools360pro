How to blur an image

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 17 November 2026 · Updated 17 November 2026

Blur is one of the most useful edits there is: it turns a busy photo into a calm background, hides a face or a number plate before sharing, and softens harsh detail in a portrait. Here is how to blur an image, how strong the blur needs to be, and the honest limits of blur as a privacy tool.

What blur is actually good for

Blurred images are workhorses in design. A heavily blurred photo makes a calm background for text, because the shapes and colours stay while the distracting detail melts away. App screens, presentation slides and website headers all use this trick — the photo sets the mood, and the words on top stay readable.

A light blur has a different job: it softens. Portraits with slightly harsh skin detail, product photos with a busy backdrop, and screenshots with small imperfections all look gentler with a blur of a few pixels. The image stays recognisable; only the hard edges relax.

Blur for privacy — and its limits

Blur is the standard way to hide a face, a number plate or an address in a photo you are about to share. It works, but only if the blur is strong enough: a light blur over a number plate can sometimes be read by guessing, and text under a weak blur is often recoverable. For anything genuinely sensitive, use a heavy blur — 15 pixels or more — or crop the detail out entirely.

One honest warning: blur is not encryption. A blurred face cannot be unblurred by a simple tool, but a determined person with enhancement software can sometimes sharpen a lightly blurred image. If the detail must never be seen, remove it with cropping or an object remover instead of relying on blur alone.

How to blur an image

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the Image Blur Tool: drop the image in, drag the blur slider, and download. The whole image is blurred; 5px is a gentle softening, 20px melts it into soft colour fields.
  2. With GIMP (free, Windows/Mac/Linux). Open the image, then choose Filters, then Blur, then Gaussian Blur, and set the radius. GIMP also lets you blur a selected area only, by selecting it first and then applying the blur.
  3. In PowerPoint. Select the picture, then Picture Format, then Artistic Effects, then Blur. PowerPoint's blur is fixed-strength, so it is best for quick slide backgrounds rather than precise work — and you can right-click the picture and save it as an image afterwards.
  4. Check the result at full size. Blur that looks strong in a small preview can look weak when the image is viewed large. Zoom to 100% before sharing, especially when the blur is hiding something sensitive.

Blur and backgrounds in presentations

Blurred backgrounds are a staple of slides and app screens, and the technique is simple: take a photo, blur it heavily — 20 pixels or more — and put your text on top. The colours and shapes of the photo set the mood, while the text stays readable because there is no sharp detail competing with it. A dark overlay on top of the blurred image makes white text pop even more.

The same trick works for video calls and profile headers, where a blurred version of a real photo looks more personal than a flat colour. The key is heavy blur: a light blur leaves recognisable shapes that distract, while a heavy blur leaves only colour fields. If you can still read a sign or recognise a face in the background, the blur is not strong enough yet.

Blur strength and file size

Blurred images compress extremely well. A photo full of fine detail is hard for JPG to squeeze; the same photo blurred to soft colour fields compresses to a fraction of the size. That makes blurred backgrounds cheap to use on websites — a blurred hero image can weigh less than a tenth of the original.

The browser tool blurs the whole image — there is no brush to blur one area. For selective blur, crop the area you want blurred, blur the crop, and place it back over the original in an editor. For whole-image softening and backgrounds, the one-step blur is the right answer.

Questions people ask

What does the blur number mean?

It is the blur radius in pixels: how far each pixel's colour spreads. 5px is a gentle softening; 20px melts the image into soft shapes and colours.

Can I blur just one face or one area?

Not with the browser tool — it blurs the whole image. For one area, crop it out, blur the crop, and place it back over the original in an editor. GIMP can blur a selected area directly.

Can a blurred photo be unblurred?

Not by a simple tool — the download contains only the blurred pixels. But a light blur can sometimes be partly sharpened by enhancement software, so use a heavy blur (15px or more) for anything genuinely sensitive.
