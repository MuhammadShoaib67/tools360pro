How to resize product images for ecommerce

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 17 November 2026 · Updated 17 November 2026

Marketplaces judge your photos before your prices. Here is how to resize product images for ecommerce — the square white standard, the sizes each marketplace wants, and how to get there without cropping your product in half.

The square white standard

Every major marketplace wants the same thing: a square image, a white background, the product filling most of the frame. Amazon requires it — 85% of the frame must be the product, on pure white, at least 1000 pixels on the long side. eBay, Etsy and Shopify do not all enforce it as strictly, but they all display square thumbnails, and a white background is what their shoppers expect.

The reason is the grid. Marketplace search results are rows of square thumbnails, and a product that arrives as a wide or tall photo gets cropped or letterboxed by the platform — often badly. A square image with a white background fills its thumbnail exactly, every time, on every marketplace. It is the one format that needs no adaptation anywhere.

Fit, never crop

The temptation is to crop a rectangular product photo into a square. The cost is the product itself: crop a tall bottle to a square and you lose its top or its bottom. The right approach is to fit the whole product inside the square and pad the remaining space with white. The product stays whole, the background stays clean, and the result meets the marketplace standard.

Padding needs the product to be roughly centred, with even margins. A product shoved into one corner of a white square looks like a mistake; centred with white all around, it looks like a studio shot. The browser tool below centres and pads automatically — drop the photo in, pick the marketplace preset, and the product is fitted, not cropped.

Zoom needs pixels

Marketplace shoppers zoom. Amazon enables zoom at 1000 pixels and recommends 2000; eBay's zoom works best at 1600; Shopify recommends 2048. A 500-pixel image cannot zoom — the shopper sees a blurry enlargement and moves on. The standard presets are generous for a reason: they give the zoom something to show.

That generosity has a limit, though. Amazon caps uploads at 10 MB, and an uncompressed 2000-pixel PNG can exceed it. Save product images as JPG at 85–90% quality: small enough to upload fast, sharp enough to zoom. And keep the originals — a marketplace that raises its minimum size tomorrow needs a resize from the original, not an upscale of today's file.

How to resize product images for ecommerce

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the Ecommerce Product Image Resizer: drop the photo in, pick the marketplace preset — Amazon 2000 × 2000, eBay 1600 × 1600, Etsy 2000 × 2000, Shopify 2048 × 2048, WooCommerce 1200 × 1200 — and download with white padding. Nothing is uploaded.
  2. In Canva. Create a design at the marketplace size, upload your photo, and resize it to fit inside the square with white around it. Canva's background remover (a paid feature) helps when the original background is not white.
  3. In GIMP. Open the photo, choose Image, then Scale Image to fit the product within the target size, then Image, then Canvas Size to extend the canvas to the square with a white fill. GIMP is free and gives exact control.
  4. Check before uploading. Open the result and confirm three things: it is square, the background is white, and the product fills most of the frame. Then check the file size — if it is over the marketplace limit, compress it with the Compress Image tool before uploading.

A consistent catalogue

One good product photo is a start; a catalogue of consistent photos is a shop. When every image is the same size, the same shape and the same background, the shop page looks like a single brand instead of a collection of snapshots — and shoppers trust it more. The marketplaces' own guidance says the same: consistency across listings is part of what makes a shop look professional.

The practical way to get there is a single workflow: shoot or source the photo, remove the background if needed, resize every image to the same preset with the same padding, and upload. Do it the same way for every product, and the catalogue builds itself. If you have many products, resize them all with the Bulk Image Resizer first, then pad the set to square.

Questions people ask

What size should Amazon product images be?

At least 1000 pixels on the longest side to enable zoom, and 2000 × 2000 is the recommended size — square, JPEG or PNG, on a pure white background, with the product filling at least 85% of the frame.

Should product images be square?

Yes — every major marketplace displays square thumbnails. A square image fills its thumbnail exactly; a rectangular one gets cropped or letterboxed by the platform, often badly.

How do I get a white background?

Fit the product into a square and pad the empty space with white — the browser tool does this automatically. If the photo itself has a busy background, remove it first with the Background Remover tool.
