How to convert AVIF to JPG

AVIF is the newest image format in common use: photos far smaller than JPG at the same quality, built on the AV1 video codec. The problem is that 'newest' also means 'least supported' — plenty of software, forms and older devices cannot open an AVIF file at all. Converting AVIF to JPG trades the tiny file for a format everything accepts. Here is how, and the browser-support catch to know about.

Where AVIF files come from

AVIF comes from the same technology as modern video streaming: it is a still image encoded with the AV1 codec, the royalty-free video format backed by Google, Netflix, Amazon and others. Newer Android phones can save photos as AVIF, some cameras offer it, and a growing number of websites serve AVIF images to browsers that support them. The files are strikingly small — often half the size of an equivalent JPG — which is why the format exists at all.

The catch is age. AVIF only began appearing in browsers around 2020, and support outside browsers is still patchy. Windows Photos on older Windows versions cannot open AVIF, many upload forms reject it, and a good deal of everyday software has never heard of it. If you have an AVIF file that something refuses, converting it to JPG is the practical answer.

The browser-support catch

Our converter decodes AVIF using your own browser, which means it works exactly where your browser can read AVIF: Chrome, Edge and Firefox on current versions, and Safari 16 and later. Older browsers and some in-app browsers cannot decode AVIF, and in that case the tool tells you plainly instead of failing silently or producing a broken file. That message is not a bug in the tool — it is the browser's own limit.

If your browser cannot decode the file, use GIMP instead: the free editor has opened AVIF since version 2.10.22 on Windows, Mac and Linux. Either way, keep the AVIF original. It is the smallest version of the picture, and as support spreads it will only become more useful.

How to convert AVIF to JPG

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the AVIF to JPG Converter: drop one or more AVIF images onto the box, set the quality (92% is a good default — AVIF originals are high quality, so do not throw that away with a low setting), press Convert, and download each JPG or grab them all as a ZIP. The conversion runs on your own device, so the pictures are never uploaded anywhere.
  2. If the browser cannot decode AVIF, use GIMP. Open the AVIF in GIMP (version 2.10.22 or later), choose File, then Export As, and change the filename to end in .jpg. GIMP decodes AVIF itself, so it works even on computers whose browser cannot. It is also the best choice when you want to edit the picture before converting.
  3. Check the result at full size. Open the converted JPG and zoom to 100%. AVIF photos are usually sharp and detailed, and a high quality setting preserves that. If the JPG looks soft, convert again from the AVIF at a higher quality rather than converting the JPG a second time.
  4. Keep the AVIF as your compact archive. The JPG is for compatibility — forms, sharing, old software. The AVIF is the better long-term copy: far smaller, and support for it grows every year. Do not delete the original once you have the JPG.

Transparency and quality notes

JPG cannot store transparency, so if the AVIF has transparent areas — uncommon for photos, possible for graphics — they become white in the JPG. If transparency matters, convert to PNG instead: AVIF to PNG keeps it.

On quality, use a high setting. AVIF's whole advantage is how much detail it packs into a small file, and a low JPG quality setting throws that detail away. At 92% the JPG keeps essentially everything the eye can see; below 80% you start to pay for the conversion with visible softness, particularly in fine textures like hair, foliage and fabric.

Questions people ask

Why won't my computer open AVIF files?

AVIF is a new format, and older software predates it. Windows Photos on older Windows versions, many upload forms and most old apps cannot read AVIF. Current browsers can — drag the file into a browser window to view it — and converting it to JPG makes it readable everywhere.

Why does the converter say my browser can't decode AVIF?

The converter uses your browser to read the AVIF file, and some browsers — older versions, and some in-app browsers — cannot decode AVIF. Update the browser, try a different one, or use GIMP, which decodes AVIF itself on any computer.

Is AVIF better than JPG?

On size, yes — AVIF files are often around half the size of an equivalent JPG. On compatibility, no — JPG works everywhere and AVIF does not yet. AVIF is the better archive format; JPG is the better sharing format, for now.
