How to convert HEIC to JPG on Android

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 16 November 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026

Someone sends you a photo from their iPhone, and your Android phone either refuses to open it or shows a blank thumbnail. The file is HEIC — Apple's format — and Android support for it is patchy at best. Here is how to turn it into a JPG your phone can actually use.

Why Android struggles with HEIC

Since iOS 11, iPhones save photos as HEIC by default because it stores the same picture in about half the space of a JPG. Inside the Apple world that works perfectly. Apple's HEIF and HEVC support page confirms the format arrived with iOS 11 and stores the same picture in less space at the same quality. The moment the photo crosses to Android, though, the support becomes a lottery: it depends on your phone maker, your Android version and the app you are trying to open it in.

Google Photos can display many HEIC photos, and the gallery app on newer Samsung phones opens them too. But even when a photo opens, that is usually where the luck ends: you often cannot edit it, attach it to a form, upload it to a website or share it into an app that expects a JPG. Some apps show the photo and others show nothing at all, with no useful error message either way. JPG, by contrast, opens everywhere on Android without a second thought. So the fix is not to fight your phone's gallery — it is to convert the photo to JPG once, and then use the JPG everywhere.

Method 1: convert in the browser (fastest, and best for several photos)

When the sender has fired over a dozen iPhone photos at once, this is the route that saves the evening: Chrome converts the whole set in a single pass, with no Play Store install, and the decoding happens on the handset itself, so the pictures are never sent to a server.

  1. Open the converter in Chrome. Go to our HEIC to JPG tool — it works in Chrome on Android with nothing to install.
  2. Pick the HEIC files. Tap the upload area, then Files when Chrome offers a source, and tick every HEIC file you need — Chrome lets you pick a batch from Downloads, Google Drive or your gallery in one go.
  3. Stick with JPG. JPG is the format every Android app, form and email client opens without hesitation, so leave the output there. PNG is worth picking only if you plan to keep editing the picture, since it avoids the extra compression round altogether.
  4. Convert, then save the JPGs. Hit Convert and let the phone decode the files where they sit. The finished JPGs can then be saved one by one, or as a single ZIP when you converted a batch — Chrome drops them into Downloads, where your gallery picks them up.
  5. Check one result before you use it. Open a converted JPG in your gallery and confirm it looks right and the file ends in .jpg. Then use that file for the form or upload, not the original HEIC.

If the finished JPGs are destined for a Windows computer, the same in-browser approach works on the PC too — our guide How to Convert HEIC to JPG on Windows 11 covers that side. And for the wider story of the format, and the places it breaks on phones and computers alike, see HEIC Photos Won't Open? How to Convert Them on Any Device.

Method 2: view it in Google Photos — and know its limit

Before converting, it is worth checking whether you even need to. Open the photo in Google Photos: on many Android phones it will simply display, and if all you wanted was to look at the picture, you are done — no conversion needed.

But do not mistake viewing for converting. Google Photos has no save-as-JPG button on Android, and saving the photo to your device keeps the original HEIC file. So the moment you need to attach the photo to a form, upload it to a website or send it to an app that only accepts JPG, Google Photos cannot help — go back to the browser method above. The same is true of Samsung Gallery on phones that open HEIC: viewing works, exporting a JPG copy does not.

Method 3: ask the sender to resend it as a JPG

Sometimes the easiest conversion is the one you never have to do. If the photo came from an iPhone, the sender can send you a JPG instead — and how they send it matters more than anything else.

If they send the photo through WhatsApp as a photo, WhatsApp converts it to JPG automatically on the way — the file that arrives on your phone is already a JPG. But if they send it as a document (the paperclip, then Document), WhatsApp sends the original file untouched, and you receive a HEIC again. So ask them to send it as a normal photo, not as a document. The same applies to most chat apps: sending as a photo converts, sending as a file preserves. The iPhone can also convert on the sender's side: when a photo is shared to a device that does not support HEIF, Apple's sharing guide notes it may automatically be shared in a more compatible format, such as JPEG.

If the same person keeps sending you HEIC files, they can stop the iPhone saving HEIC in the first place: Settings, then Camera, then Formats, then Most Compatible. From then on their new photos are JPG — our guide How to Convert HEIC to JPG on iPhone explains that setting, including the trade-off that JPG photos take roughly twice the storage space.

Quality, moving pictures and keeping the original

Expect the JPG to be a touch larger than the HEIC it came from: HEIC is the more efficient compressor, and JPG is lossy, so some data is discarded in the switch. In practice the loss is invisible — at the tool's default 92% quality the photo looks the same on your screen, because the pixels are unchanged and only the container is different. Just don't make a habit of converting the same picture repeatedly, since every re-encode throws away a little more.

There is one catch with photos that move. A Live Photo from an iPhone is a still frame with a short video clip attached, and a JPG only preserves the still frame — the motion is gone. If the sender's moving moment matters, ask them to keep the original Live Photo, and use the JPG only where a plain still is what's needed. And whichever route you took, keep the original HEIC on the phone until the JPG has been opened where it counts: conversion should leave you with two copies, never cost you the only one.

Questions people ask

Can Android open HEIC files?

Sometimes, but not reliably. Google Photos can display many HEIC photos, and the gallery app on newer Samsung phones opens them too. But support varies by phone maker and Android version, and even when a photo opens, you usually cannot edit it, upload it to a form, or share it to an app that expects JPG. Viewing a HEIC file is not the same as having a JPG of it.

Can I convert HEIC to JPG on Android without installing an app?

Yes. Open our HEIC to JPG tool in Chrome on your Android phone, pick the HEIC photos, choose JPG and download the results — no Play Store install, and nothing sent to a server, since the conversion runs on the phone itself. For a whole batch at once, it is the quickest route there is.

Does Google Photos convert HEIC to JPG?

No — Google Photos can display HEIC photos, but it has no save-as-JPG button on Android, and saving the photo to your device keeps the original HEIC file. If you only need to look at the photo, Google Photos is enough. If you need a JPG for a form, an upload or another app, convert it with a browser converter instead.

Does converting HEIC to JPG lose quality?

A fraction, and you won't see it. HEIC stores the same picture more efficiently, so the JPG ends up bigger, and JPG drops a little data each time it saves. At the tool's default of 92% quality, the photo looks identical in normal viewing — the pixels are unchanged, only the encoding is different.
