How to convert HEIC to JPG on Android

Someone sends you a photo from their iPhone, and your Android phone either refuses to open it or shows a blank thumbnail. The file is HEIC — Apple's format — and Android support for it is patchy at best. Here is how to turn it into a JPG your phone can actually use.

Why Android struggles with HEIC

Since iOS 11, iPhones save photos as HEIC by default because it stores the same picture in about half the space of a JPG. Inside the Apple world that works perfectly. The moment the photo crosses to Android, though, the support becomes a lottery: it depends on your phone maker, your Android version and the app you are trying to open it in.

Google Photos can display many HEIC photos, and the gallery app on newer Samsung phones opens them too. But even when a photo opens, that is usually where the luck ends: you often cannot edit it, attach it to a form, upload it to a website or share it into an app that expects a JPG. Some apps show the photo and others show nothing at all, with no useful error message either way. JPG, by contrast, opens everywhere on Android without a second thought. So the fix is not to fight your phone's gallery — it is to convert the photo to JPG once, and then use the JPG everywhere.

Method 1: convert in the browser (fastest, and best for several photos)

This is the method to use whenever you have more than one photo, because it converts a whole batch at once and needs no app at all. The conversion runs on your phone, so your photos are never uploaded anywhere.

  1. Open the converter in Chrome. Go to our HEIC to JPG tool — it works in Chrome on Android with nothing to install.
  2. Pick your photos. Tap the upload area and choose the HEIC files from your phone. You can select several at once.
  3. Choose JPG. JPG is the safe, universal option for forms, emails and every Android app. Choose PNG only if you plan to edit the picture further and want to avoid any extra compression.
  4. Convert and download. Press Convert, wait a moment while the decoding happens on your device, then download each photo — or all of them at once as a ZIP if you converted a batch.
  5. Check one result before you use it. Open a converted JPG in your gallery and confirm it looks right and the file ends in .jpg. Then use that file for the form or upload, not the original HEIC.

If the photo needs to go to a Windows computer instead, the same browser method works there too — our guide How to Convert HEIC to JPG on Windows 11 covers the PC side. For the full picture of what HEIC is and where it fails, see HEIC Photos Won't Open? How to Convert Them on Any Device.

Method 2: view it in Google Photos — and know its limit

Before converting, it is worth checking whether you even need to. Open the photo in Google Photos: on many Android phones it will simply display, and if all you wanted was to look at the picture, you are done — no conversion needed.

But do not mistake viewing for converting. Google Photos has no save-as-JPG button on Android, and saving the photo to your device keeps the original HEIC file. So the moment you need to attach the photo to a form, upload it to a website or send it to an app that only accepts JPG, Google Photos cannot help — go back to the browser method above. The same is true of Samsung Gallery on phones that open HEIC: viewing works, exporting a JPG copy does not.

Method 3: ask the sender to resend it as a JPG

Sometimes the easiest conversion is the one you never have to do. If the photo came from an iPhone, the sender can send you a JPG instead — and how they send it matters more than anything else.

If they send the photo through WhatsApp as a photo, WhatsApp converts it to JPG automatically on the way — the file that arrives on your phone is already a JPG. But if they send it as a document (the paperclip, then Document), WhatsApp sends the original file untouched, and you receive a HEIC again. So ask them to send it as a normal photo, not as a document. The same applies to most chat apps: sending as a photo converts, sending as a file preserves.

If the same person keeps sending you HEIC files, they can stop the iPhone saving HEIC in the first place: Settings, then Camera, then Formats, then Most Compatible. From then on their new photos are JPG — our guide How to Convert HEIC to JPG on iPhone explains that setting, including the trade-off that JPG photos take roughly twice the storage space.

What about quality, Live Photos and originals?

Converting to JPG does lose a little quality on paper — HEIC compresses more efficiently, so the same photo as a JPG is usually a bigger file, and JPG is a lossy format. In practice, at around 92% quality the difference is not visible in normal viewing: the pixels are the same, only the file encoding changes. What you should not do is convert the same photo back and forth several times, because every re-encode discards a little more detail.

Live Photos need one extra thought. A Live Photo is a still image plus a short video clip, and converting it to JPG keeps the still frame — the motion part does not survive as a JPG. If the movement matters, ask the sender for the original Live Photo as well, and use the JPG only where a still image is what is asked for. And whatever method you use, keep the original HEIC until the converted file is confirmed working: conversion should add a usable copy, never destroy the only one you have.

Questions people ask

Can Android open HEIC files?

Sometimes, but not reliably. Google Photos can display many HEIC photos, and the gallery app on newer Samsung phones opens them too. But support varies by phone maker and Android version, and even when a photo opens, you usually cannot edit it, upload it to a form, or share it to an app that expects JPG. Viewing a HEIC file is not the same as having a JPG of it.

Can I convert HEIC to JPG on Android without installing an app?

Yes. Open a browser-based converter such as our HEIC to JPG tool in Chrome on your Android phone, pick the HEIC photos, choose JPG and download the results. Nothing is installed and nothing is uploaded — the conversion runs on your phone. That is the fastest route when you have several photos to convert at once.

Does Google Photos convert HEIC to JPG?

No — Google Photos can display HEIC photos, but it has no save-as-JPG button on Android, and saving the photo to your device keeps the original HEIC file. If you only need to look at the photo, Google Photos is enough. If you need a JPG for a form, an upload or another app, convert it with a browser converter instead.

Will converting HEIC to JPG reduce quality?

A little, but not visibly at a good quality setting. HEIC compresses more efficiently than JPG, so the same photo as a JPG is usually a bigger file, and JPG is a lossy format. At around 92% quality the difference is not visible in normal viewing — the pixels are the same, only the file encoding changes.
