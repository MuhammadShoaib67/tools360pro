How to convert WebP to PNG

WebP is a fine format until something refuses it — an upload form, an old app, a printer, a design tool that predates it. PNG is the format that works everywhere, and converting WebP to PNG keeps the picture and its transparency while trading the small file for universal acceptance. Here is how, and what the trade costs.

Why convert back to PNG

The usual reason is a refusal: a form that accepts JPG and PNG but not WebP, an older version of a design tool, a print workflow, or a colleague whose software predates the format. WebP is well supported in browsers, but outside them it is still the new arrival, and plenty of everyday software has not caught up. PNG, by contrast, has been readable by essentially everything since the late 1990s.

The other reason is editing. PNG is a lossless working format: open it, edit it and save it as often as you like without losing anything further. A lossy WebP re-saved after editing would lose a little more each time, so converting to PNG first freezes the quality where it is and gives you a stable file to work on.

A third situation is sharing. A WebP attached to an email or sent in a chat may arrive as a file the recipient's older phone or computer cannot open, while a PNG opens everywhere without a second thought. Converting before sending costs you a bigger attachment, but it costs the recipient nothing — no confusion, no 'I can't open this' reply, and no second attempt.

What changes in the conversion

The file gets bigger — often several times larger than the WebP. That is the whole trade: WebP's clever compression is what made the file small, and PNG's lossless storage is what makes it universal. A 100 KB WebP photo commonly becomes a PNG of several hundred kilobytes, and a detailed image can grow past a megabyte. Expect it, and do not mistake the bigger file for a better picture.

What does not change is the picture itself. The pixels are preserved exactly as decoded, and transparency carries over: a WebP with a see-through background becomes a PNG with a see-through background. One honest limit — if the WebP was lossy, its compression artefacts are part of the pixels now, and converting to PNG preserves them faithfully. The conversion never improves quality; it only stops further loss.

How to convert WebP to PNG

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the WebP to PNG Converter: drop one or more WebP images onto the box, press Convert, and download each PNG or grab them all as a ZIP. There is no quality setting to choose, because PNG is lossless. The conversion runs on your own device, so the pictures are never uploaded anywhere.
  2. With GIMP, the free desktop editor. Open the WebP in GIMP, choose File, then Export As, and change the filename to end in .png. GIMP handles WebP natively and is the best choice when you are about to edit the picture anyway.
  3. On a Mac, with Preview. On macOS 11 (Big Sur) and later, Preview opens WebP files. Open the image, choose File, then Export, and pick PNG as the format. On older macOS versions Preview cannot open WebP, so use the browser converter or GIMP instead.
  4. Check transparency if it matters. Open the converted PNG and view it against a coloured background. If the background shows through the transparent areas, transparency survived the conversion — it should, but a ten-second check beats a rejected upload.

When JPG would be the better target

If the picture has no transparency and the destination accepts JPG, convert to JPG instead — WebP to JPG produces a much smaller file than WebP to PNG, and JPG is accepted everywhere PNG is. PNG is the right choice only when you need transparency or a lossless file for editing.

And if the destination is a website or a modern app, consider not converting at all. WebP is the smallest file of the three formats, and every modern browser displays it. The conversion is worth doing when compatibility demands it — not as a reflex.

Questions people ask

Does converting WebP to PNG keep transparency?

Yes. WebP stores transparency in an alpha channel, and PNG has one too, so transparent areas survive the conversion. Check the result against a coloured background to confirm — if the colour shows through, transparency is intact.

Why is the PNG so much bigger than the WebP?

WebP compresses far more aggressively than PNG. A WebP photo of around 100 KB commonly becomes a PNG of several hundred kilobytes or more. The bigger file is the normal price of PNG's lossless, universally supported storage.

Will converting WebP to PNG improve quality?

No. The conversion preserves the pixels exactly as they are in the WebP — including any artefacts from lossy WebP compression. It prevents further quality loss during editing, but it cannot restore detail the WebP already discarded.
