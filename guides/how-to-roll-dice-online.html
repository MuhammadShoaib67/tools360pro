How to Roll Dice Online

No dice in the house? An online dice roller covers the whole standard set — from a 4-sided pyramid to a 20-sided die — with totals and a history of recent rolls. This guide shows how to roll, explains dice notation, and answers the fairness question every player asks.

Why roll dice online

Physical dice get lost, roll under furniture, and are never around when you need them — especially the odd ones. Board games need a d6, but role-playing games use the full set: d4, d6, d8, d10, d12 and d20, and few people own all six. An online roller covers the whole set, shows every die's result and the total, and keeps a history of recent rolls so a disputed result can be checked in seconds.

Online rolling also suits remote play. When a group plays over a call or a chat, a shared roller gives everyone the same numbers at the same time, with no one having to trust that a die rolled off-camera really showed what was claimed. The history of recent rolls settles any dispute in seconds, because the result is written down where everyone can see it.

Dice notation explained

Dice notation is a shorthand used in tabletop games: the number before the d is how many dice you roll, and the number after it is how many sides each die has. 2d6 means two 6-sided dice; 1d20 means one 20-sided die; 3d8 means three 8-sided dice. The total is the sum of all the dice rolled.

Different games use different dice. Board games mostly use d6, while role-playing games use the full set. A d20 decides most actions in games like Dungeons and Dragons, where a roll of 20 is a critical success and a roll of 1 is a critical failure. Modifiers are sometimes added — 1d20+5 means roll a d20 and add 5 — which this roller leaves to you: roll the dice, then add the modifier to the total yourself, and the history will show the unmodified rolls.

Are online dice fair?

A fair die gives every face the same chance. Physical dice can be biased by uneven weight, rounded corners or worn faces — which is why serious players test their dice in salt water. A digital roller has no such problem: the random number generator has no memory, no wear, and no preference for any face.

Good online rollers use the browser's cryptographic random number generator, the same source browsers use for secure random values. That said, randomness includes streaks. Rolling three 1s in a row on a d20 is unlikely but entirely possible, and it does not mean the roller is broken or the next roll is due a high number. Each roll is independent — the dice have no memory of what came before.

Other ways to roll

Phone apps exist for dice rolling, usually with 3D animations and sound effects, but they need installing and often show adverts. Voice assistants can roll a die on request, which is handy mid-game but slow for several dice at once. A spreadsheet can simulate dice with a random function, which suits bulk rolls for statistics or game design.

For a quick roll during a game, a browser roller is fastest — no install, no account, and the result appears in one click, with the history there to settle any dispute about what was rolled.

Questions people ask

Are online dice rolls really random?

Yes, when the roller uses the browser's cryptographic random number generator. Every face has an equal chance, and each roll is independent of the last.

What does 2d6 mean?

Dice notation: the number before the d is how many dice you roll, and the number after it is how many sides each die has. 2d6 means two 6-sided dice, added together.

Can I roll more than 6 dice at once?

This roller caps at 6 dice per roll to keep the display readable. For larger rolls, roll in batches and add the totals together.

What is a natural 20?

A roll of 20 on a 20-sided die — a critical success in role-playing games like Dungeons and Dragons. A roll of 1 is a critical failure.
