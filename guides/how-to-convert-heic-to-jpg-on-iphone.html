How to convert HEIC to JPG on iPhone

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 15 November 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026

Your iPhone saves every photo as HEIC — then the form, the website or the Windows computer you send it to refuses to open it. You do not need to install an app to fix that: convert the photos on the phone itself, in the browser, in a minute.

Why your iPhone saves photos as HEIC

Since iOS 11, iPhones save photos in the HEIC format by default because it stores the same picture in about half the space of a JPG. That saves storage on your phone and keeps more photos in iCloud for the same space. Inside the Apple world it works perfectly: the Photos app, AirDrop and iMessage all understand HEIC without you ever noticing.

The trouble starts the moment a photo leaves that world. Windows PCs, many application forms, older Android phones, marketplace listings and plenty of websites either refuse HEIC files outright or show them as blank. JPG, by contrast, opens on every device and every website built in the last thirty years. That is why the same photo often needs to be a JPG the moment it is going somewhere other than another Apple device — and why this is one of the most searched iPhone questions there is.

Method 1: convert in the browser (fastest for several photos)

When a whole album needs converting — say, every photo from a holiday you want to email — this is the quickest route. Safari handles the entire batch in one go, with nothing to install from the App Store, and the work happens inside Safari on your iPhone, so no photo ever leaves the handset.

  1. Open the converter in Safari. Go to our HEIC to JPG tool — it works in Safari on iPhone with nothing to install.
  2. Pick your photos from the library. Tap the upload area, then Photo Library when Safari asks where to pick from, and tap each photo you need — Safari lets you tick a whole run of shots before you confirm.
  3. Choose JPG as the output. Leave the output on JPG: it is the format every form, email client and Windows PC accepts without complaint. Switch to PNG only when you intend to keep editing the picture afterwards, since PNG skips the extra compression step entirely.
  4. Convert and download the results. Tap Convert and give Safari a moment to decode the files on the phone itself. Each JPG then appears ready to save, and when you converted a batch you can pull the lot down in one ZIP instead of tapping through them one by one.
  5. Check one result before you send it. Open a converted JPG and confirm it looks right and the file ends in .jpg. Then send that file, not the original HEIC.

Sending the pictures to a Windows PC rather than a website? The same in-browser trick works on the computer as well, and our guide How to Convert HEIC to JPG on Windows 11 walks through the PC side, Microsoft's codecs included. For the background — what HEIC actually is and where it tends to break — our overview HEIC Photos Won't Open? explains the format and every device that struggles with it.

Method 2: use the Files app for one or two photos

For a single photo, the built-in Files app can do the job with no website at all. Open the photo in the Photos app, tap Share, then Save to Files and pick a folder. On recent iOS versions this saves a JPG copy of the photo — the iPhone converts it as part of the share.

Try it with one photo first and check the result: in the Files app, long-press the saved file and look at its name. If it ends in .jpg, the method works on your iOS version and you can use it for the rest. If it still ends in .heic, your iOS version kept the original format, and the browser method above is the reliable route instead. Either way, do not delete the original until you have confirmed the JPG opens where you need it.

Method 3: stop the iPhone saving HEIC in the first place

If you are tired of converting, change the camera setting so new photos are JPG from the start. Go to Settings, then Camera, then Formats, and choose Most Compatible. From then on, the iPhone saves new photos as JPG instead of HEIC, and the problem never comes back.

Two honest trade-offs come with that change. First, JPG photos take roughly twice the space of HEIC, so your phone storage and iCloud fill up faster — on a phone that is already nearly full, that matters. Second, the setting only changes new photos. Every HEIC photo already in your library stays HEIC, so the photos you need today still have to be converted with one of the methods above. Most people who send photos to forms and Windows computers find the trade worth it; people who almost never share outside Apple devices can leave High Efficiency switched on.

What about quality, Live Photos and originals?

On paper a JPG is a step down: HEIC packs the same image into a smaller file, so the JPG that comes out is normally larger, and JPG throws away a little data every time it saves. In everyday viewing, though, you will not spot the difference — the tool's default of 92% quality keeps the picture looking identical on a phone screen or a print, because only the way the pixels are stored has changed. The one habit to avoid is round-tripping: converting a JPG back to HEIC and then to JPG again, since each fresh save shaves off a little more detail.

Live Photos deserve a pause before you convert. A Live Photo is really two things joined together — the still frame and a few seconds of video around it — and a JPG can only carry the still frame, so the movement is left behind. When the motion is the point of the picture, hold on to the original in the Photos app and send the JPG only to places that asked for a still image. More generally, treat conversion as making a second copy: leave the original HEIC in your library until you have opened the JPG where it is needed and seen it work.

Questions people ask

Can I convert HEIC to JPG on iPhone without installing an app?

Yes. Open our HEIC to JPG tool in Safari, pick the photos from your library, choose JPG and download the results — no App Store download, and no photo sent to a server, because the conversion runs inside Safari on your iPhone. The Files app method below needs no app either, but it is slower for more than a couple of photos.

Will converting HEIC to JPG reduce quality?

Slightly, on paper — and not in any way you will notice. HEIC squeezes the same picture into a smaller file, so the JPG comes out larger, and JPG discards a little data each time it saves. At the tool's default of 92% quality, though, the picture looks identical in normal viewing, because only the file encoding has changed, not the pixels themselves.

How do I stop my iPhone saving photos as HEIC?

Go to Settings, then Camera, then Formats, and choose Most Compatible. From then on the iPhone saves new photos as JPG instead of HEIC. Photos already saved as HEIC stay HEIC — that setting only changes new photos, so existing pictures still need converting.

Does the Files app convert HEIC to JPG?

On recent iOS versions, sharing a HEIC photo to the Files app usually saves it as a JPG copy. Try it with one photo first and check the file extension, because the behaviour can vary by iOS version and by how the photo is shared. For several photos at once, a browser converter is faster and more predictable.
