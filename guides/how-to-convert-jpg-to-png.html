How to convert JPG to PNG

Converting JPG to PNG is the rare conversion that makes a file bigger — sometimes several times bigger — without improving it in any way. That sounds pointless until you meet the situations where PNG is required: editing without further loss, tools that only accept PNG, and documents that need a lossless master. Here is what JPG to PNG actually does, the honest limits, and how to do it in three places.

The honest truth: quality never comes back

JPG compression throws image data away permanently. When a JPG is created, the encoder discards subtle colour information the eye barely notices — and once it is gone, no conversion can bring it back. Converting that JPG to PNG simply stores the already-damaged pixels losslessly: every pixel is preserved exactly as it is, including the blur, the blocky patches and the smudged edges the JPG compression left behind. A soft JPG becomes a soft PNG, just a heavier one.

The file gets bigger for the same reason. PNG stores every pixel exactly, while JPG stores a clever approximation. A 300 KB JPG photo can easily become a 1-2 MB PNG, and a detailed photo can grow several times over. That growth is not a bug and not a sign of higher quality — it is the price of the lossless format, and you should expect it before you convert.

Why convert anyway

The best reason is editing. Every time a JPG is opened, edited and saved again, it loses a little more quality — the damage accumulates with each generation. PNG has no such problem: open it, edit it and save it as often as you like, and the pixels stay exactly as they are. Converting a JPG to PNG before a round of edits freezes the quality where it is, so the editing itself costs nothing further.

The other reasons are practical. Some tools, printers, game engines and design workflows accept PNG and not JPG. Some documentation and print pipelines specify PNG as the house format. And some people simply want a stable, lossless master of a picture before they crop, annotate or recolour it. In all of those cases the bigger file is a fair trade for a format that will not degrade.

How to convert JPG to PNG

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the JPG to PNG Converter: drop one or more JPG images onto the box, press Convert, and download each PNG or grab them all as a ZIP. There is no quality setting to choose, because PNG is lossless — the output is simply the JPG's pixels, stored exactly. The conversion runs on your own device, so the pictures are never uploaded anywhere.
  2. On Windows, with Paint. Open the JPG in Paint, choose File, then Save as, then PNG picture. It works on every version of Windows and needs no download, but it converts one file at a time.
  3. On a Mac, with Preview. Open the JPG in Preview, choose File, then Export, and pick PNG as the format. Like Paint, Preview converts one file at a time — fine for the occasional picture, slow for a whole folder.
  4. Expect a bigger file — that is normal. Do not be surprised when the PNG is several times the size of the JPG. If the PNG is destined for the web, consider compressing it afterwards, or converting to WebP instead — JPG to WebP gives you a smaller file than the JPG you started with.

What not to expect

Do not expect a sharper picture, a smaller file, or a transparent background. Transparency cannot be created by converting: a JPG never had any transparent pixels, so the PNG has a solid background too — removing a background is a different job entirely, done by an editor or a background remover, not by a format converter.

And do not convert to PNG in the hope of fixing a low-quality JPG. Upscaling tools can make a small image larger, and careful editing can improve how it looks, but no format change restores data that JPG compression discarded. Convert to PNG for editing and compatibility — those are the jobs it is genuinely good for.

Questions people ask

Will converting JPG to PNG improve quality?

No. JPG compression discards image data permanently, and converting to PNG only stores the remaining pixels losslessly — the blur and artefacts stay exactly as they are. The conversion is useful for editing without further loss and for compatibility, not for improving the picture.

Why is the PNG so much bigger than the JPG?

PNG stores every pixel exactly, while JPG stores a compressed approximation. A photo JPG of a few hundred kilobytes commonly becomes a PNG of one to several megabytes. The bigger file is the normal, expected result of a lossless format.

Can I add transparency by converting JPG to PNG?

No. A JPG has no transparency information, so the PNG produced from it has a solid background. To get a transparent background you need to remove the background in an editor or with a background-removal tool, then save the result as a PNG.
