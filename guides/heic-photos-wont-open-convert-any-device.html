HEIC photos won't open? How to convert them on any device

You take a photo on your iPhone, send it to your Windows laptop, and the file will not open — or worse, an upload form rejects it without explanation. The culprit is almost always the same: your photo is saved as HEIC, a format Apple loves and the rest of the world barely supports. Here is what HEIC is, where it breaks, and how to convert it to JPG on any device.

What HEIC is — and why your iPhone uses it

HEIC is Apple's version of the HEIF image format, and iPhones have used it as the default camera format since iOS 11. The reason is simple arithmetic: HEIC stores the same photograph in roughly half the space of a JPG at the same quality, so a phone with limited storage can hold twice as many photos. It also supports features JPG cannot, such as storing a burst of shots or a Live Photo's short video clip inside a single file. For Apple, it was an easy choice.

The trade-off is compatibility. JPG has been the universal photo format for thirty years — every operating system, every browser, every printer and every upload form understands it. HEIC is supported natively on Apple devices and on recent versions of Windows 11 and Android, but support everywhere else is patchy or absent. The file itself is not broken; the device or website you are sending it to simply does not know how to read it. That is also why the same photo can look fine on your phone and fail completely on your laptop.

Where HEIC files refuse to open

The most common failure is a Windows PC without the HEIF extensions installed: double-clicking the photo opens an error instead of the picture, and the Photos app offers to take you to the Microsoft Store. Upload forms are the second trap — job portals, exam applications, visa forms and marketplace listings often accept only JPG, PNG or PDF, and a HEIC file is rejected silently or with a vague "unsupported format" message. Older Android phones, many email clients and plenty of websites have the same problem, and even some photo printers cannot read HEIC files from a memory card.

There is one more subtle case: messaging apps. Some apps convert HEIC to JPG automatically when you share, so the photo arrives fine; others send the original file untouched, so the person on the other end gets a file they cannot open. If a photo you sent shows up as a blank or a download that never previews, HEIC is the first thing to check. The fix in every case is the same: convert the photo to JPG once, and the file works everywhere.

How to convert HEIC on each device

On iPhone, stop the problem at the source. Open Settings, go to Camera, then Formats, and choose Most Compatible. From then on, new photos are saved as JPG. This does not change photos you have already taken — those stay HEIC until you convert them — but it prevents the problem from recurring.

On Windows 11, the Photos app can open HEIC files once the free HEIF Image Extensions are installed from the Microsoft Store; after that, you can open a photo and use Save As to export a JPG copy. On older Windows 10 machines the extensions are sometimes a paid download, which is why a browser tool is often the quicker route.

On Mac, the Preview app opens HEIC files natively: open the photo, choose File, then Export, and pick JPEG as the format. On Android, Google Photos can open HEIC files on recent versions, and sharing a photo from the gallery to an app usually converts it automatically — but downloading the original file from a cloud backup gives you the HEIC file again, so conversion is still needed when a form demands a JPG.

JPG or PNG — which should you pick?

For almost every real-world purpose, pick JPG. It is the format every form, website and printer accepts, and at 90–92% quality a converted photo looks identical to the HEIC original in normal viewing. JPG files are also smaller than PNG, which matters when a form caps uploads at 200KB or 1MB.

Choose PNG only when you plan to edit the photo further — cropping, retouching, adding text — because PNG is lossless and will not accumulate extra compression damage through repeated saves. PNG is also the right choice for screenshots and images with sharp text. The price is file size: a PNG of a photograph can be several times larger than the same picture as a JPG, so do not use it for uploads with a size limit.

What survives conversion — and what does not

A converted JPG keeps the picture itself, its dimensions and usually its basic details such as the date it was taken. Two things do not survive: the space saving — a JPG of the same photo is normally a little larger than the HEIC original, because HEIC compresses more efficiently — and Live Photo motion, since a JPG is a still image and the short video clip inside a Live Photo has nowhere to go. If the motion matters, export the video separately from your Photos app before converting.

One habit is worth keeping: always convert from the original HEIC file, and keep that original as your master copy. Converting a JPG back to HEIC and then to JPG again throws away a little detail each time, while a single conversion from the original is visually lossless. And if you take a lot of photos for forms and listings, switching your iPhone to Most Compatible now will save you this conversion step entirely in future.

Questions people ask

Why are my iPhone photos HEIC instead of JPG?

Since iOS 11, iPhones save photos as HEIC by default because the format stores the same picture in roughly half the space of a JPG, which saves storage on your phone. Apple chose it for efficiency, not for compatibility. You can change it in Settings under Camera, then Formats, by choosing Most Compatible — new photos will then be saved as JPG.

Will converting HEIC to JPG reduce quality?

At a sensible quality setting, no visible difference. HEIC is more efficient than JPG, so a JPG of the same photo is usually a little larger — but at 90–92% quality the picture looks identical in normal viewing. Avoid converting the same photo back and forth between formats, because every lossy conversion discards a little detail. Convert once, from the original HEIC file, and keep the original as your master copy.

Can I convert HEIC to JPG without installing anything?

Yes. A browser-based converter does the whole job with no install: open the HEIC to JPG tool, drop your photos in, and download the JPGs. Because the conversion runs on your own device, your photos are never uploaded anywhere, which matters for personal pictures. It works on Windows, Mac, Android and Chromebook alike, since the browser is the only thing you need.
