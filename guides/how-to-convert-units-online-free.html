How to convert units online free

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 26 November 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026

A unit converter turns one measurement into another — kilometres into miles, kilograms into pounds, Celsius into Fahrenheit — in seconds, with no maths on your side. This guide explains how online conversion works, which conversions people actually use, the mistakes that produce wrong answers, and how to convert units online free with the browser tool on this site.

What a unit converter does

A unit converter is a calculator that knows the fixed relationship between two units of the same kind. One inch is exactly 2.54 centimetres, one mile is exactly 1.60934 kilometres, and one kilogram is 2.20462 pounds. Because those relationships never change, a converter only has to multiply your number by the right factor — and the result is exact, not an estimate. That is why converting units online is so reliable: there is no judgement involved, only arithmetic, and a good converter shows you the factor it used so you can check the maths yourself.

The reason we need converters at all is that the world runs on two measurement systems at once. Most countries use the metric system — metres, kilograms, litres and Celsius — while the United States still uses imperial units — feet, pounds, gallons and Fahrenheit — in daily life. A recipe from an American site lists cups and ounces, a European pattern lists grams and centimetres, a car in the US reports miles per gallon while a car in Pakistan reports kilometres per litre. Every time those worlds meet, something has to be converted, and doing it by hand is where the errors creep in.

The conversions people use most

Length and weight

Length is the everyday leader: millimetres to inches for screen sizes and screws, centimetres to feet for height, kilometres to miles for road trips. A 6-foot person is about 183 centimetres, a 10-kilometre run is about 6.2 miles, and a 13-inch laptop screen is about 33 centimetres across.

Weight follows close behind. Travellers convert kilograms to pounds for luggage limits, cooks convert grams to ounces for recipes, and gym-goers convert pounds to kilograms for body weight. Seventy kilograms is about 154 pounds, and one ounce is 28.35 grams — small enough that a recipe converted carelessly can go noticeably wrong.

Temperature, volume, speed, area and data

Temperature is the one people argue about: 20 °C is a comfortable 68 °F, 30 °C is a hot 86 °F, and 0 °C is freezing at 32 °F. Weather reports, oven settings and air-conditioner thermostats all live in one system or the other, so this conversion comes up daily for anyone reading foreign content.

Volume matters in kitchens and at fuel stations: litres to gallons, millilitres to fluid ounces, cups to millilitres. Speed matters on the road — kilometres per hour to miles per hour — and area matters in property, where square feet and square metres describe the same flat in very different numbers. Data storage is the newest category: kilobytes, megabytes and gigabytes, where the decimal and binary definitions quietly disagree (more on that below).

Why temperature conversion is different

Almost every conversion is a simple multiplication, but temperature is the exception: Celsius and Fahrenheit do not share a zero point, so you cannot just multiply. Water freezes at 0 °C but at 32 °F, and boils at 100 °C but at 212 °F. The formulas are Celsius to Fahrenheit: multiply by 9/5 and add 32; Fahrenheit to Celsius: subtract 32 and multiply by 5/9. Kelvin, the scientific scale, is simply Celsius plus 273.15. A converter handles the offset behind the scenes, which is exactly why it is worth using one instead of guessing — the "add 30 and double it" shortcut people use for Celsius to Fahrenheit is off by several degrees at exactly the temperatures that matter.

There is one neat coincidence worth knowing: at −40, the two scales meet, so −40 °C is exactly −40 °F. Everywhere else they diverge, and the gap between the scales grows as the temperature rises — which is why a 10-degree change in Celsius is an 18-degree change in Fahrenheit.

The mistakes that give wrong answers

US gallon vs UK gallon, and fluid ounces

US gallon vs UK gallon. A US gallon is 3.785 litres, while a UK (imperial) gallon is 4.546 litres — about 20% larger. Fuel economy, tank sizes and recipes all inherit this trap, and it is the single most common volume error in online conversion.

Fluid ounce vs ounce. A fluid ounce measures volume, an ounce measures weight — they share a name and nothing else. Eight fluid ounces of water happens to weigh about eight ounces, which is why the confusion survives, but eight fluid ounces of honey weighs far more, and converting one as if it were the other silently breaks recipes.

Kilobyte vs kibibyte, and stone

Kilobyte vs kibibyte. A kilobyte (KB) is 1,000 bytes in the decimal system, while a kibibyte (KiB) is 1,024 bytes in the binary system. Operating systems and storage makers have used the two definitions interchangeably for years, which is the usual reason a "500 GB" drive shows up smaller than expected. Our converter lists both, so pick the one your context actually means.

Stone and pounds. In the UK, body weight is often given in stone, where one stone is 14 pounds (6.35 kg). Entering "11" as pounds when you meant 11 stone gives an answer fourteen times too small — always check whether the number in front of you is stone, pounds or kilograms before converting.

The fix for all four is the same habit: check the unit you have, not just the number, and glance at the conversion factor the tool shows. A converter that displays its factor makes a wrong-unit mistake visible in seconds, while a bare answer hides it until something goes wrong.

Questions people ask

How do I convert kg to lbs?

Multiply kilograms by 2.20462. So 1 kg is about 2.205 lbs, 70 kg is about 154 lbs, and 1 lb is about 0.454 kg. Our Unit Converter does this live: pick Weight, enter the kilograms, and choose pounds.

How do I convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?

Multiply the Celsius value by 9/5 and add 32. So 20 °C is 68 °F, 30 °C is 86 °F and 0 °C is 32 °F. Temperature is the one conversion that cannot use a simple multiplier, because Celsius and Fahrenheit do not share a zero point — the converter handles the offset for you.

What is the difference between a US gallon and a UK gallon?

A US gallon is 3.785 litres, while a UK (imperial) gallon is 4.546 litres — about 20% larger. It is the most common volume mistake in online conversion, so check which gallon a recipe, fuel figure or tank size means before converting.
