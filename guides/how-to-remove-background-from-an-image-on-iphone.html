How to remove background from an image on iPhone

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 24 November 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026

You do not need to install an app, pay for a subscription, or upload your photo to a server to remove a background on an iPhone. Safari can do it free in the browser, and newer iPhones can do it inside the Photos app itself. Here is how to remove the background from an image on iPhone both ways, which one to use for product photos and portraits, and what to do when the edges come out rough.

Method 1: Safari — free, no app, nothing uploaded

No upload, no account, no watermark

The browser method uses our Background Remover, which runs a small AI model on your iPhone itself. The first time you use it, the model — about 40 MB — downloads to your device and is cached, so do that first run on Wi-Fi. After that, every photo is processed right there in Safari: the picture is never sent to a server, which matters for private family photos and unreleased product pictures, and there is no account, no watermark and no limit on full-size downloads. The same in-browser approach is used by open-source libraries such as img.ly’s background-removal-js, which removes backgrounds directly in the browser without sending the image to a server.

  1. Open Safari and go to the Background Remover. Any recent iPhone works — the tool runs in the browser, so there is nothing to install. If you will use it often, tap Share and then Add to Home Screen, so it behaves like an app icon.
  2. Tap the upload box and choose the photo. Pick from your Photo Library, or take a fresh photo with the camera. If the photo is HEIC — the format iPhones save in by default — Safari usually accepts it directly; if a particular photo will not load, convert it first with our HEIC to JPG tool and use the JPG.
  3. Press Remove background and wait a few seconds. The first run takes longer while the model downloads; later runs are quick. Your subject appears on a transparent background.
  4. Choose transparent or a colour, then download the PNG. Transparent is the right choice when the cutout will go onto another design, a listing template or a coloured page. Pick a colour — usually white — when the photo is for a marketplace or an ID card that asks for a plain background. The download is a PNG at the same size as your original photo, and the original is never modified.

Method 2: the built-in Photos trick (iOS 16 and later)

Since iOS 16, the Photos app can lift a subject out of a picture on its own. Open the photo, press and hold the subject — a person, a pet, a product — until it glows with an outline, then tap Copy or Share. Share it to Messages or WhatsApp as a sticker-like cutout, or choose Save Image to keep the lifted subject as a new photo in your library.

It is the fastest option when you only need a quick share: no website, no download, no waiting. But it is a shortcut, not a tool — you get no control over the edges, no choice of background colour, and the saved result is sized for sharing rather than for a marketplace listing that asks for a specific size or a clean white background. For anything you will publish — an Etsy or eBay listing photo, a logo on a coloured page, a portrait for a CV — the browser method gives the cleaner, full-size result.

Which method should you use?

Use the Photos trick when the cutout is going straight into a chat: sending a product photo to a buyer, making a quick sticker, sharing a pet photo with the background gone. Use the browser method when the result has to look professional — product photos for a shop, portraits for documents, images for a design — because you get the full-size PNG, the transparent option, and a plain colour background when the listing asks for one. If you need a saved transparent PNG without opening a browser, the Files app gives you one in a tap, and if you do this every day, a shortcut in the share sheet is the fastest route of all.

One honest warning about both methods: neither can rescue a bad starting photo. A cutout is only as clean as the edge it has to find, and that edge is found by contrast — a subject that stands out from what is behind it. A white shirt against a white wall, dark hair against a dark sofa, or a blurry photo taken in dim light will give soft, guessy edges in any tool, free or paid. The fix is at the taking stage: good light, a plain background behind the subject, and the subject filling the frame. Our full guide on removing a background from a photo for free works through the same steps on desktop, with more detail on the photos that cut out cleanly and the ones that fight back.

Photos that cut out cleanly — and ones that fight back

The model does its best work when the subject is in focus and clearly separated from the background: a person against a plain wall, a product on a table, a dog on grass. Fill the frame with the subject rather than cropping a tiny figure out of a wide shot, and keep the whole subject in the picture — a cut-off hand or ear cannot be restored. If the edges come out slightly soft, place the result on a background colour instead of transparency: the fringe disappears against a solid colour, while on transparency it shows as a faint halo.

After the download, the PNG is yours to use anywhere. Drop it onto a listing template, a poster, a social post or a document — and if the listing needs an exact size, resize it with our image tools rather than stretching it. One more iPhone habit worth building: check the downloaded PNG in the Files app before you upload it anywhere, because Safari saves downloads there, not in Photos. Open Files, tap Downloads, and the cutout is waiting — share it from there to any app you like.

Method 3: the Files app — a real transparent PNG, and batches

The Photos trick has one weak point: it hands you a clipboard cutout, not a file. If you need an actual image with a transparent background — for a listing, a design, or a document — the Files app does it in one tap. Since iOS 16, Files has a Remove Background Quick Action that saves a new PNG with the background removed in the same folder, leaving your original untouched, as How-To Geek's walkthrough of the feature shows.

  1. Move the photo into Files. In Photos, tap Select, mark the photo, tap Share and choose Save to Files, then pick a folder. If the photo is already in Files — a download, an attachment, a scan — skip this step.
  2. Long-press the thumbnail and choose Quick Actions, then Remove Background. Press on the file itself, not an open preview. The iPhone cuts the subject out and saves a new PNG in the same folder.
  3. Use the new PNG anywhere. Share it straight from Files to any app, or long-press it and choose Share, then Save Image to put it back in your Photos library.

One photo, or a whole folder at once

For a whole product shoot, do them in one pass: inside the folder, tap the three-dot menu, tap Select, mark every photo, then tap the three-dot menu again and choose Remove Background. Each photo gets its own transparent PNG. If Remove Background does not appear in the menu at all, the phone is probably too old for the feature — it needs iOS 16 or later — and the Safari method above is the fallback.

Method 4: Shortcuts — one tap in the share sheet

Build it once, then it lives in the share sheet

If you remove backgrounds often, stop opening a website or an app every time and build a shortcut instead. The Shortcuts app, which comes pre-installed on iPhone, has a built-in Remove Background action: open Shortcuts, tap the plus button, tap Add Action and search for "Remove Background", then add a "Save to Photo Album" action after it. Name the shortcut something plain like "Remove BG" and tap Done.

From then on, the shortcut lives in every share sheet on the phone: open a photo, tap Share, scroll to your "Remove BG" shortcut, and the cutout saves straight to your library — no paste-then-save step, no upload, nothing to install. It uses the same Apple subject-isolation as the Photos trick, so expect the same edges: quick and clean on plain backgrounds, softer on hair, fur and busy scenes. The win is repetition — ten product photos, ten taps, done.

Saving and sharing the cutout — keeping the transparency

PNG keeps the transparency — JPG does not

A cutout is only useful if the transparency survives the save. Save it as a PNG, not a JPG: MDN's image format guide puts it plainly — PNG is the format to use when transparency is needed, while JPEG has no transparency at all, so a cutout saved as JPG gets its background filled in, usually white. When you paste a copied subject into Messages, Mail or Notes, the transparency travels with it; when you save through Share and Save Image, check the file is a PNG before you send it on.

On iOS 17 and later, the Photos lift menu also offers Add Sticker, which saves the cutout to your sticker drawer for use in Messages. And remember where Safari puts downloads: Files, in the Downloads folder — open Files, tap Downloads, and share the PNG from there, because it never lands in Photos on its own. If a marketplace or a form rejects the PNG, do not re-save it as a JPG: put it on a white background first with the browser tool's colour option and download that version instead. The file stays a PNG, but now has the solid background the uploader accepts.

Questions people ask

Can I remove a photo background on iPhone without an app?

Yes, two ways. In Safari, open our free Background Remover, pick the photo and download the cutout as a PNG — nothing is installed and nothing is uploaded. Or, on iOS 16 and later, long-press the subject in the Photos app and share the lifted subject as a new image. The browser method gives you a full-size transparent PNG and a colour-background option; the Photos trick is faster for a quick share.

Does the iPhone Photos app remove backgrounds for free?

Yes, on iOS 16 and later. Open the photo, long-press the subject until it glows, then tap Copy or Share and save the lifted subject as a new image. It is free and built in, but you get no control over edges, no colour-background choice, and the result is sized for sharing rather than for a marketplace listing — for that, the browser tool gives a cleaner full-size PNG.

Why does the background remover need to download a model first?

Because the AI runs on your iPhone instead of on a server: the first time you use the Background Remover, a compact AI model of about 40 MB downloads to your device and is cached for later use. That download is the reason your photo never has to be uploaded anywhere — the cutting happens in your browser, on your phone, and the photo stays private.

My iPhone photo is HEIC — will the background remover accept it?

Safari usually handles HEIC photos directly, since iPhones save photos in that format by default. If a particular photo will not load, convert it to JPG first with our free HEIC to JPG tool and then remove the background from the JPG. The converted file works everywhere, including apps and marketplaces that do not accept HEIC.
