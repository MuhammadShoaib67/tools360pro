How to resize images for social media

Every platform crops your images to its own shape — and its automatic crop is rarely kind. Here is how to resize images for social media yourself, so the important part of the picture survives every feed.

Every platform has its own shape

Instagram posts are square, 1080 × 1080; stories and reels are tall, 1080 × 1920. Facebook posts are wide, 1200 × 630, and covers are wider still. X posts are 1600 × 900, LinkedIn posts 1200 × 627, YouTube thumbnails 1280 × 720, Pinterest pins tall at 1000 × 1500. One photo cannot fit all of those — each platform needs its own version.

Uploading the wrong shape does not produce an error; it produces a crop. The platform takes what fits and discards the rest, usually from the edges — which is where faces, text and logos tend to be. The result is the cropped head, the cut-off headline, the logo sliced in half. Resizing before uploading puts you in control of what survives.

Crop vs pad: the two ways to fit

When a photo and a preset disagree, there are only two ways to reconcile them. Cropping cuts the photo to the target shape — you lose the edges, but the result fills the frame. Padding keeps the whole photo and fills the remaining space, usually with white or black — nothing is lost, but the photo sits smaller inside the frame.

Crop when the subject fills the frame and the edges are expendable: a landscape, a crowd, a product shot with room to spare. Pad when the whole image matters: an infographic, a poster, a photo with text at the edges. The browser tool below pads with white by default, which suits feeds; for a dark aesthetic, choose black padding. Either way, the decision is yours — not the platform's.

The sizes that matter in 2026

The standard presets have been stable for years, and they are the ones in the tool: Instagram Post 1080 × 1080, Instagram Portrait 1080 × 1350, Story and Reel 1080 × 1920, Facebook Post 1200 × 630, Facebook Cover 820 × 312, X Post 1600 × 900, LinkedIn Post 1200 × 627, YouTube Thumbnail 1280 × 720, Pinterest Pin 1000 × 1500. These are the sizes the platforms publish and the sizes their feeds display.

Two cautions. First, platforms change: check the size when something looks off, because a redesign can shift a preset overnight. Second, these are display sizes, not maximums — uploading larger than the preset is fine, since the platform downscales; uploading smaller is not, since it upscales and softens. When in doubt, upload at the preset or slightly above.

How to resize images for social media

  1. In your browser — fastest, nothing to install. Use the Social Media Image Resizer: drop the image in, pick the platform preset, choose whether to crop or pad, and download at the exact size. Nothing is uploaded.
  2. In Canva, for designed posts. Create a design at the exact preset size — Canva offers the standard social sizes as templates — then place your photo and adjust it to fill the frame. Canva is free for this, and it suits posts where text and graphics join the photo.
  3. On your phone, with the built-in editor. Open the photo, tap Edit, then Crop, and choose the ratio that matches the platform: 1:1 for a post, 9:16 for a story, 16:9 for a wide post. The phone editor crops but cannot resize to exact pixels — for that, use the browser tool.
  4. In GIMP, for exact control. Open the image, choose Image, then Scale Image for the size, then Image, then Canvas Size for the padding. GIMP is free and gives exact pixel control, but it is slower than the browser tool for a single post.

What to do when a platform changes its sizes

Platforms do change their sizes — Instagram's portrait ratio shifted, X rebranded and adjusted its cards — and when it happens, old presets quietly go out of date. The symptom is a post that looks slightly cropped or slightly blurry when it used to look right. The fix is to check the platform's own help pages for the current size, and to keep the original photo so you can resize it again.

Keeping originals is the habit that saves you here. A photo resized to 1080 × 1080 cannot become a 1080 × 1350 portrait without losing quality — the pixels are gone. The original can become anything. Store originals, resize copies, and a platform change is a five-minute job instead of a lost photo.

Questions people ask

What size should an Instagram post be?

1080 × 1080 pixels (square) is the standard. Instagram also supports portrait posts at 1080 × 1350 and landscape at 1080 × 566 — the square preset is the safest default.

Is it better to crop or pad?

Crop when the edges are expendable and you want the frame filled. Pad when the whole image matters — infographics, posters, photos with text at the edges — so nothing is cut off.

Why does my uploaded photo look blurry?

Usually because it was smaller than the display size, so the platform upscaled it. Upload at the preset size or larger — downscaling keeps images sharp, upscaling softens them.
