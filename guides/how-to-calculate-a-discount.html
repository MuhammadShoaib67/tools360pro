How to Calculate a Discount

By Muhammad Shoaib · Published 17 November 2026 · Updated 17 November 2026

A discount is a percentage taken off a price, and calculating it takes two steps: find the saving, then subtract it from the price. This guide shows the formula, works through examples, and explains the two places where discount maths catches people out — stacked discounts and comparing different kinds of deal.

The discount formula

A discount is a percentage of the original price. The saving is the price multiplied by the discount percent and divided by 100, and the sale price is the original price minus that saving. On a 200 item at 25% off, the saving is 200 times 25 divided by 100, which is 50, so the final price is 150. The whole calculation is two multiplications and a subtraction.

The same maths works in reverse. If you know the original price and the sale price, the discount percent is the difference divided by the original price, times 100. A jacket marked down from 120 to 90 has a discount of 30 on 120, which is 25% off. Shops sometimes show only the two prices and leave you to work out the percentage — now you can.

Why discounts feel bigger than they are

Percentages hide the absolute amount. A 50% discount on a 20 item saves 10, while a 10% discount on a 500 item saves 50 — the smaller percentage is worth five times more money. That is why it is worth working out the saving in plain numbers next to the percentage: the percent tells you the deal, the amount tells you what it is worth.

The original price also sets expectations. A 200 item at 50% off feels like a bargain because the saving looks large, but the question is always the same: is 100 a good price for this item, regardless of what it used to cost? The discount is a comparison, not a value — and a bad deal at half price is still a bad deal.

Calculate a discount in four steps

  1. Find the original price. This is the price before the discount, on the tag or the listing. If the item is already reduced and you want the discount from the very first price, use that first price.
  2. Find the discount percent. This is on the sign or the listing — 25% off, 40% off, half price (50% off). If the listing shows only the two prices, work the percent out from the difference instead.
  3. Work out the saving. Multiply the price by the discount percent and divide by 100. Or open the Discount Calculator, enter the price and the percent, and read the saving and the final price at once.
  4. Subtract the saving from the price. The result is the sale price — what you actually pay. Check the share of the original price you still pay too: at 25% off, you pay 75% of the original price.

Stacked discounts do not add up

A 20% discount followed by a further 10% off is not 30% off in total, because the second discount applies to the already reduced price. Twenty percent off 100 leaves 80, and ten percent off 80 leaves 72 — a total saving of 28, not 30. The combined discount is 28%, and the gap grows with bigger percentages.

The order does not matter: 10% off then 20% off gives the same result, because 0.9 times 0.8 is the same as 0.8 times 0.9. What matters is that each discount applies to a smaller base than the one before it. If a shop offers an extra discount on top of a sale price, multiply the remaining fractions together — 0.8 times 0.9 is 0.72, so you pay 72% of the original.

Other ways to calculate a discount

For common percentages, mental maths is fast. Ten percent is the price with the decimal point moved one place: 10% of 240 is 24. Twenty-five percent is a quarter: 25% of 240 is 60. Fifty percent is half. For anything else, a phone calculator does the job in seconds — the formula is the same.

For many items, a spreadsheet is the better tool: one column of prices, one of discount percents, and a formula in the third column. But for a single price in a shop, the browser calculator is fastest — two numbers in, the answer out, with no app to open and no sign-up.

Questions people ask

What is the formula for a discount?

Multiply the price by the discount percent and divide by 100 to get the saving, then subtract the saving from the price. A 25% discount on 200 saves 50, so the sale price is 150.

How do I calculate 30% off?

Multiply the price by 0.30 to get the saving, then subtract it. A 30% discount on 90 saves 27, so you pay 63.

Is 20% off then 10% off the same as 30% off?

No. The second discount applies to the reduced price, so the total is 28% off, not 30%.

Do I pay tax on the original price or the sale price?

In most places, tax is charged on the discounted price, so a discount saves a little tax too. The exact rule varies by country, so check your local rules if it matters.
