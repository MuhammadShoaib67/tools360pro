How to Create a Username

A username is the first thing people see, and the name you pick tends to stick. This guide shows how to come up with username ideas from a name or a hobby, which styles fit which platform, and how to check whether a name is actually available before you commit to it.

Start with something personal

The best usernames start from something real: your name, a nickname, a hobby, or a word you like. A name built on something personal is easier to remember and harder to confuse with someone else's. Start with one word — your first name, your craft, your favourite thing — and build out from there.

Then add one twist. A second word that says what you do (alexdraws, lunacooks), a number that means something to you, or a separator that makes the name readable (alex_draws, luna.cooks). One twist is enough: a name with three additions starts to look like a password, and nobody will remember it.

What makes a username good

A good username is short, readable and hard to confuse. Two syllables beat five, one word beats three, and a name you can spell out loud without explaining it wins every time. Avoid strings of random digits — alex123 is forgettable and looks like a placeholder, while alexdraws says something about you.

Think about where the name will live. A gaming handle can be playful; a professional profile should be plain and searchable. If you plan to use the name in more than one place, pick something that works everywhere — some platforms ban dots, others ban underscores, and most have a length limit. A name built from letters alone travels best.

Create a username in four steps

  1. Pick your starting word. Your first name, a nickname, a hobby, or a word you like. One word is enough — the generator does the rest.
  2. Generate ideas. Open the Username Generator, enter your word, pick a style, and click Generate. You get 12 fresh ideas per run, and every run is different.
  3. Shortlist three or four. Say them out loud. Drop any that are hard to spell, hard to say, or easy to confuse with another name. Keep the ones that still sound good on the second hearing.
  4. Check availability on the platform. Try each shortlisted name on the sign-up page or search of the platform where you will use it. No browser tool can check this for you — availability lives inside each platform's own database.

Styles that fit each platform

Gaming handles reward personality: short, punchy names with numbers or wordplay work well, and a name that reads as a callsign (silentfox, novastrike) fits the culture. Social media favours readable names — your name plus what you do, with dots or underscores to separate the parts.

Professional platforms want plain names: first name plus last name, or first name plus initial, with no jokes and no numbers. Creative platforms sit in between — a name that says what you make, in a style that matches your work. The generator's style options cover the range: numbers for gaming, underscores and dots for readable names, short for tight character limits.

When your first choice is taken

Resist the urge to add a random year or a string of digits — that is how forgettable names are born. Change the word instead: add what you do, where you are from, or a word you like. Alexdraws beats alex1998, and it tells people something real about you.

If you plan to build an audience around the name, check the obvious platforms early, before you commit to one you cannot use elsewhere. A name you can use consistently across platforms is worth more than a clever name you can only use in one place.

Questions people ask

How do I check if a username is taken?

Try it on the platform itself — its sign-up page or search. No browser tool can check availability across platforms, because that information lives inside each platform's own database.

What makes a good username?

Short, readable, and hard to confuse with someone else's. A name you can spell out loud without explaining it, built from a word that means something to you, beats a long name with random digits.

Should my username be the same everywhere?

If you can manage it, yes — a consistent name is easier for people to find and remember. Check the main platforms early, before you commit to a name you can only use in one place.

Can I change my username later?

Most platforms let you change it, but the old name may be taken by someone else, and people who knew the old name may not find you. Pick carefully the first time if you can.
