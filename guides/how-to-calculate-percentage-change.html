How to Calculate Percentage Change

Percentage change measures how much a number grew or shrank, relative to where it started. This guide shows the formula, works through increases and decreases, and clears up the two confusions that catch people out: which value is the base, and the difference between percentage change and percentage points.

The percentage change formula

Percentage change is the difference between two numbers, expressed as a share of the starting number. The formula is the new value minus the old value, divided by the old value, times 100. From 50 to 75, the difference is 25, and 25 divided by 50 is 0.5 — a 50% increase. The same three steps work for any two numbers.

The sign is part of the answer. A positive result means growth: the new value is larger than the old one. A negative result means a fall. Zero means the values are the same. Always keep the sign — plus 12% and minus 12% are opposite results, and dropping it hides which one happened.

Why the base value matters

The old value is always the base, and that matters more than it looks. Going from 50 to 100 is a 100% increase, but going back from 100 to 50 is only a 50% decrease, because the base changed from 50 to 100. The same two numbers give two different percentages depending on which direction you travel — so always be clear about which value is old and which is new.

This shows up in real arguments. A salary that rises 10% one year and falls 10% the next does not end up where it started — the fall applies to the higher salary, so it ends up 1% lower. A shop that raises prices by 20% and then cuts them by 20% has not returned to the original price. The base moves every time, and the percentages move with it.

Calculate percentage change in four steps

  1. Identify the old value. This is the starting number — last month's sales, last year's salary, the price before the change. Everything is measured from here.
  2. Identify the new value. This is the number it changed to. Make sure both numbers are in the same units: both in dollars, both in kilograms, both in the same currency.
  3. Subtract, divide, multiply. Take the new value minus the old value, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100. Or open the Percentage Change Calculator and enter both numbers.
  4. Read the sign. A positive result is an increase, a negative result is a decrease. Check the plain difference too — it tells you the change in real units, not just as a percentage.

Percentage change vs percentage points

This is the most common percentage confusion, and it catches professionals too. If an interest rate rises from 10% to 12%, the rise is 2 percentage points — the simple difference between the two rates. But the percentage change is 20%, because 2 is 20% of the starting rate of 10. Both numbers are correct; they answer different questions.

Use percentage points when you compare two percentages directly, and percentage change when you want to say how much something grew or shrank relative to where it started. A headline that says a rate rose by 20% when it went from 10% to 12% is technically right but usually misleading — and a headline that says it rose by 2% is simply wrong.

When percentage change misleads

Percentage change is most misleading with small starting numbers. Going from 1 to 2 is a 100% increase, which sounds dramatic, but the absolute change is only 1. Going from 1,000 to 1,100 is a 10% increase — a smaller percentage, but a change of 100. Always look at the plain difference alongside the percentage, especially when the starting number is small.

There is one case where percentage change cannot be calculated at all: when the old value is zero. The formula divides by the old value, and dividing by zero has no answer. If the old value is zero, use the plain difference instead — and be suspicious of any tool that shows you a percentage for it.

Questions people ask

What is the formula for percentage change?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100. From 50 to 75 that is 25 divided by 50, times 100, which is 50%.

How do I calculate percentage increase?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. From 40 to 50, the increase is 10 divided by 40, which is 25%.

Can percentage change be more than 100%?

Yes, for increases. A value that triples has risen by 200%. Decreases cannot pass minus 100% for positive quantities.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

Percent change is relative to the starting value. Percentage points are the simple difference between two percentages: a rise from 10% to 12% is 2 percentage points.
