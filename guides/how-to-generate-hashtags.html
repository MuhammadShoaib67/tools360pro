How to Generate Hashtags

Hashtags help the right people find your post — when they are relevant. This guide shows how to generate hashtag ideas from any topic, how many to use on each platform, and which tags are worth using and which are not, with a free generator that builds a set in seconds.

What hashtags do now

Hashtags used to be the main way posts were discovered, and early advice was to pile on as many as possible. Platforms have moved on: posts are now ranked mostly on content and behaviour, and hashtags work as a discovery aid rather than a growth hack. A relevant tag helps the right people find a post; an irrelevant one does nothing, and a wall of generic tags can make a post look automated.

That changes how to think about them. Instead of collecting the maximum allowed, the job is to pick tags that genuinely describe the content — the topic, the format, the audience — and let the platform match the post to people who follow those tags. Quality beats quantity, and a handful of specific tags outperforms a long list of broad ones.

How hashtag ideas are built

A hashtag generator builds tags from your topic using templates: your topic itself as a tag, then combinations with words like life, tips, daily, community and goals, plus a few tags typical of the platform you picked. The result is a set of readable, relevant tags you can paste straight into a post.

The suggestions are template-based, not AI-generated, and they are not ranked by live popularity — no browser tool can see how many posts use a tag today. Treat the list as a starting point, then check the tags that matter on the platform itself: search the tag and look at what actually shows up.

Generate hashtags in four steps

  1. Enter your topic. Open the Hashtag Generator and type a word or a short phrase — coffee, home workout, digital art.
  2. Pick the platform. Each platform adds a few of its own typical tags to the set, and the right count differs by platform.
  3. Choose how many tags you want. Ten, twenty or thirty — then click Generate hashtags to build the set.
  4. Copy and trim. Click any hashtag to copy it on its own, or Copy all for the whole set. Delete any tag that does not actually describe your post before publishing.

How many hashtags per platform

Instagram allows up to 30 hashtags per post, and most guides suggest somewhere between 5 and 15 relevant ones — enough to cover the topic without looking like spam. TikTok posts usually carry 3 to 5. On X, one or two is the norm, and more than three tends to hurt readability. LinkedIn posts work best with 3 to 5 professional tags.

More is not always better. A wall of barely related tags signals low effort and can make a post look automated. A smaller set of tags that genuinely describe the content reaches the right people more reliably than 30 generic ones. Pick the count that fits the platform, then delete any tag that does not describe your post.

Tags that work vs tags that do not

The tags that work best are specific. A broad tag like photography is used on hundreds of millions of posts, so a new post disappears from it in seconds. A narrower tag like filmphotography or streetphotography has a smaller but far more interested audience, and a new post stays visible in it much longer. Mix one or two broad tags with several specific ones.

Avoid tags that promise what the post does not deliver — tagging a photo with followforfollow or likeforlike attracts engagement that never converts. And skip banned or broken tags: some tags are restricted by platforms, and a post that uses one can be hidden from search entirely. When in doubt, search the tag on the platform and look at what actually shows up.

Questions people ask

Are generated hashtags ranked by popularity?

No. The suggestions are built from templates in your browser and are not ranked by live popularity or post counts. Check any hashtag on the platform itself to see how widely it is used.

How many hashtags should I use on Instagram?

Instagram allows up to 30 per post, but most guides suggest 5 to 15 relevant ones. A smaller set of specific tags usually works better than 30 generic ones.

Do hashtags still matter?

Yes, but less than they used to. Platforms now rank posts mostly on content and behaviour, and hashtags work as a discovery aid rather than a growth hack. Relevant tags help the right people find a post.

Can I use the same hashtags on every platform?

You can, but each platform has its own norms — TikTok favours 3 to 5 tags, X usually one or two, LinkedIn 3 to 5 professional ones. Generate a set per platform instead of copying one everywhere.
