What this audio tool covers

Trimming is the most common audio job there is: a voice note with silence at the start, a recording with noise trailing off at the end, a song you only need the chorus of, a podcast clip worth sharing on its own. The Audio Trimmer handles that one job properly — it decodes your file in the browser, shows you the waveform so you can see the quiet and loud parts, lets you pick exact start and end points, previews the selection before you commit, and downloads the trimmed clip as a WAV.

It works with the formats browsers can play: MP3, WAV, OGG, M4A/AAC and FLAC in most browsers. The download is a WAV file because browsers cannot encode MP3 — WAV is uncompressed, so the part you keep sounds exactly as it did in the original, and every audio app, editor and player opens it. A tiny 10-millisecond fade at each cut point keeps the clip from clicking at the start and end.

Frequently asked questions

What audio formats can I trim?

Any format your browser can play and decode: MP3, WAV, OGG, M4A/AAC and FLAC in most browsers. If a file will not load, the browser cannot decode that format.

Why is the trimmed file a WAV?

Browsers can decode many audio formats but cannot encode MP3 — there is no MP3 encoder built into the browser. The trimmer exports uncompressed WAV instead, which every audio app, editor and player opens.

Does trimming reduce the quality?

No. Trimming keeps the original decoded samples for the part you keep — nothing is re-compressed. A tiny 10-millisecond fade at each cut point keeps the start and end from clicking.

Is my audio uploaded anywhere?

No. Every tool on this page runs in your browser; your file is decoded, trimmed and encoded on your own device, and nothing is uploaded or stored.

Do the audio tools work on my phone?

Yes — the audio trimmer works in a mobile browser, with no app to install. Very long files need more memory, so a short clip trims fastest on a phone.
