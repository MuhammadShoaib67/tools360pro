How to use the Time Zone Converter

  1. Choose the time zone your starting time is in from the “From” dropdown.
  2. Pick the date and time you want to convert — it is read as wall-clock time in that zone.
  3. Choose the target zone from the “To” dropdown and read the converted time in the result panel.
  4. Use Swap to reverse the zones, or “Use current time” to reset the input to now.

This task often leads to two others: Timestamp Converter converts Unix timestamps to readable dates and back, and Date Calculator counts the days between two dates, or adds days to a date.

Why the gap between two cities keeps changing

Time zones are not fixed distances. Many regions move their clocks forward in spring and back in autumn for daylight saving time, so the gap between two cities can change by an hour twice a year — and the change happens on different dates in different places. The southern hemisphere runs on the opposite cycle, which means the difference between, say, London and Sydney shifts several times a year. A converter that only knows today's offset will quietly give the wrong answer for a meeting booked three months away.

This converter avoids that trap by calculating the offset for the exact date you enter, using the same IANA time zone database your operating system uses. Pick any date and time, and the result shows the offset in force on that day for both zones, plus how far ahead or behind the second city is. Some zones also sit on half-hour offsets — India, for example, is UTC+05:30 — so the difference between two cities is not always a whole number of hours.

Scheduling across the date line

Converting a time can also change the day. A 9 a.m. meeting in New York is already evening in Tokyo, and a late call from Sydney can land on the previous morning in Los Angeles. The result panel shows the full weekday and date for the converted time, so a shift across midnight is visible at a glance rather than discovered when someone misses the call. Always read the date as well as the time — the two travel together.

For a quick check, the world clock at the bottom shows live times for eight major cities — New York, London, Dubai, Karachi, Singapore, Tokyo, Sydney and UTC — updating every second, with the current date and UTC offset under each one. Use the Swap button to reverse the direction and check a time both ways, which catches mistakes when you are converting in your head. And when the meeting is set, send the invite with the time zone named, not just the hour: “3 p.m. Karachi time” leaves no room for doubt.

Frequently asked questions

How are daylight saving changes handled?

The converter uses your browser’s built-in time zone database (the same IANA data used by operating systems), so daylight saving time is applied automatically for the exact date you enter. A city can be an hour further ahead or behind in summer than in winter.

What does a UTC offset mean?

A UTC offset tells you how far a zone is from Coordinated Universal Time. UTC+05:00 means the local time is five hours ahead of UTC, while UTC−04:00 means it is four hours behind.

Why can the same city have different offsets on different dates?

Many regions move their clocks forward in spring and back in autumn for daylight saving time. The offset shown here is calculated for the date and time you entered, not just for today.

Is any of my data uploaded?

No. All conversions and the world clock run locally in your browser using the Intl API. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

What is “Local time” in the dropdown?

It is the time zone your device is currently set to, detected automatically by your browser. Its IANA name is used for the conversion, so it stays correct even if you travel.
