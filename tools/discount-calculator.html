How a discount is calculated

A discount is a percentage of the original price. The saving is the price multiplied by the discount percent and divided by 100, and the final price is the original price minus that saving. On a 200 item at 25% off, the saving is 200 times 25 divided by 100, which is 50, so the final price is 150.

The same maths works in reverse. If you know the original price and the sale price, the discount percent is the difference divided by the original price, times 100. A jacket marked down from 120 to 90 has a discount of 30 on 120, which is 25% off. This calculator does the first direction — price and percent in, final price and saving out.

Why the saving feels different from the price

Percentages hide the absolute amount. A 50% discount on a 20 item saves 10, while a 10% discount on a 500 item saves 50 — the smaller percentage is worth five times more money. That is why the calculator shows the saving in plain numbers next to the percentage: the percent tells you the deal, the amount tells you what it is worth.

Stacked discounts are another place where percentages mislead. A 20% discount followed by a further 10% off is not 30% off in total, because the second discount applies to the already reduced price. Twenty percent off 100 leaves 80, and ten percent off 80 leaves 72 — a total saving of 28, not 30. Shops know this; now you can check it.

Discounts, tax and the final amount

This calculator shows the price before tax. In most places, sales tax or VAT is charged on the discounted price rather than the original, so a discount saves a little tax too — but the exact rule varies by country and by the type of product, so the number at the till may differ slightly from the number here.

One more detail is rounding. Shops round the final price to the nearest cent or the nearest whole unit of currency, and this calculator shows two decimal places. On a single item the difference is a few cents at most; it only matters if you are pricing hundreds of items, in which case round each line yourself after calculating.

Comparing discounts across shops is the other everyday use. Two shops rarely offer the same deal in the same form: one offers 20% off, the other offers a fixed 15 off, and the better deal depends on the price. On a 100 item, 20% off saves 20 and beats the fixed 15; on a 60 item, 20% off saves only 12, so the fixed 15 wins. Run both numbers through the calculator before you buy — it takes ten seconds and settles the question.

Final price, saving and share: three numbers from one discount

A sale price calculator answers the question shoppers actually ask: what do I pay? Enter the original price and the percent off, and the final price appears at once, with the saving beside it — the amount that stays in your pocket. The share of the original price is the third number: at 25% off, you pay 75% of the original, which is a useful check when a discount sounds bigger than it is.

This calculator works in one direction only — price and percent in, final price and saving out — and it keeps the maths honest in one more way: tax is not included, so the final price is the price before tax, exactly as a shop label shows it. Use it for a single discount at a time; a second discount on an already-discounted price is a different calculation, and the guide on this site explains how stacked discounts work.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a 20% discount?

Multiply the price by 0.20 to get the saving, then subtract it from the price. A 20% discount on 80 saves 16, so you pay 64.

What is the difference between a discount and a sale price?

The discount is the amount taken off; the sale price is what you actually pay. On a 200 item at 25% off, the discount is 50 and the sale price is 150.

Do two discounts add up?

No. A 20% discount followed by 10% off is 28% off in total, not 30%, because the second discount applies to the already reduced price.

Which currency does this calculator use?

Any. It works with plain numbers, so enter the price in dollars, pounds, rupees or any other currency and the results are in the same currency.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

How much do I save with a 20% discount?

20% of the original price: on a 100-dollar item, that is 20 saved and 80 to pay. Enter the original price and 20 as the discount percent here and the calculator shows the final price and the saving at once.

Does the calculator include tax?

No — tax is not included. The final price is the price before tax, the way a shop label shows it. Add your local tax rate to the final price if you need the amount you will actually pay at the till.

Can I use any currency?

Yes. The calculator works with plain numbers, so it works in dollars, pounds, rupees or any other currency — enter the price in your currency and the results are in the same currency.
