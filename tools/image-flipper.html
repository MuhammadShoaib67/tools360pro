Why front-camera photos look mirrored

Front cameras show you a mirror preview while you take the picture, so the framing feels natural — and some phones save the photo exactly as you saw it, mirrored. The result looks wrong the moment you notice it: text on a T-shirt reads backwards, a logo faces the wrong way, and a familiar face looks subtly strange. A horizontal flip puts everything back the way the world actually sees it.

Whether your phone mirrors selfies depends on the phone and its settings. iPhones have a “Mirror Front Camera” option; many Android phones save the un-mirrored version by default. Either way, if a selfie looks backwards, one flip fixes it — no editing app needed.

Flipping for design and print

Flipping is also a design tool. A photo of a person looking to the left can be flipped so they face into the page instead of off the edge — a small change that makes a layout feel balanced. The same trick works for product photos on a listing, where the subject should face the text or the buy button rather than away from it.

Iron-on transfers are the classic case: text printed for a T-shirt transfer must be mirrored, or it reads backwards on the shirt. Flipping the image before printing is the whole job. Just remember that a flip also mirrors any text in the picture, so check the preview before you print.

Flipping also helps with screenshots and diagrams. A screenshot of an app in a right-to-left language reads more naturally when the layout is mirrored, and a diagram drawn for a left-hand page can be flipped for the right-hand page so the labels sit on the correct side. The rule of thumb is simple: if the picture contains text, check that the text still reads correctly after the flip — mirrored text is the one side effect that always gives a flip away.

Flipping vs rotating

Flipping and rotating are different operations, and they are not interchangeable. A horizontal flip mirrors the picture left-to-right; a 180° rotation turns it upside down. Flipping both horizontally and vertically gives the same result as a 180° rotation — which is why this tool offers “Both” as a single choice.

Like rotation, flipping only rearranges existing pixels, so nothing is lost in the flip itself. Any quality change comes from the save afterwards: JPG re-compresses the image, while PNG keeps every pixel. Your original file is never changed — the download is always a new file.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Flipping runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device.

Why does my selfie look backwards?

Front cameras show a mirror preview, and some phones save the photo mirrored too. A horizontal flip restores the photo to the way the world sees you — text reads correctly again.

What is the difference between flip and rotate?

Flipping mirrors the picture: horizontal flip swaps left and right, vertical flip swaps top and bottom. Rotating turns the picture around its centre. Flipping both ways at once equals a 180° rotation.

Will flipping change my image quality?

The flip itself only rearranges pixels, so nothing is lost. Quality only changes if you save as JPG, which re-compresses the image. Choose PNG if you want a pixel-perfect result.

Can I flip just part of an image?

No — this tool flips the whole image. To flip one element, crop it out first with the Crop Image tool, flip the cropped piece, and place it back in an editor.

What formats can I download?

Keep the original format, or choose JPEG, WebP or PNG. PNG keeps transparency and loses nothing; JPG makes the smallest files for photos.

What is the difference between flipping horizontally and vertically?

A horizontal flip mirrors the image left to right, like looking in a mirror — the fix for a mirrored selfie. A vertical flip mirrors it top to bottom, turning the image upside down. Flip both and the image is rotated 180 degrees.

Can I flip an image for an iron-on transfer?

Yes — flip the image horizontally before printing, so the transfer reads correctly when it is pressed onto fabric. Text and logos must be reversed in the print, and this tool does that in one click.

Can I flip an image and rotate it too?

Not in one step — this tool flips only, and rotation lives in the separate Image Rotator tool. Flip the image here, download it, then rotate the downloaded file if it still needs turning.
