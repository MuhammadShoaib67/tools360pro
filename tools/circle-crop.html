Where circle photos are used

Almost every profile picture on the internet is a circle: messaging apps, social profiles, comment sections, team pages and email avatars all display photos inside a round frame. Uploading a square photo works — the app crops it for you — but the app chooses the crop, and it often cuts off the top of a head or an important detail at the edge.

Cropping the circle yourself means you choose what is inside it. Centre the face, leave a little room around the head, and the result looks deliberate instead of accidental. The same applies to logos and icons shown in round frames on websites and app stores.

Getting the crop right

The circle is cut from the centre of your image, and its size is a percentage of the shorter side. At 100% the circle uses the full width of a portrait photo or the full height of a landscape one. Shrink it to zoom in — useful when the face is small in the frame and you want it to fill the circle.

Leave breathing room. A face that touches the edge of the circle looks cramped once it is displayed small, and a little space around the head reads as confidence rather than a mistake. If the subject is not centred in the original photo, crop the image first with the Crop Image tool so the subject sits in the middle, then circle-crop the result.

PNG transparency, explained

A circle has corners, and the corners have to be something: transparent, in this case. PNG is the format that stores transparency, which is why circle crops are always saved as PNG here — a JPG would fill the corners with white, and the “circle” would show as a white square on any coloured background.

Some apps show a transparent PNG with a checkerboard pattern in their preview; that is just how transparency is displayed, and the file itself is correct. When the image is placed on a page or in a profile, the page's own background shows through the corners, and the photo reads as a clean circle.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Cropping runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device.

Why is the download always PNG?

Only PNG (and WebP) can store transparency. A JPG would fill the circle's corners with white. PNG keeps the corners transparent, so the photo reads as a clean circle on any background.

Why is the circle cut from the centre?

The circle is centred on your image. If your subject is off-centre, crop the image first with the Crop Image tool so the subject sits in the middle, then circle-crop the result.

What size should a profile picture be?

Most platforms display profile pictures at 200–500 pixels, but accept much larger uploads. A circle of 500–1000 pixels is a safe choice: sharp on every screen without a heavy file.

My preview shows a checkerboard — is that in the file?

No. The checkerboard is how viewers display transparency; it is not part of the image. On a real page, the background shows through the corners instead.

Can I add a coloured ring around the circle?

Not in this tool — it crops only. For a ring or border, place the PNG over a coloured circle in any design app, or use a slide tool where a shape sits behind the image.
