The percentage change formula

Percentage change is the difference between two numbers, expressed as a share of the starting number. The formula is the new value minus the old value, divided by the old value, times 100. From 50 to 75, the difference is 25, and 25 divided by 50 is 0.5 — a 50% increase.

The old value is always the base, and that matters more than it looks. Going from 50 to 100 is a 100% increase, but going back from 100 to 50 is only a 50% decrease, because the base changed from 50 to 100. The same two numbers give two different percentages depending on which direction you travel — so always be clear about which value is old and which is new.

Increase, decrease and the sign

A positive result means growth: the new value is larger than the old one. A negative result means a fall. Zero means the values are the same. The sign is part of the answer, which is why this calculator shows it explicitly — plus 12% and minus 12% are opposite results, and dropping the sign would hide which one happened.

Percentage increases have no upper limit: a value can rise by 200%, 500% or more. Decreases are different — for positive quantities, a fall cannot pass minus 100%, because that would mean dropping below zero. A price can fall by 90%, but a fall of 110% would mean the seller pays you, which is a different kind of deal entirely.

Percentage change turns up everywhere once you look for it. A salary rising from 40,000 to 44,000 is a 10% increase; a share price falling from 25 to 20 is a 20% decrease; a test score improving from 60 to 75 is a 25% increase. In each case the same formula applies, and the same care is needed about which value is old and which is new — the base decides the answer.

Percentage change vs percentage points

This is the most common percentage confusion, and it catches professionals too. If an interest rate rises from 10% to 12%, the rise is 2 percentage points — the simple difference between the two rates. But the percentage change is 20%, because 2 is 20% of the starting rate of 10. Both numbers are correct; they answer different questions.

Use percentage points when you compare two percentages directly, and percentage change when you want to say how much something grew or shrank relative to where it started. A headline that says a rate rose by 20% when it went from 10% to 12% is technically right but usually misleading — and a headline that says it rose by 2% is simply wrong.

Increase or decrease between two numbers: the change, the sign, the reading

A percent increase calculator and a percent decrease calculator are the same tool wearing two hats: enter the old value and the new value, and the sign tells you which one it is — a positive result is an increase, a negative one is a decrease. The percentage change between two numbers is measured against the old value, so going from 50 to 75 is a 50% increase, while going from 75 to 50 is a 33.3% decrease: the same 25-point move, measured against different starting points.

Two things worth knowing before you trust the number. A change from zero has no percentage at all — there is no starting value to measure against — so the tool refuses rather than inventing a figure. And percentage change is not the same as percentage points: a rise from 20% to 25% is a 5 percentage-point move, but a 25% change, because it is measured against the starting 20. The page explains the difference, so the number you quote is the one you mean.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for percentage change?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100. From 50 to 75 that is 25 divided by 50, times 100, which is 50%.

Can percentage change be negative?

Yes. A negative result means the new value is smaller than the old one — a decrease. From 80 to 60 is a change of minus 25%.

Why can I not calculate a change from zero?

The formula divides by the old value, and dividing by zero has no answer. If the old value is zero, use the plain difference instead — this tool says so rather than showing a misleading number.

What is the difference between percent change and percentage points?

Percent change is relative to the starting value; percentage points are the simple difference between two percentages. A rise from 10% to 12% is 2 percentage points, but a 20% change.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

How do I find the percent increase from 50 to 75?

Enter 50 as the old value and 75 as the new value. The increase is 25, measured against the starting 50, so the result is a 50% increase — the calculator shows the sign, the exact difference and a plain-English reading too.

Why is a change from zero not a percentage?

Because a percentage change is measured against the old value, and zero gives nothing to measure against — any number divided by zero is undefined. The tool says so plainly instead of inventing a figure.

What is the difference between percentage change and percentage points?

Percentage points are the simple gap between two percentages: 20% to 25% is 5 percentage points. Percentage change measures that gap against the starting value, so the same move is a 25% change. They answer different questions, so check which one you need before quoting a number.
