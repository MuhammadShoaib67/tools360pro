How to use the Pomodoro Timer

  1. Add the task you are working on and how many pomodoros you think it needs.
  2. Pick a preset — Classic 25/5 is the standard — or set your own lengths with Custom.
  3. Press Start and work on nothing but that task until the alarm sounds, then take the break.
  4. After four focus sessions you get a long break. The timer keeps counting even if you reload the page.

Why 25 minutes works

Twenty-five minutes is short enough that starting feels easy and long enough to do real work. That is the whole trick of the Pomodoro Technique: instead of facing "study for four hours", you face one 25-minute session, and the break at the end of it is a promise, not a reward you have to earn by finishing. Students who struggle to start usually struggle with the size of the task, not the task itself — and a small, timed box makes the start small too.

The timer also protects your attention. During a focus session, anything that is not the task waits: the message, the video, the other chapter. If something important pops into your head, write it down and keep going — the break is only minutes away. Over a day, four or five honest pomodoros beat eight distracted hours, because the work in them is actually done rather than half-done while scrolling.

Making the breaks count

A break is not a smaller work session. The five minutes between pomodoros are for standing up, stretching, getting water, looking out of a window — anything that is not a screen. Checking your phone for five minutes is not a break; it is a different kind of focus, and you return to the next session just as tired. The long break after four sessions is the real reset: take a short walk, eat something, and let your brain file away what you just studied.

If 25 minutes feels too short for deep work, try the Deep work preset (50 minutes of focus, 10 off) or Ultradian (90/20), which matches the brain's natural 90-minute rest-activity cycle. If you are just getting started or the task is dull, Sprint (15/3) lowers the bar until the habit sticks. The right length is the one you will actually repeat — a timer you abandon after one session helps nobody, while a short session you repeat six times a day changes a semester.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Pomodoro Technique?

A time-management method developed in the late 1980s by Francesco Cirillo, named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer he used. You work in focused 25-minute sessions called pomodoros, take a 5-minute break after each one, and a longer break after four.

Why is there a long break after four sessions?

Four pomodoros is about two hours of focused work, and attention drops after that. The 15-minute long break lets your brain rest properly, so the next set of sessions starts fresh instead of tired.

Does the timer survive a page reload?

Yes. The timer counts from the real clock, and its state is saved in your browser. If you reload the page or close the tab, reopening it picks the timer up where the clock says it should be — a finished phase is marked complete.

Do I need to install anything or sign up?

No. The timer runs entirely in your browser on any phone or computer, with no app and no account. Your tasks and today's count are saved only in this browser.

Can I change the session lengths?

Yes. Pick a preset — Classic 25/5, Deep work 50/10, 52/17, Sprint 15/3 or Ultradian 90/20 — or choose Custom and set your own focus, short-break and long-break lengths from 1 to 180 minutes each.

Can I track tasks with the Pomodoro Timer?

Yes — add a task and how many pomodoros it should take, then work through them one session at a time. The task list sits beside the timer, so you always know what the current session is for.

How many pomodoros have I done today?

Today's count is saved in your browser and shown beside the timer, so you can see at a glance how many focus sessions you have finished today. The count resets each day.

Does the timer work on my phone?

Yes — it works on mobile as well as desktop, with no app to install and no sign-up. Open the page in your phone's browser and the timer runs the same way.
