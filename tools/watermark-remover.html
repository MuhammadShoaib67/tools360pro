What is a watermark remover?

A watermark remover is a tool that erases a watermark — text, a logo, a date stamp or a faint overlay — from a photo and rebuilds the background that was underneath it. This one works with AI inpainting: you paint over the watermark with a brush, the AI studies the pixels around it, and it fills the painted area with a background that matches. The photo never leaves your device — the whole job runs in your browser, with no upload and no sign-up.

It works best on the watermarks people most often want gone: a date stamp in the corner of an old camera photo, a logo on a screenshot you own, a text overlay on an export from a free app. Small and medium watermarks on simple backgrounds — sky, a plain wall, a smooth surface — come out cleanest. A large watermark stretched over busy detail, like a face or a crowd, is the hardest case, and a second pass over the same spot usually improves it.

How to remove a watermark from a photo

  1. Choose a photo. Drop a JPG, PNG or WebP image onto the box above, or click to browse. The photo stays on your device — nothing is uploaded.
  2. Paint over the watermark. Use the brush to cover the whole watermark, painting a little beyond its edges. A smaller brush suits thin text; a larger one covers logos faster.
  3. Run the AI. Press Remove watermark. The first time, the AI model (about 28 MB) downloads and is cached in your browser, so the first run takes a little longer — later runs are faster.
  4. Download the clean photo. Check the result, run it again on any spot that needs a second pass, then download it as a PNG or JPG.

When watermark removal works best

The AI rebuilds the painted area from the pixels around it, so the surroundings decide the result. A watermark on a plain sky, a smooth wall or a blurred background has plenty of clean pixels to copy from, and the repair is usually invisible. A watermark over a face, patterned fabric or fine text has less to work with, so the result is softer there — good enough for a share, less so for a close look.

Size matters too. A small date stamp or a short line of text is a quick job. A large watermark covering a big part of the photo is harder, because more of the image has to be invented — work on it in sections, one pass at a time, and check each pass before moving on. If a pass leaves a smudge, paint over just that spot and run again; repeat passes are the normal way to a clean result.

This tool works on photos only: it cannot remove watermarks from videos, and it does not find the watermark by itself — you paint over it with the brush. It handles one photo at a time, and it cannot remove invisible watermarks or marks embedded in a file's metadata. For the watermark itself, that is the honest limit: if you can see it and paint over it, the tool can try; if you cannot see it, it cannot.

One more limit is yours to respect: only remove watermarks from images you own or have permission to edit. Removing someone else's watermark may infringe copyright.

Painting over a watermark: brush tips

Paint a little beyond the watermark's edges — a border of a few pixels gives the AI a clean seam to blend into, so the repair does not leave a visible outline. For thin text, a small brush and a steady stroke work best; for a solid logo, a larger brush and a quick fill is faster. If you paint too much, Undo takes back the last stroke, and Clear paint wipes the mask clean so you can start over.

Semi-transparent watermarks need a full coat: even a faint ghost of the mark is still the mark, so cover every letter and edge. Date stamps in a corner are the easiest job of all — one rectangle of paint over the corner, one run, done. And if the first run leaves a trace, do not repaint the whole area: paint just the leftover trace and run again.

Frequently asked questions

How does the watermark remover work?

You paint over the watermark with a brush. An AI inpainting model (MI-GAN) studies the pixels around the mark and rebuilds the background underneath it, then blends the repair into your original photo at full resolution.

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No. The AI model is served with this website and runs entirely in your browser, on your own device. Your photo is never uploaded anywhere.

Why does the first removal take longer?

On first use the AI model (about 28 MB) and its engine download to your device. Your browser caches them afterwards, so the next removals start much faster.

What kinds of watermarks can it remove?

Visible watermarks you can paint over: text, logos, date stamps, corner marks and faint overlays. Small and medium watermarks on simple backgrounds come out cleanest; a large watermark over busy detail is the hardest case. It cannot remove invisible watermarks or marks embedded in a file's metadata.

Is it legal to remove a watermark?

Only remove watermarks from images you own or have permission to edit. Removing someone else's watermark may infringe copyright — a watermark is often there precisely to show who owns the image. When in doubt, ask the owner first.

Can I remove a watermark from a video?

No. This tool works on photos (JPG, PNG and WebP) only. Removing a watermark from a video needs a different kind of tool.

Does it find the watermark automatically?

No. There is no auto-detection: you paint over the watermark with the brush, and the AI repairs exactly the area you painted. That also means you stay in control of what gets changed.

Can I remove several watermarks at once?

On one photo, yes — paint over every watermark on the photo before pressing Remove watermark, and they are all repaired in one run. There is no batch mode: photos are processed one at a time.

Does the photo lose quality?

Only the painted area is rebuilt by AI. Everything else stays exactly as it was, at the photo's original resolution. Downloading as PNG keeps every pixel; JPG is smaller but slightly lossy, as with any JPG.
