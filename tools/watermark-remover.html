What is a watermark remover?

A watermark remover is a tool that erases a watermark — text, a logo, a date stamp or a faint overlay — from a photo and rebuilds the background that was underneath it. This one works with AI inpainting: press Auto-detect and remove and the tool finds the watermarks on its own, however many there are; the AI then studies the pixels around each mark and fills the area with a background that matches. The photo never leaves your device — the whole job runs in your browser. Use it to remove a watermark from a photo online free: no upload, no sign-up.

It works best on the watermarks people most often want gone: a date stamp in the corner of an old camera photo, a logo on a screenshot you own, a text overlay on an export from a free app. Small and medium watermarks on simple backgrounds — sky, a plain wall, a smooth surface — come out cleanest, and those are also the ones auto-detection finds most reliably. A large watermark stretched over busy detail, like a face or a crowd, is the hardest case, and a second pass over the same spot usually improves it. If auto-detection misses a mark, paint over it with the brush — the manual brush is always there as the fallback.

When watermark removal works best

The AI rebuilds the watermark area from the pixels around it, so the surroundings decide the result. A watermark on a plain sky, a smooth wall or a blurred background has plenty of clean pixels to copy from, and the repair is usually invisible. A watermark over a face, patterned fabric or fine text has less to work with, so the result is softer there — good enough for a share, less so for a close look.

Size matters too. A small date stamp or a short line of text is a quick job. A large watermark covering a big part of the photo is harder, because more of the image has to be invented — work on it in sections, one pass at a time, and check each pass before moving on. If a pass leaves a smudge, paint over just that spot and run again; repeat passes are the normal way to a clean result.

This tool works on photos only: it cannot remove watermarks from videos, and it handles one photo at a time. It cannot remove invisible watermarks or marks embedded in a file's metadata. Auto-detection finds most visible watermarks, but a faint mark over very busy detail can still be missed — that is what the brush is for. For the watermark itself, that is the honest limit: if you can see it, the tool can try, automatically or with the brush; if you cannot see it, it cannot.

One more limit is yours to respect: only remove watermarks from images you own or have permission to edit. Removing someone else's watermark may infringe copyright.

Painting over a watermark: brush tips

Auto-detection finds most visible watermarks, but if one is missed — a faint mark over busy detail, for example — the brush is the fallback. Paint a little beyond the watermark's edges — a border of a few pixels gives the AI a clean seam to blend into, so the repair does not leave a visible outline. For thin text, a small brush and a steady stroke work best; for a solid logo, a larger brush and a quick fill is faster. If you paint too much, Undo takes back the last stroke, and Clear marks wipes the paint and the auto-detected regions clean so you can start over.

Semi-transparent watermarks need a full coat: even a faint ghost of the mark is still the mark, so cover every letter and edge. Date stamps in a corner are the easiest job of all — one rectangle of paint over the corner, one run, done. And if the first run leaves a trace, do not repaint the whole area: paint just the leftover trace and run again.

Auto-detect: how the tool finds watermarks on its own

A text-detection model spots the marks, then the MI-GAN inpainting model rebuilds the background behind each one, studying the pixels around the mark so the fill matches. Only the watermark area is rebuilt: the rest of the photo stays exactly as it was, at its original resolution, and you download the result as PNG or JPG.

The marks it finds most reliably are the ones people most often want gone: a date stamp in the corner of an old camera photo, a text overlay on an export from a free app, a faint stamp across a scan. A solid logo may only partly disappear in one run, and diagonal or tiled text on a busy background can be missed: paint over whatever is left with the brush and press Remove again. Frequently asked questions

How does the watermark remover work?

Press Auto-detect and remove and the tool finds the watermark itself — however many there are — then an AI inpainting model (MI-GAN) studies the pixels around each mark and rebuilds the background underneath it, blending the repair into your original photo at full resolution. Anything it misses, you paint over with the brush.

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No. The AI model is served with this website and runs entirely in your browser, on your own device. Your photo is never uploaded anywhere.

Why does the first removal take longer?

On first use the AI models (about 28 MB for removal plus about 5 MB for text detection) and their engine download to your device. Your browser caches them afterwards, so the next removals start much faster.

What kinds of watermarks can it remove?

Visible watermarks the auto-detection can find, or that you can paint over: text, logos, date stamps, corner marks and faint overlays. Small and medium watermarks on simple backgrounds come out cleanest; a large watermark over busy detail is the hardest case. It cannot remove invisible watermarks or marks embedded in a file's metadata.

Is it legal to remove a watermark?

Only remove watermarks from images you own or have permission to edit. Removing someone else's watermark may infringe copyright — a watermark is often there precisely to show who owns the image. When in doubt, ask the owner first.

Can I remove a watermark from a video?

No. This tool works on photos (JPG, PNG and WebP) only. Removing a watermark from a video needs a different kind of tool.

Does it find the watermark automatically?

Yes. Press Auto-detect and remove and the tool finds the watermarks on its own — all of them, in one run — and highlights what it found before removing them. A watermark over very busy detail can still be missed; paint over anything it misses with the brush and run it again.

On one photo, yes — auto-detection finds all the watermarks on the photo and removes them in a single run, and you can also paint over every watermark before pressing Remove watermark. There is no batch mode: photos are processed one at a time.

Does the photo lose quality?

Only the watermark area is rebuilt by AI. Everything else stays exactly as it was, at the photo's original resolution. Downloading as PNG keeps every pixel; JPG is smaller but slightly lossy, as with any JPG.

Can it remove a date stamp from an old photo?

Yes — a date stamp in the corner of an old camera photo is one of the marks this tool handles. Try Auto-detect and remove first; the detector looks for text-like marks, and the inpainting rebuilds the corner behind the stamp. If the stamp is missed, paint over it with the brush and press Remove.

Does it remove text watermarks automatically?

Yes. Auto-detect scans the photo for text watermarks and faint overlays on its own, however many there are, and removes them in one run — no need to mark each one by hand. If a mark is missed, the manual brush is the fallback: paint over it and press Remove.

Will the rest of my photo change?

No. Only the watermark area is rebuilt by the inpainting model; the rest of the photo stays exactly as it was, at its original resolution. You can download the result as PNG or JPG.
