Why are iPhone photos HEIC — and when should you convert them?

Apple made HEIC the default iPhone photo format because it stores the same picture in about half the space of a JPG, which saves storage on your phone. The trouble starts when the photo leaves the Apple world: Windows PCs, many application forms, older Android phones and plenty of websites either refuse HEIC files or show them as blank. Converting to JPG solves that instantly, because JPG opens on every device and every website built in the last thirty years.

Choose JPG when the photo is going to a form, an email, a marketplace listing or a Windows computer — it is the safe, universal option. Choose PNG when you plan to edit the picture further and want to avoid any extra compression. Either way, your original HEIC files are never changed or uploaded; the conversion runs on your own device, so even personal family photos stay completely private.

Frequently asked questions

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. The HEIC decoding happens entirely in your browser using a WebAssembly library. Your photos never leave your device, which is why this tool is safe for private pictures.

Why are my iPhone photos saved as HEIC?

Since iOS 11, iPhones save photos in the HEIC format because it keeps the same quality in roughly half the file size of JPG. The downside is that Windows, many websites and older apps cannot open HEIC files.

Will converting to JPG reduce quality?

At the default 92% quality setting the difference is not visible in normal viewing. JPG files will usually be a little larger than the HEIC originals, because HEIC compresses more efficiently.

Can I convert many HEIC files at once?

Yes. Add as many HEIC or HEIF files as you like, convert them together, and download the results one by one or all together in a single ZIP file.

What is the difference between HEIC and HEIF?

HEIF is the container format and HEIC is the version Apple uses for iPhone photos. In practice the two names mean the same thing, and this tool converts both.

One of my files failed to convert. Why?

A few HEIC variants, such as 10-bit or HDR files from some devices, cannot be decoded by the browser library. The file may also be damaged. Your other files still convert normally.
