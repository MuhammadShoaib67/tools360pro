How to use the Final Grade Calculator

  1. Pick a mode: Percentages if your grade is already a percent, Points if your course runs on points, or Weighted categories if your grade is built from homework, quizzes and a midterm.
  2. Enter your current grade, the grade you want, and how much the final exam is worth.
  3. The score you need appears live — along with what you need for every letter grade.

The formula, in plain English

Your final grade is a weighted average: the work you have already done counts for part of it, and the final exam counts for the rest. If your current grade is 82% and the final is worth 30%, then 70% of your grade is already locked in at 82 — the final can only move the remaining 30%. The calculator reverses that maths: needed score = (target − current × (1 − weight)) ÷ weight. With a 90% target, that is (90 − 82 × 0.7) ÷ 0.3 = 108.7% — more than a perfect paper, which is why the honest answer is sometimes "not reachable".

Knowing that before the exam is more useful than knowing it after. If the A is out of reach, the table shows what a B needs — often a very gettable score — and you can aim your revision at the grade that is actually available instead of chasing one that is not. If the target is already secured, you can revise calmly instead of panicking. Either way, the number replaces the guesswork.

Why the final's weight changes everything

A final worth 20% and a final worth 50% are different exams, even with the same questions. At 20%, a student sitting on 82% needs 122% for an A — impossible — but only 72% for a B, which is comfortable. At 50%, that same student needs 98% for the A: hard, but possible. The weight decides how much of your grade is still in play, and students who ignore it either panic over nothing or relax into a grade they cannot actually reach.

Weighted categories make the picture more honest still. A midterm worth 30% moves your grade three times as much as quizzes worth 10%, so a weak midterm hurts more than a weak quiz — and a strong one protects you more. Build your current grade from the real categories in your syllabus and the calculator shows the true starting point, not the average of a few remembered marks. As always, your syllabus is the authority: it states the weights, the cut-offs and whether the final can be skipped, and no calculator can overrule it.

Frequently asked questions

How is the required final score calculated?

With the formula: needed score = (target grade − current grade × (1 − final weight)) ÷ final weight. For example, a current grade of 82% with a target of 90% and a final worth 30% needs (90 − 82 × 0.7) ÷ 0.3 = 108.7% — which is not reachable, so the calculator tells you that too.

What does “Not reachable” mean?

It means even a perfect 100% on the final cannot lift you to your target grade, because your current grade and the final's weight do not leave enough room. The calculator also shows the highest final grade you can still reach.

What does “Already secured” mean?

It means your current grade is already high enough that you would reach your target even with 0% on the final. You still need to sit the exam if your school requires it — but the grade itself is safe.

Which grade scale does the letter table use?

The common US cut-offs: A 90%, B 80%, C 70%, D 60%, and Pass 50% (the pass mark used in many systems outside the US). Scales vary by school and course, so your syllabus is the one that counts.

Can I use points instead of percentages?

Yes — switch to Points mode and enter the points you have earned, the points possible so far, and the final exam's points. The calculator works out exactly how many points you need on the final.
