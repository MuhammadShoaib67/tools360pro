How to download a YouTube thumbnail

  1. Copy the link of any YouTube video (or a Shorts link, or just its video ID).
  2. Paste it above and press Get thumbnails.
  3. Pick a size and press Download — Full HD (1280×720) is the biggest, when it exists.

Why creators save other videos' thumbnails

A thumbnail is the single biggest reason a video gets clicked, so creators study them the way chefs taste rivals' cooking: what made this one work — the face, the three big words, the contrast, the curiosity gap? Saving the full-size version lets you zoom into the details that the small feed view hides, keep a reference folder for your niche, and check how a design holds up at every size YouTube displays. Bloggers and teachers also use thumbnails when writing about or embedding videos.

One honest line: the image belongs to the video's creator. Downloading it for study, commentary or with permission is normal practice; passing it off as your own design is not. YouTube itself serves these images publicly at fixed addresses — this tool simply finds the right address for the link you paste and hands you the file, without an account, a queue or a watermark.

Frequently asked questions

Which link formats work?

Any YouTube link: a normal watch link (youtube.com/watch?v=…), a short link (youtu.be/…), a Shorts link, an embed link — or just the 11-character video ID on its own.

Why is the Full HD thumbnail sometimes missing?

YouTube only creates the 1280×720 "maxres" image for videos uploaded in HD. Older or low-resolution videos top out at smaller sizes — the tool then shows the largest that exists.

Can I use a downloaded thumbnail on my own video?

Thumbnails belong to their creators. Use them for inspiration, reference, commentary or with the owner's permission — re-uploading someone else's thumbnail as your own can breach YouTube's rules and copyright.

Does this download the video itself?

No — only the thumbnail image (the picture shown before the video plays). It does not download videos or audio.
