Why marketplaces want square white photos

Every major marketplace displays products in square thumbnails, and most require — or strongly prefer — a white background. The reasons are practical: square thumbnails line up neatly in search results, and a white background makes the product the only thing in the frame. A listing with a square white photo looks professional next to one with a cluttered background, and shoppers notice the difference even if they cannot say why.

Amazon's rule is the strictest: the main image must be on a pure white background, with the product filling at least 85% of the frame. eBay and Etsy are more forgiving, but the same look wins clicks everywhere — a centred product on white, with nothing cropped. This tool builds exactly that: your photo fitted into the marketplace's square, centred, on white.

Fit, never crop

This tool fits rather than crops: the whole product is scaled to fit inside the square, and the leftover space becomes the background. Nothing is cut off — a tall bottle keeps its cap, a wide box keeps its corners. That matters because marketplaces crop uploaded photos themselves if they are not square, and their crop is centred — it does not know where your product ends.

If the product looks small in the frame, the photo itself has too much empty space around it. Crop the photo tightly first with the Crop Image tool, then resize here — the product will fill far more of the square. And if the background is not white to begin with, remove it first with the Background Remover, then let this tool add the clean white pad.

Size, zoom and file weight

Marketplaces ask for large images because shoppers zoom. Amazon wants at least 1000 pixels on the longest side for zoom to work, and recommends 2000; eBay recommends 1600; Shopify recommends 2048. Below those sizes the zoom is disabled or blurry, and a shopper who cannot inspect the product is a shopper who moves on.

Large images mean large files, but marketplaces compress uploads anyway — your job is to give them enough pixels to work with. Start from the largest original you have, and do not upscale a small photo to 2000 pixels expecting sharpness: enlarging spreads pixels thinner. If the original is small, the AI Image Upscaler rebuilds detail instead. Your original file is never changed — the download is always a new file.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Resizing runs on a canvas in your browser. Your product photos never leave your device.

What size should an Amazon product image be?

2000 × 2000 pixels on a pure white background, with the product filling at least 85% of the frame. That is the preset this tool offers for Amazon.

Will any of my product be cropped?

No. This tool fits the whole product inside the square and pads the leftover space — nothing is cut off. If the product looks small, crop the photo tightly first with the Crop Image tool.

Why is a white background so important?

Marketplaces display products in square thumbnails, and a white background makes the product the only thing in the frame. Amazon requires pure white for the main image; other marketplaces strongly prefer it.

Can I use a transparent background instead?

Yes — choose Transparent and download as PNG or WebP. JPEG cannot be transparent, so the background becomes white. Note that Amazon's main image must be white, not transparent.

What formats can I download?

Keep the original format, or choose JPEG, WebP or PNG. JPG is the usual choice for listings — marketplaces accept it everywhere and it keeps files small.
