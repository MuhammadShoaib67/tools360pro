Why convert JPG to WebP?

WebP compresses photographs better than JPG — typically 25 to 35 percent smaller at the same visual quality. That saving adds up fast: a page with twenty photos can shed megabytes by switching formats, and the page loads noticeably faster as a result. Faster pages keep visitors around longer, and search engines treat page speed as a ranking factor, so converting a site's photos to WebP is one of the cheapest performance improvements available.

Every modern browser displays WebP natively — Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari — so for anything shown on the web there is no compatibility worry. The main reason JPG still dominates is habit: cameras and phones save JPG by default, and people upload what their device produced. Converting to WebP before publishing is a small extra step with a real payoff in speed.

How much smaller — and is anything lost?

The saving depends on the photo. Smooth images with soft backgrounds — portraits, skies, product shots — compress especially well and can come out 40 percent smaller or more. Busy, detailed photos save less, but still save something. Each result shows its before-and-after size, so you can see the exact saving per photo instead of guessing.

WebP compression is lossy, like JPG, so a tiny amount of data is discarded. At the default 92% quality the loss is not visible in normal viewing. One honest caveat: converting a JPG to WebP cannot improve the photo — it can only preserve what is already there at a smaller size. If the JPG was saved at low quality and already looks soft, the WebP will look the same.

Quality and batch tips

The quality slider runs from 10% to 100%. Photos with smooth areas compress well even at 75%, while photos with fine detail — foliage, hair, fabric — deserve 85% or higher. Try one photo at 92% first, compare the size and the look, then convert the rest at the setting you liked.

Converting changes the format, not the dimensions: a 4000-pixel-wide JPG becomes a 4000-pixel-wide WebP. Your originals are never changed, so keep them as the archive. For a whole folder — a site's media library, a product catalogue — drop everything in at once and download the results as a ZIP.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Conversion runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device, which is why this tool is safe for private photos.

How much smaller is WebP than JPG?

Typically 25 to 35 percent smaller at the same visual quality. Smooth photos save more; busy, detailed photos save less. Each result shows its before-and-after size.

Will converting to WebP reduce quality?

WebP compression is lossy, so a tiny amount of data is discarded — but at the default 92% quality the difference is not visible in normal viewing.

Do all browsers support WebP?

Yes — Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari all display WebP natively. Very old browsers and some older apps do not, which is the main reason to keep a JPG copy for those cases.

Can I convert many JPG files at once?

Yes. Add as many JPG files as you like, convert them together, and download the results one by one or all together in a single ZIP file.

Will the WebP keep the same dimensions?

Yes. Converting changes the format, not the size of the picture. A 4000-pixel-wide JPG becomes a 4000-pixel-wide WebP. To change the dimensions, use the Resize Image tool.

Why convert JPG to WebP for a website?

Because smaller images load faster. A WebP is typically 25 to 35 percent smaller than the same photo as a JPG at the same visual quality, so a page full of WebP images loads noticeably quicker — better for visitors on slow connections and for your site's loading speed.

What quality setting should I use?

The 92% default is a good balance for most photos. Go lower (70–80%) for thumbnails and background images where file size matters most, and higher (95% or above) for hero images where fine detail counts.

Can I convert JPEG files too?

Yes — JPEG and JPG are the same format, just different file endings. Files ending in .jpeg convert exactly the same way: drop them here and download the WebP results.
