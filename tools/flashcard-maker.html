How to use the Flashcard Maker

  1. Paste your cards into the bulk box — one per line, written as Term = Definition — or add them one at a time. A spreadsheet works too: save it as CSV and import it.
  2. Click Study due cards to review what is due today, or Study all cards for the whole deck. Click a card (or press the spacebar) to flip it.
  3. After each flip, answer honestly: Knew it pushes the card further into the future, Still learning brings it back tomorrow.
  4. Use Test me when you want to be quizzed — it shows the definition and asks you to type the term.

Why flashcards work

Reading notes again feels productive, but it mostly builds familiarity — the feeling of having seen something before, which is not the same as being able to recall it in an exam. Flashcards force the opposite: you see the term and have to produce the definition from memory before you flip. That effort is the point. Every time you retrieve an answer, the memory gets easier to retrieve next time, and every time you fail, you find out exactly which card needs more work instead of re-reading the whole chapter.

The second ingredient is timing. Reviewing a card you already know is almost wasted time; reviewing a card you have half-forgotten is exactly when the memory strengthens most. That is what the schedule does: cards you know get longer and longer gaps, cards you struggle with come back tomorrow. A short daily session on the due cards beats a long cramming session the night before, and it takes less time overall. Pair it with focused study blocks — Pomodoro Timer runs 25-minute sessions with breaks — and summarise long notes first with Text Summarizer so your cards hold only what matters.

Spaced repetition, in plain English

This maker uses the SM-2 schedule, the same algorithm Anki popularised. Every card starts with an ease factor of 2.5 and an interval of zero days. When you mark a card Knew it, its interval grows — first to 1 day, then to 6 days, then by multiplying the last interval by the ease factor, so known cards gradually move to weeks and months. When you mark a card Still learning, its interval resets to 1 day and its ease factor drops a little, so it returns sooner and stays in rotation until it sticks.

Two honest notes. First, the two buttons are a simplification of SM-2's five-point scale: Knew it counts as a perfect recall and Still learning as a failed one, which is enough for everyday revision but coarser than Anki's Again / Hard / Good / Easy. Second, the schedule only works if you answer honestly — marking a card Knew it when you guessed pushes it weeks away, and the gap will find you out in the exam. Your decks, intervals and due dates are stored only in this browser, so they never leave your device.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add cards quickly?

Paste a list into the bulk box — one card per line, written as Term = Definition — and click Add cards. You can also add one card at a time with the term and definition boxes, or import a CSV file from a spreadsheet.

What is spaced repetition, and how does the schedule work?

Spaced repetition shows you a card again just before you are likely to forget it. This maker uses the SM-2 schedule: a card you know moves to a longer gap (1 day, then 6 days, then longer), while a card you mark Still learning comes back the next day. Cards you find hard appear more often, cards you know fade into the background.

Do my decks stay saved?

Yes — decks are saved in your browser's local storage, on this device only. They are still there when you close the tab and come back. They are not uploaded anywhere, so they do not follow you to another device; use CSV export to move a deck.

Can I print my flashcards?

Yes. Print cards opens your browser's print dialog with a cut-out grid: each card's term and definition print side by side in bordered boxes, ready to cut apart. Print to PDF from the same dialog if you want a file instead of paper.

Can I import cards from a spreadsheet?

Yes — save the sheet as CSV with the term in the first column and the definition in the second, then use Import CSV. Export CSV gives you the same format, so you can move a deck between this tool and a spreadsheet.
