Why photos come out sideways

Almost every sideways photo has the same cause: the phone was held sideways when the picture was taken, and the file stores an orientation flag telling viewers to rotate it. Most apps read that flag, but some — older viewers, certain upload forms, printers — ignore it, so the photo appears on its side. Rotating the actual pixels, as this tool does, fixes the picture itself rather than relying on a flag that not every app honours.

The same problem shows up with scans. A document fed into a scanner upside down, or a photo scanned at an angle, needs a real rotation before it can be read or shared. A 90° or 180° turn handles the common cases; the custom angle slider is for the in-between cases, like a scan that sits a few degrees off level.

Straightening scans and documents

A scan that is only slightly crooked is more annoying than one that is fully sideways: it looks almost right, but the text runs uphill and the page edges are visibly tilted. Small angles — 1° to 5° in either direction — are usually enough to fix it. Move the slider slowly and watch the preview: the goal is text that runs level with the frame, not a perfectly exact angle.

When you rotate by a custom angle, the corners of the picture swing outside the original frame. This tool grows the canvas to fit the whole rotated image, so nothing is cropped away. The new corners are transparent in PNG and WebP, or white in JPG. If the extra space bothers you, crop the result afterwards with the Crop Image tool.

Rotation, quality and file size

Rotating by 90° or 180° only rearranges the existing pixels — no detail is lost in the turn itself. What can cost quality is the save afterwards: if you download as JPG, the image is re-compressed, and a JPG saved from a JPG loses a little sharpness each time. For a photo you will edit further, download as PNG to keep every pixel intact; for a photo you are sending or uploading, JPG at the default 92% quality looks identical in normal viewing.

Your original file is never changed. This tool works on a copy in your browser and the download is a new file, so the sideways original stays exactly as it was. If you rotate the wrong way, just rotate again — or reload the original and start over.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Rotation runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device, which is why this tool is safe for private photos and documents.

Why does my photo look sideways only in some apps?

Your phone stores an orientation flag in the photo instead of rotating the pixels. Most apps read the flag, but some viewers, upload forms and printers ignore it. Rotating here rewrites the pixels themselves, so the photo looks right everywhere.

Why is there empty space around my rotated image?

At custom angles the corners of the picture swing outside the original frame, so the canvas grows to fit the whole image. The new corners are transparent in PNG and WebP, or white in JPG. Use the Crop Image tool afterwards if you want them trimmed.

Does rotating reduce quality?

The rotation itself only rearranges pixels, so nothing is lost in the turn. Quality is only affected when you save as JPG, which re-compresses the image. Download as PNG if you plan to edit the picture further.

Can I rotate by an exact angle, like 3 degrees?

Yes. Drag the angle slider to any whole degree between -180° and 180°. Small angles of 1° to 5° are the usual fix for crooked scans.

What formats can I download?

Keep the original format, or choose JPEG, WebP or PNG. PNG keeps transparency and loses nothing; JPG makes the smallest files for photos; WebP sits in between with transparency support.

Why are my phone photos sideways?

Phones sometimes save a photo sideways and rely on an orientation flag to say which way is up. Viewers that ignore the flag show the photo sideways. Rotating the image here fixes the pixels themselves, so the photo is the right way up everywhere.

Can I rotate by a custom angle?

Yes — the custom-angle slider rotates by any angle, not just 90° steps. That is the way to straighten a scan that sits a few degrees off level. The canvas grows to fit, so no corners are cut off.

Will rotating cut off the corners?

No. When you rotate by a custom angle, the canvas grows to fit the whole rotated image, so the corners stay in the picture. The image is never cropped by rotating it here.
