What the 'bill amount' should be

The calculator works from whatever amount you type, so the one decision that matters is what that amount is. Most people tip on the bill before tax, because tax is a charge the restaurant passes on, not part of the service — but others tip on the total shown at the bottom of the receipt, which is simpler and slightly more generous. Either way, the tool does the same maths; the choice is yours, and it is worth agreeing on it before the bill arrives if the table is splitting.

Discounts raise the same question. If a voucher took a third off the bill, tipping on the discounted total means the tip shrinks with the discount, while tipping on the original amount keeps the reward in line with the service actually received. There is no rule — only the habit you prefer. Type the amount you have decided on, pick a percentage or enter a custom one, and the tip, the total and each person's share update live.

Splitting fairly when the bill is not even

This calculator splits the total equally between everyone at the table, which is fair when the group ate roughly the same amount. When one person ordered a starter and a salad while another had a steak and two drinks, an equal split stops feeling fair — in that case, work out each person's share of the food first, then use the calculator to add the tip on each share, or agree that the heavier order covers a larger slice of the total before the rest is split.

Rounding is the last detail. Amounts are rounded to two decimal places, so when a total does not divide evenly, the shares can differ by a cent or two — one person pays a cent more than the rest, or a cent is left over. In practice the group just rounds the nearest share up or down and moves on. The 'Tip per person' tile is useful here too: it shows the tip alone divided by the number of people, so anyone who wants to leave their tip in cash can see exactly what to put on the table.

Tipping by setting: restaurant, taxi and takeaway

The right percentage depends on where you are and what the service was. In the United States, 20% has become a common restaurant default for full table service, and a restaurant tip calculator set to 20 percent is a useful starting point there — but customs vary widely by country, and in many places the service is already included or a smaller tip is normal. This tool does not prescribe a figure: the presets cover the common choices from 0% to 25%, and you can type any custom percentage that fits your situation.

Where the calculator earns its keep is the split. A tip calculator that splits the bill divides the total — bill plus tip — equally between everyone, and shows the tip per person too, so nobody has to do the maths at the table. As a bill splitter calculator it works just as well when there is no tip at all: set the tip to 0% and it simply divides the bill. Amounts are shown to two decimal places, so a split can differ by a cent or two — the person who had the extra drink usually volunteers to cover it.

Frequently asked questions

How is the tip calculated?

The tip is the bill amount multiplied by the tip percentage divided by 100. For example, a 15% tip on a bill of 100 is 100 × 15 ÷ 100 = 15, giving a total of 115.

How does splitting work?

The total (bill plus tip) is divided equally by the number of people for the “Each pays” figure, and the tip alone is divided the same way for “Tip per person”. Amounts are rounded to two decimal places, so small rounding differences of a cent can occur when splitting.

What tip percentage should I choose?

Customs vary widely by country and setting, and this tool does not prescribe one — the presets simply cover common choices from 0% to 25%. Pick what fits your situation, or type any custom percentage.

What happens if I type a custom tip?

Typing in the Custom tip % field deselects the preset buttons and uses your value instead. Clear the custom field and tap a preset to go back to a preset percentage.

Is my bill amount uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser and nothing is sent or stored.

What is 20% of a bill?

Multiply the bill by 0.20 — a 20% tip on an 80 bill is 16, making 96 in total. Type the bill amount and pick 20% here and the calculator shows the tip, the total and each person's share at once.

Can I split the bill without adding a tip?

Yes — set the tip to 0% and the calculator simply divides the bill equally between the number of people you enter. The per-person figure is then just your share of the bill.

What if everyone ordered different amounts?

This calculator splits the total equally between everyone — it does not track who ordered what. For uneven orders, work out each person's share separately, or use the even split as a starting point and adjust by hand.
