The EMI formula

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment: the fixed amount you pay every month towards a loan. The standard formula is EMI equals P times r times one plus r to the power of n, divided by one plus r to the power of n minus one — where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the number of monthly payments.

The rate needs converting first: an annual rate of 10% becomes a monthly rate of 10 divided by 12 divided by 100, which is 0.00833. The formula then spreads the loan and its interest evenly across the tenure, so every monthly payment is the same size even though the split between interest and principal changes each month.

Why early payments are mostly interest

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is highest at the start of the loan. On a 100,000 loan at 10% for one year, the first month's interest is 100,000 times 0.00833, which is 833 — so of the first EMI of about 8,792, only 7,958 repays the loan itself. By the final month, the balance is small, the interest is a few dozen, and almost the whole EMI goes to principal.

This is why extra payments early in a loan save the most interest. Any amount you pay above the EMI goes straight to the principal, which shrinks the balance that future interest is charged on. The schedule below shows the split month by month, so you can see exactly where each payment goes.

What changes your EMI

Three things decide the size of your EMI: the amount borrowed, the interest rate, and the tenure. A bigger loan or a higher rate raises the monthly payment; a longer tenure lowers it, because the same debt is spread over more months. But a longer tenure raises the total interest — you pay less each month and more overall.

That is the trade-off to weigh. A five-year loan at 10% on 100,000 costs about 2,125 a month and 27,500 in total interest; a one-year loan costs about 8,792 a month but only about 5,500 in interest. Use this calculator to compare tenures before you commit, and pick the shortest tenure whose monthly payment you can comfortably afford.

Rates themselves come in two kinds. A fixed rate stays the same for the whole tenure, so your EMI never changes; a floating rate moves with the market, so your EMI can rise or fall. Fixed rates are easier to budget around, floating rates can be cheaper if rates fall — but they can also cost more. This calculator assumes a fixed rate for the whole tenure, which is how most EMI quotes are given.

Frequently asked questions

What does EMI stand for?

Equated Monthly Instalment: the fixed amount you pay every month towards a loan, covering both interest and principal.

How is EMI different from a loan payment?

It is the same thing under a different name. EMI is the term used most often in India, Pakistan and other South Asian countries for the fixed monthly payment on a home, car or personal loan.

Why is the first payment mostly interest?

Interest is charged on the full outstanding balance, which is highest at the start. As the balance falls, the interest part shrinks and more of each EMI goes to the principal.

Does the schedule include the last payment?

Yes. The final month is adjusted so the balance ends at exactly zero, even with rounding.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Nothing you enter is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere.
