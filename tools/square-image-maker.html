How to use the Square Image Maker

  1. Drop an image onto the box above, or click to browse.
  2. Choose a method: Crop to square trims the longer edges; Pad to square keeps the whole image and fills the sides.
  3. For padding, pick a colour — or tick transparent padding for a PNG with see-through sides.
  4. Pick an output format if you want one, then press Download square image.

Why square still matters

Square is the shape the internet keeps coming back to: Instagram posts, profile pictures, marketplace thumbnails, app icons and product grids all favour it. A square image displays identically everywhere, with no awkward cropping by the platform — which is why making the square yourself beats letting an app guess.

The classic problem is a landscape photo uploaded to a square slot: the platform crops the left and right edges, often cutting off the subject. Making the square yourself means you decide what stays — crop deliberately, or pad so that nothing is lost at all.

Crop or pad: which to choose

Crop when the edges do not matter: a landscape where the subject sits in the middle, a group photo with space at the sides, a screenshot with wide margins. Cropping keeps the image full-bleed — no borders, no filler — and the result looks like it was always square.

Pad when the edges matter: a product photo where the item touches the frame, a diagram or poster where cutting would remove information, a tall portrait where cropping would cut off a head or feet. Padding adds space instead of removing picture, and a colour matched to the photo's background looks intentional. Transparent padding is the cleanest option for graphics: the square has see-through sides, so it sits on any background.

Square images and quality

Cropping to square removes pixels, so the result is smaller than the original — a 4000×3000 photo becomes 3000×3000. That is still plenty for screens, and the file gets smaller too. Padding keeps every original pixel and adds new ones, so the file is slightly larger and the dimensions grow to the longer side.

One detail to watch: padding with a colour that does not match looks like a mistake. White padding on a dark photo draws the eye to the border; a colour sampled from the photo's own edge blends in. If you cannot match the colour, transparent padding (PNG) is the safer choice — the page behind the image provides the background.

Profile pictures deserve a note of their own. Most platforms display them as circles cut from the centre of a square upload, so a square image with the subject centred survives the platform's crop. If your subject sits at the edge of the square, pad the image first so the subject moves to the middle — the platform will then crop the padding, not the person.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Everything runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device.

What is the difference between crop and pad?

Crop trims the longer edges away, so the square is cut from the middle of your photo. Pad keeps the whole photo and adds space on the shorter sides, so nothing is cut off.

Why did my transparent padding turn white?

JPEG cannot store transparency. If you tick transparent padding and download as JPEG, the padding is filled with your chosen colour instead. Use PNG or WebP for transparency.

What size is the square image?

Cropping makes a square from the shorter side of your photo; padding makes a square from the longer side. The exact dimensions are shown above the preview.

My photo is already square — what happens?

Nothing changes: cropping and padding a square image both return the same picture. The tool is only useful for rectangles.

What formats can I download?

Keep the original format, or choose JPEG, WebP or PNG. Use PNG or WebP if you want transparent padding; JPG is the smallest choice for photos.
