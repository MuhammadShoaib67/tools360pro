How to use the Barcode Generator

  1. Type the value the barcode should carry — a product number, a serial, or any short text for CODE128.
  2. Pick the format: EAN-13 or UPC-A for retail products, CODE128 for everything else, CODE39 for older systems.
  3. Adjust the bar height and width until the preview looks right, and keep the printed value on for human-readable labels.
  4. Download the PNG for documents and printing, or the SVG if you need a perfectly sharp image at any size.

Retail barcodes feel mysterious because the numbers are assigned, not invented: a company registers a prefix with GS1, adds its own item numbers, and the final digit is a check digit calculated from all the others, which is how tills catch mis-scans. This tool handles that last digit for you — enter the first 12 digits of an EAN-13 or the first 11 of a UPC-A and the correct check digit is added; enter the full number and it is verified instead.

For everything that is not a shop product — stock labels, asset tags, membership cards, event tickets, file references — CODE128 is the practical choice because it carries letters and numbers of any length. Whatever the format, two habits keep barcodes scannable: leave clear white space around them (the "quiet zone" scanners look for first) and never resize them in only one direction, which squeezes the bars together. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between CODE128, EAN-13, UPC and CODE39?

EAN-13 is the retail standard outside North America and UPC-A is its North American cousin — both encode a fixed product number (a GTIN) that shops scan at the till. CODE128 is the flexible one: it encodes any text, numbers and symbols, so warehouses and shipping labels use it for serial numbers and references. CODE39 is the older, simpler code still found on some industrial and library labels.

Do I have to work out the EAN-13 or UPC check digit myself?

No. Type the first 12 digits of an EAN-13 (or the first 11 of a UPC) and the tool calculates the final check digit for you. If you type the full number including its check digit, the tool verifies it and warns you if it does not add up.

Will a barcode made here actually scan?

Yes, if it is printed or displayed cleanly. Keep the quiet white space on both sides, do not stretch it out of proportion, print at a reasonable size (at least about 30 mm wide for retail codes) and test it with your phone camera before printing a whole batch.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The barcode is drawn in your browser from the text you type; nothing is sent to a server, and you download the PNG or SVG straight from this page.
