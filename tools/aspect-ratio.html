What an aspect ratio is

An aspect ratio is the shape of a rectangle, written as two numbers: width to height. 16:9 means 16 units wide for every 9 tall; 1:1 means as wide as it is tall. The numbers are reduced to their smallest whole form, so 1920 × 1080 and 1280 × 720 are both 16:9 — the same shape at different sizes.

The ratio is independent of size, which is what makes it useful. A phone screen, a cinema screen and a presentation slide can all be 16:9 while differing enormously in inches. When two things share a ratio, one fits the other exactly with no cropping and no empty space; when they differ, something has to give — a crop, a stretch, or padding.

The ratios you meet every day

1:1 is the square: profile pictures, Instagram posts, product thumbnails. 4:3 is the classic photo and old television shape — still the default of many cameras and projectors. 3:2 is the shape of most camera sensors and printed photos. 16:9 is widescreen: modern TVs, monitors, slides and video. 21:9 is ultrawide: cinema screens and wide monitors. 9:16 is 16:9 turned on its side — vertical video, stories and reels.

Knowing the ratio tells you what will happen when an image meets a frame. A 3:2 photo in a 16:9 slide leaves empty space top and bottom; a 16:9 video in a 1:1 post loses its left and right edges. Check the ratio before uploading, and resize or crop to match — the platform's automatic crop is rarely kind.

Finding a missing dimension

The common practical question is the reverse one: given a ratio and one side, what is the other? A 16:9 image that is 1920 wide must be 1080 tall; a 4:3 print that is 8 inches wide must be 6 tall. The maths is one multiplication — width times the ratio's height divided by its width — and the calculator below does it instantly, including for ratios you type in yourself.

This matters whenever you resize: typing both dimensions by hand risks a stretched image if the numbers do not share the original ratio. Compute the matching dimension first, then resize to the pair. Or use the Resize Image tool with its aspect lock, which does the same arithmetic for you.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the aspect ratio of an image?

Divide the width by the height and reduce to whole numbers: 1920 × 1080 becomes 16:9. Or drop the image on this page — the calculator detects its dimensions and ratio automatically.

What is the aspect ratio of 1920 × 1080?

16:9 — widescreen. It is the ratio of Full HD video, most monitors, and presentation slides.

What is the difference between 16:9 and 16:10?

16:10 is slightly taller: for the same width, it has more height. 16:9 is the video standard; 16:10 is common on laptop screens. The difference is small but visible — a 16:9 video on a 16:10 screen leaves thin bars top and bottom.

Can an aspect ratio be a decimal instead?

Yes — 16:9 is 1.78:1, and 21:9 is 2.33:1. The decimal form is handy for comparing shapes; the whole-number form is what people recognise and what platforms publish.

Does aspect ratio affect image quality?

No. The ratio is only the shape. Quality comes from the pixel dimensions: a 1920 × 1080 image and a 1280 × 720 image are both 16:9, but the first has more than twice the pixels and looks sharper.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. If you drop an image to detect its ratio, it is measured in your browser and never uploaded. The calculator itself needs no image at all.

How do I find the missing dimension from a ratio?

Pick the ratio, enter the side you know, and the calculator works out the other side. For example, with a 16:9 ratio and a width of 800 pixels, the height is 450 pixels — the same shape, scaled to your size.

What aspect ratio should I use for a YouTube video?

16:9 — widescreen. It is the standard ratio for YouTube, most monitors and presentation slides. A video in any other ratio will show bars on the sides or the top and bottom when it plays.

What is 21:9 used for?

Ultrawide monitors and cinematic video. 21:9 is much wider than 16:9, so it suits wide screens and film-style footage — and it is one of the presets on this calculator.
