How to use the Audio Trimmer

  1. Drop in your audio file. Drag an MP3, WAV, OGG or M4A file onto the dropzone, or click it to browse. The file is decoded in your browser — it is never uploaded.
  2. Pick your start and end points. Drag the sliders, type exact seconds into the number fields, or look at the waveform: flat stretches are silence, tall spikes are the loud parts. The green region is what you will keep.
  3. Preview the selection. Click Play Selection to hear exactly what the download will contain, or click anywhere on the waveform to play from that point to the end of the selection.
  4. Download the trimmed clip. Click Download Trimmed WAV. The file downloads with "-trimmed" added to its name, in WAV format, ready to use anywhere.

What the trimmer does with your file

When you drop a file in, your browser decodes it into raw audio samples using the Web Audio API — the same decoder that plays audio on every website. That is why the trimmer accepts whatever your browser can play: MP3, WAV, OGG, M4A/AAC and FLAC in most browsers, with small differences between browsers and devices. A file your browser cannot play cannot be trimmed here, and the tool will say so plainly instead of failing silently.

The waveform is drawn from those decoded samples: each vertical bar shows the loudest moment in its slice of the file, so silence shows as a flat line and loud passages as tall spikes. Your start and end points pick a slice of the samples, and the download is built from exactly that slice — the original decoded audio for the part you keep, with a tiny 10-millisecond fade at each cut point so the clip starts and ends cleanly instead of clicking. Nothing is re-compressed, so the part you keep sounds exactly as it did in the original file.

Why the download is a WAV file

Browsers can decode many audio formats, but they cannot encode MP3 — there is no MP3 encoder built into any browser, and adding one would mean shipping a large encoder library or sending your file to a server. Neither fits a tool that promises your files never leave your device, so the trimmer exports WAV: uncompressed 16-bit PCM audio, the format every audio app, editor, video editor and media player has opened for decades.

The trade-off is size. WAV stores every sample, so a trimmed clip is bigger than the same audio as an MP3 — roughly 10 MB per minute of stereo audio at CD quality (44.1 kHz). The tool shows the estimated size before you download, so you know what to expect. If you need the smaller MP3 file afterwards, any audio app can convert the WAV: open it, export or save as MP3, and the trimmed clip is ready to share anywhere.

Trimming tips

Start with the waveform. Long flat stretches at the beginning or end are silence or room noise, and cutting them is the most common trim of all — a voice note that starts three seconds late, a recording with applause trailing off at the end. Zoom in with your eyes, not the tool: the sliders move in hundredths of a second, and the number fields take any value, so you can land the cut exactly on a word or a beat.

Always preview before you download. Ears catch what eyes miss: a cut that lands half a word early, a breath that belongs to the next sentence, a fade that starts too soon. Play the selection once through, adjust the points if anything sounds clipped, and only then download. And keep the original file — trimming here never touches it, so you can always come back and cut a different section from the same recording.

Frequently asked questions

What audio formats can I trim?

Any format your browser can play and decode: MP3, WAV, OGG, M4A/AAC and FLAC in most browsers. If a file will not load, the browser cannot decode that format — try opening it in another browser, or convert it to WAV first in any audio app.

Why is the download a WAV file?

Browsers can decode many audio formats but cannot encode MP3 — there is no MP3 encoder built into the browser. The trimmer exports uncompressed WAV instead, which every audio app, editor and player opens. If you need MP3, convert the WAV afterwards in any audio app.

How big will the trimmed file be?

WAV is uncompressed, so it is bigger than MP3: roughly 10 MB per minute of stereo audio at CD quality (44.1 kHz). The tool shows the estimated size of your trimmed file before you download it, so there are no surprises.

Does trimming reduce the quality?

No. Trimming keeps the original decoded samples for the part you keep — nothing is re-compressed. A tiny 10-millisecond fade at each cut point keeps the start and end from clicking, and the rest of the audio is untouched.

Is my audio uploaded anywhere?

No. Your file is decoded, trimmed and encoded entirely in your browser. It never leaves your device, and nothing about it is stored or sent anywhere.
