How the dice are rolled

Each die is rolled with your browser's cryptographic random number generator, crypto.getRandomValues — the same source browsers use for secure random values like encryption keys. It produces unbiased random numbers, so every face of every die has an equal chance of coming up, and no roll can influence the next one.

The result of each die is shown separately, so you can see the individual rolls as well as the total. That matters in games where a single die decides something — a natural 20 on a d20, or doubles on two d6 — and not just the sum. The history below keeps the last ten rolls, so a disputed result can be checked against what was actually rolled a moment ago.

Dice notation explained (2d6, 1d20)

Dice notation is a shorthand used in tabletop games: the number before the d is how many dice you roll, and the number after it is how many sides each die has. 2d6 means two 6-sided dice; 1d20 means one 20-sided die; 3d8 means three 8-sided dice. The total is the sum of all the dice rolled.

Different games use different dice. Board games mostly use d6, while role-playing games use the full set: d4, d6, d8, d10, d12 and d20. A d20 decides most actions in games like Dungeons and Dragons, where a roll of 20 is a critical success and a roll of 1 is a critical failure. This roller covers the whole standard set.

Fairness and randomness

A fair die gives every face the same chance. Physical dice can be biased by uneven weight, rounded corners or worn faces — which is why serious players test their dice in salt water. A digital roller has no such problem: the random number generator has no memory, no wear, and no preference for any face.

That said, randomness includes streaks. Rolling three 1s in a row on a d20 is unlikely but entirely possible, and it does not mean the roller is broken or the next roll is due a high number. Each roll is independent — the dice have no memory of what came before.

Frequently asked questions

Are the dice rolls really random?

Yes. Rolls use your browser's cryptographic random number generator, which produces unbiased random numbers. Every face has an equal chance, and each roll is independent of the last.

What does 2d6 mean?

Dice notation: the number before the d is how many dice you roll, and the number after it is how many sides each die has. 2d6 means two 6-sided dice, added together.

Can I roll more than 6 dice at once?

This roller caps at 6 dice per roll to keep the display readable. For larger rolls, roll in batches and add the totals together — or use the Random Number Generator for bulk random numbers.

Does the roller remember my roll history?

Only on this page, and only the last 10 rolls. Reloading the page clears the history, and nothing is sent or stored anywhere.

Can I use this for tabletop games?

Yes — it covers the full standard set of dice (d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20) used in role-playing and board games, with totals and a visible history of recent rolls.

Can I roll a d20 online?

Yes — pick one die with 20 sides and roll. The result shows the die and the total, and the roll is added to your last-10 history. A d20 roll is the standard check in tabletop role-playing games.

How many dice can I roll at once?

1 to 6 dice at a time, each with d4, d6, d8, d10, d12 or d20 sides. Every die's result is shown separately, along with the total of all the dice.

Are the rolls really random?
