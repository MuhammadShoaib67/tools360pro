How to use the Stopwatch

  1. Click Start to begin timing. The display shows minutes, seconds and milliseconds.
  2. Click Pause to stop the clock, and Start again to resume from the same time.
  3. Click Lap at any moment to record the time without stopping the stopwatch — each lap shows its total time and its split since the previous lap.
  4. Click Reset to clear the time and all laps and start over.

How the stopwatch works

The stopwatch has three controls: Start and Pause on one button, Lap, and Reset. The display shows minutes, seconds and milliseconds, and hours appear automatically once the stopwatch passes 60 minutes. Laps are listed below the display with their total time and their split — the time since the previous lap.

Timing uses the browser's high-resolution clock rather than counting screen updates, so the stopwatch keeps accurate time even if the tab is busy or you switch to another tab. The display refreshes about 30 times a second, which is why the milliseconds look smooth without flickering.

Laps and split times

A lap records the time at a moment you choose, without stopping the stopwatch. Runners use laps for each circuit, swimmers for each length, and cooks for each stage of a recipe. Each lap shows two numbers: the total time since the stopwatch started, and the split — the time since the previous lap — so you can compare segments directly.

Laps stay on screen until you press Reset, so you can read them back at the end of a session. There is no limit on the number of laps, and no need to write anything down — the table keeps every lap in order.

Accuracy

The stopwatch is accurate enough for workouts, cooking, study sessions and everyday timing. The underlying clock is precise to a fraction of a millisecond, and the display updates about 30 times a second, so what you see is never more than a few hundredths of a second behind the true time.

It is not an official race timer. Reaction time on the Start and Lap buttons is the real limit — a human finger is accurate to roughly a tenth of a second at best — and a browser tab is not a certified timing device. For anything where hundredths of a second decide the result, use dedicated timing equipment.

Stopwatch vs timer

A stopwatch counts up from zero; a timer counts down to zero. They sound interchangeable, but they suit different jobs. Use a stopwatch when you want to measure how long something takes — a run, a task, a meeting. Use a timer when you want to stop after a set time — a Pomodoro session, a cooking step, a break. The two tools answer opposite questions: how long did that take, and how long is left.

This site has both. The Pomodoro Timer counts down in focused 25-minute sessions with breaks, which suits deep work; this stopwatch counts up with laps, which suits measuring. If you are timing a workout, use the stopwatch and record a lap per set. If you are trying to focus for half an hour without distractions, use the Pomodoro Timer instead. Pick the tool that matches the question, and the answer comes for free.

What is a lap?

A lap records the time at a moment you choose, without stopping the stopwatch. Each lap shows the total time and the split since the previous lap.

Yes, the timing keeps running, because it is based on the clock rather than on screen updates. Closing the tab stops it.
