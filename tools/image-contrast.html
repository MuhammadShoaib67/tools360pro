What contrast actually does

Contrast is the distance between the darkest and lightest parts of a picture. Raising it pushes darks darker and lights lighter, which makes edges crisper, colours richer and the whole image feel sharper. Lowering it pulls everything toward the middle grey, which softens the image and mutes harsh light.

Photos that look flat almost always suffer from low contrast: overcast days, hazy air, indoor lighting and scanned documents all compress the tonal range. A modest lift — 110% to 130% — is often the single biggest improvement you can make to such a photo, more noticeable than any brightness change.

Scanned documents are a special case. A scan of a printed page often has greyish paper and greyish ink, and raising the contrast pushes the paper toward white and the ink toward black — which is exactly what makes a scan readable. But the same move on a scan of a pencil drawing crushes the light shading into the paper, so go gently with hand-drawn originals and check the preview at full size before downloading.

When to raise it, when to lower it

Raise contrast for flat photos: landscapes on grey days, indoor shots under even lighting, scans of old prints that have faded to a narrow grey range. The extra separation between light and dark restores the sense of depth that flat light removes.

Lower contrast for harsh photos: midday sun that throws hard black shadows, flash photography with blown highlights, or portraits where strong contrast exaggerates skin texture. Softening the contrast evens out the light and is kinder to faces. The same applies to scanned handwriting, where too much contrast turns pencil shading into blotchy black patches.

Contrast, brightness and colour together

Contrast works best as part of a pair. Raising contrast alone can darken a photo overall, because the shadows move down faster than the highlights move up — a small brightness lift afterwards balances it. And because contrast makes colours appear richer, it often removes the need for a saturation change: many photos that look “dull” need contrast, not colour.

If you adjust several things, work in this order: brightness first, to set the overall lightness; contrast second, to set the punch; saturation last, and only if the colours still look flat. Each step is a separate tool here, so adjust one, download, and open the result in the next tool.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. The adjustment runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device.

What does 100% contrast mean?

100% is your original image, unchanged. Higher values push darks darker and lights lighter; lower values pull everything toward grey. At 0% the image is a flat grey.

Why does raising contrast make my photo darker?

Contrast moves the shadows down and the highlights up, and the shadows usually move further. Add a small brightness lift afterwards if the photo feels too dark overall.

Can contrast fix a blurry photo?

No. Contrast makes edges look crisper, which can give a slight impression of sharpness, but it cannot restore detail that blur removed. For genuinely blurry photos, the AI Photo Enhancer is a better try.

Why can't I download in my browser?

This tool applies its effect with canvas filters, which Safari 17 and older do not support. The preview still works, but downloading needs Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari 18+.

What formats can I download?

Keep the original format, or choose JPEG, WebP or PNG. PNG loses nothing; JPG makes the smallest files for photos.
