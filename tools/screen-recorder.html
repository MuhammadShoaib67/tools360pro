How to use the Screen Recorder

  1. Tick the microphone option if you want your voice in the recording.
  2. Press Start recording and choose what to share: your whole screen, one window, or one browser tab.
  3. Do your demonstration — the timer shows how long you have been recording.
  4. Press Stop recording (or stop sharing from the browser's bar). Preview the video, then download it.

Tutorials, bug reports and demos — without installing anything

Screen recordings are the fastest way to show instead of tell: a teacher walking through a lesson, a support agent reproducing a bug, a freelancer demonstrating finished work to a client, or a student recording a presentation. Until now that usually meant installing recording software — some of it paid, some of it bundled with things you never asked for. Your browser already has everything needed, which is what this tool uses.

Two habits give cleaner results. Record a single window or tab instead of the whole screen when you can: the file stays smaller, the text stays sharper, and nothing private (notifications, other windows, your desktop) can wander into the frame. And download your recording before you close the tab — it lives in the tab's memory, privately and by design, until you save it.

Frequently asked questions

Is my recording uploaded anywhere?

No. The recording is captured and stored only in your browser tab's memory. It exists as a file only when you press Download — nothing is uploaded, streamed or saved on any server.

Which browsers and devices work?

A desktop or laptop browser: Chrome, Edge or Firefox on Windows, Mac or Linux. Most mobile browsers do not allow websites to capture the screen, so this tool cannot record on phones or tablets.

Can I record sound as well?

Yes. Tick the microphone option to add your voice. Sound from a browser tab can also be captured in Chrome and Edge when you share a tab and enable the audio toggle in the sharing dialog. Whole-screen sharing does not include system sound.

What format is the video, and how big will it be?

WebM in Chrome, Edge and Firefox (MP4 in newer Safari). A typical 1080p recording uses roughly 2–5 MB per minute. WebM plays in all modern browsers and can be uploaded to YouTube directly.

Is there a time limit?

There is no built-in limit, but the recording is held in your tab's memory until you download it. For very long sessions (over an hour), split the recording into parts so a browser crash cannot lose everything.

Why did the recording stop by itself?

If you stop sharing from the browser's own sharing bar, or close the window or tab you were recording, the capture ends and this tool finalises the video for you — you can still download what was recorded.
