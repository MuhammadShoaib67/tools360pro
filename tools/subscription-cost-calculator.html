Small monthly fees add up

Ten a month sounds like nothing. It is 120 a year, and 600 over five years — for one subscription. Three subscriptions at ten a month each come to 360 a year, which is a holiday, a new phone, or several months of groceries, depending on where you live. Monthly billing is designed to feel small; the yearly total is the honest number.

The effect gets worse with time, because subscriptions renew themselves. A service you signed up for two years ago and stopped using six months ago is still charging you, and the amounts are small enough that most people do not notice them on a bank statement. Adding up the yearly totals is the first step to deciding which ones earn their place.

Free trials are the other trap. A trial that converts to a paid plan after seven or fourteen days is easy to forget, and the first charge is often the moment you realise the trial ended. If you start a trial, set a reminder for the day before it converts, and cancel the moment you decide you do not want it — the service will usually let you keep the trial until it expires.

Monthly vs yearly billing

Many services offer a yearly plan priced below twelve monthly payments — the saving is the service's way of buying your commitment for a year. Enter both prices and this calculator shows the exact saving, so you can decide whether the commitment is worth it. A saving of 20 a year on a 120 subscription is real money, but only if you will still use the service in twelve months.

Yearly billing is not always cheaper. Some yearly plans cost exactly twelve months with no discount, and some charge a fee for cancelling early. The calculator tells you plainly when yearly billing saves nothing — or when it actually costs more — so you never switch on an assumption.

The subscription audit

Once a year, list every subscription you pay for: streaming, music, cloud storage, apps, magazines, gym memberships. Write the monthly price next to each one, add them up, and multiply by twelve. Most people find at least one subscription they forgot about, and one they pay for twice under different names.

Then cancel what you do not use, and downgrade what you use rarely. A family plan shared properly is cheaper per person than separate accounts; a free tier is often enough for an app you open once a month. The audit takes twenty minutes and usually pays for itself many times over.

Frequently asked questions

How do I work out what a subscription costs per year?

Multiply the monthly price by 12. A 10 monthly subscription costs 120 a year; over five years that is 600.

Is yearly billing always cheaper?

Usually, but not always. Some yearly plans cost exactly twelve months with no discount, and some charge a cancellation fee. Enter both prices to see the real saving before switching.

Can I add up several subscriptions?

This calculator totals one subscription at a time. To add up several, run each one and add the yearly totals together — or use the 5-year figures for a longer view.

What if the price goes up?

Prices rise, so treat the 5-year figure as a minimum based on today's price. Re-check once a year to keep the numbers honest.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Nothing you enter is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere.
