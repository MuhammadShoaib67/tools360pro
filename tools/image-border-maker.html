Why add a border at all

A border does two jobs at once: it frames the picture, and it separates it from whatever sits next to it. On a white web page, a photo with white edges melts into the background; a thin border gives it an edge and makes it look finished. On a crowded social feed, a coloured border is what makes one post recognisable as yours before anyone reads the caption.

Borders are also practical. A printed photo with a white border is easier to handle and frame, and a screenshot with a border is easier to spot in a document full of images. The key is restraint: a border should frame the picture, not compete with it. For most photos, 10 to 30 pixels is enough; thicker borders suit posters, certificates and images that need to feel formal.

Choosing a thickness and colour

Thickness is a matter of scale. On a small image — a 500-pixel profile picture — a 20-pixel border is already prominent, while on a 4000-pixel print the same border is barely visible. As a rule of thumb, start at about 2% of the image's longest side and adjust from the preview: if the border disappears, go thicker; if it dominates, go thinner.

Colour sets the mood. White is the classic choice: it reads as a clean frame and works on any background. Black feels formal and makes colours look richer, which is why galleries use it. A brand colour turns the border into a signature — the same colour on every post makes a feed look deliberate. Avoid colours that clash with the photo itself: a bright red border around a green landscape fights the picture instead of framing it.

Borders, size and quality

This tool adds the border outside the image, so the picture itself is never resized or cropped — the file simply grows by twice the thickness on each side. A 1000 × 800 photo with a 20-pixel border becomes 1040 × 840. If the exact final size matters, for example for a listing that needs a specific dimension, resize the result afterwards with the Resize Image tool.

The border itself is a flat colour, which compresses extremely well — adding a border barely changes the file size. Quality only changes if you save as JPG, which re-compresses the whole image including the photo. For a picture you will edit further, download as PNG; for sharing or uploading, JPG at the default 92% quality looks identical in normal viewing. Your original file is never changed — the download is always a new file.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. The border is drawn on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device.

Will the border cover part of my photo?

No. The border is added around the outside, so the whole photo stays visible. The file grows by twice the thickness on each side — a 20-pixel border makes the image 40 pixels wider and 40 pixels taller.

What thickness should I use?

Start at about 2% of the image's longest side — around 20 pixels for a 1000-pixel photo. Thinner borders look clean and modern; thicker ones suit posters, certificates and formal images.

Can I add a border to a transparent PNG?

Yes. The border is drawn around the full rectangle of the image, including its transparent areas, so the result is a framed rectangle. If you want the border to follow the shape of a cut-out subject, use the Image Shadow Maker instead, which follows the image's outline.

Why is my border a different colour than I picked?

Colour pickers show the colour on your screen, and screens vary — the same hex value can look slightly different on another display. The saved file uses exactly the colour you picked; what changes is how each screen renders it.

What formats can I download?

Keep the original format, or choose JPEG, WebP or PNG. PNG keeps transparency and loses nothing; JPG makes the smallest files for photos.

Does the border cover part of my photo?

No — the border is added outside the image, so nothing is cropped or covered. The file grows by the border thickness on every side, and every pixel of the original photo survives.

Can I add a white border for Instagram?
