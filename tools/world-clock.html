What each clock shows

Every clock shows three things: the current time in that city, ticking every second; the date, including the weekday, so you can see at a glance whether it is already tomorrow there — or still yesterday; and the UTC offset in force right now, which tells you how far ahead of or behind Coordinated Universal Time that city sits. The offset matters because it is the number you actually calculate with: if Karachi is UTC+05:00 and London is UTC+00:00, Karachi is five hours ahead, and a 3 p.m. call in Karachi is 10 a.m. in London.

The clocks use your device clock and the same IANA time zone database your operating system uses, through the browser's Intl API. That means daylight saving is handled automatically — when a city moves its clocks forward or back, the time and the offset shown here move with it, with no setting to change. There is no server involved and no network request: the page works offline once loaded, and nothing you select is uploaded or stored anywhere.

Planning across time zones

A world clock earns its place on the day you work with people in more than one city. Before you send a meeting invite, glance at the clocks: is it 9 a.m. for you and 2 a.m. for them? Before you call family abroad, check whether it is a reasonable hour there. Before a product launch or a live event, confirm the start time in every city your audience sits in, and write the time zone next to the hour in the announcement — "3 p.m. Karachi time" leaves no room for doubt, while "3 p.m." on its own invites exactly the confusion you are trying to avoid.

The date line is the part people forget. A late-evening call from Sydney lands on the previous morning in Los Angeles, and a morning meeting in New York is already evening in Tokyo. That is why each clock shows its date as well as its time: a shift across midnight is visible at a glance rather than discovered when someone misses the call. Why the gap between two cities keeps changing

Time zones are not fixed distances. Many regions move their clocks forward in spring and back in autumn for daylight saving time, so the gap between two cities can change by an hour twice a year — and the change happens on different dates in different places. The southern hemisphere runs on the opposite cycle, which means the difference between, say, London and Sydney shifts several times a year. A clock that only knew today's offset would quietly give the wrong answer for a meeting booked three months away.

Some zones also sit on half-hour or 45-minute offsets — India is UTC+05:30, Nepal UTC+05:45 — so the difference between two cities is not always a whole number of hours. The world clock shows the live offset for each city, so the arithmetic is always done with the number in force today, not the one you remember from last month. Frequently asked questions

How accurate are the clocks?

They use your device clock and the browser's built-in time zone database, so they are as accurate as your device's clock. If your device clock is wrong, the world clocks will be wrong by the same amount.

Can I add a city that is not listed?

The add list covers 29 common time zones, from Honolulu to Auckland. If your city is not listed by name, pick any city in the same time zone — the time shown will be the same.

Do the clocks keep working if I switch tabs?

Yes, the clocks keep updating while the page is open, even in a background tab. Browsers may slow background tabs down, so the seconds may tick less often until you switch back — the time shown stays correct.

What does the UTC offset mean?

A UTC offset is the difference between a time zone and Coordinated Universal Time. UTC+05:00 means five hours ahead of UTC; UTC−04:00 means four hours behind. The offset can change during the year where daylight saving applies.

Is any of my data uploaded?

No. The clocks run entirely in your browser using the Intl API. Nothing you enter or select is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere.

Which cities does the world clock show?

Eight clocks load automatically: New York, London, Dubai, Karachi, Singapore, Tokyo, Sydney and UTC. Add more from the 29-zone dropdown — Honolulu to Auckland — and remove any clock you don't need.

Why do some clocks show a different date?

Because the world crosses midnight at different times: when it is late evening in Sydney, it is still the previous morning in Los Angeles. Each clock shows its own date as well as its time, so a shift across midnight is visible at a glance.

Can I switch between 12-hour and 24-hour time?

Yes — switch between 24-hour and 12-hour formats at any time. The clocks keep ticking every second either way, and each clock keeps showing its date and UTC offset.
