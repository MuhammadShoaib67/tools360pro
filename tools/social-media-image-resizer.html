Why platforms crop your photos

Every platform shows images in its own shape, and anything that does not match gets cropped — usually from the centre, with no regard for what matters in the picture. A wide landscape uploaded as an Instagram post loses its left and right edges; a tall portrait uploaded to Facebook loses its top and bottom. The crop happens silently, and the result is a photo that looks wrong for no obvious reason.

Resizing to the platform's size before uploading puts you in control of the crop. You decide what stays in the frame, and the platform displays the image exactly as you made it. That is the whole job of this tool: nine presets covering the sizes each platform recommends, so the upload looks the way you intended.

Crop to fill vs fit with padding

Crop to fill scales the image until it covers the whole frame, then trims the overflow from the edges. The result fills the screen edge to edge, which is what posts and thumbnails want. The cost is the trimmed part: check the preview to make sure nothing important — a face, a logo, a line of text — sits in the trimmed zone.

Fit with padding scales the whole image down until it fits inside the frame, then fills the leftover space with a colour of your choice. Nothing is cropped, which suits diagrams, posters and product photos where every edge matters. The padding is part of the image, so a white pad on a white feed looks invisible, while a coloured pad looks like a deliberate frame.

The sizes, and why they change

The presets here are the standard sizes each platform publishes: Instagram post 1080 × 1080, portrait 1080 × 1350, story and reel 1080 × 1920; Facebook post 1200 × 630 and cover 820 × 312; X post 1600 × 900; LinkedIn post 1200 × 627; YouTube thumbnail 1280 × 720; Pinterest pin 1000 × 1500. Those numbers are stable, but platforms do revise them — if a size ever looks off, check the platform's current help page and use the custom route in the Resize Image tool.

One caution: enlarging a small image to a large preset does not add detail. A 500-pixel photo stretched to a 1080-pixel post looks soft. Start from the largest original you have; if the original is small, the AI Image Upscaler rebuilds detail instead of merely stretching. Your original file is never changed — the download is always a new file.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Resizing runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device.

What size should an Instagram post be?

1080 × 1080 pixels for a square post, 1080 × 1350 for a portrait post, and 1080 × 1920 for a story or reel. This tool has all three as presets.

What is the difference between crop to fill and fit with padding?

Crop to fill scales the image until it covers the frame and trims the overflow — the frame is full, but the edges are cut. Fit with padding shows the whole image and fills the leftover space with a colour you choose.

Why does the platform still crop my image?

Usually because the uploaded file was not the resized one — check the filename and dimensions. A few surfaces, like link previews, crop to their own shape regardless of the file; for those, keep the important content in the centre of the image.

Are these sizes still current?

They are the standard sizes each platform recommends, and they change rarely. If a platform has revised a size, the Resize Image tool lets you type any exact dimensions.

What formats can I download?

Keep the original format, or choose JPEG, WebP or PNG. JPG is the usual choice for social uploads — small files that upload quickly.
