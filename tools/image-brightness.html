Why photos come out too dark

Phone cameras make a guess about exposure every time you press the shutter, and the guess is often wrong in tricky light: a bright window behind your subject, a dim restaurant, a sunset that fools the meter. The result is a photo where the subject is a silhouette or the whole scene sits in murky shadow. Raising the brightness lifts those shadows back into view.

Brightness works by scaling every pixel's value up or down. It cannot recover detail that was never captured — a fully black shadow has no information to lift — but most “too dark” photos are only underexposed by a stop or two, and a modest lift of 120–140% brings back faces, food and scenery that looked lost.

How much brightness is too much

The danger of brightening is at the other end: pixels that were already bright hit pure white and lose all detail. A sky that was pale blue becomes a flat white sheet; a white shirt loses its folds. Watch the brightest parts of your photo as you drag — when they start to go flat, you have gone too far.

The same applies in reverse. Darkening a washed-out photo restores depth, but push it too far and the shadows crush to black. Small moves are usually enough: most fixes land between 80% and 130%. If a photo needs more than that, the exposure was badly off, and a gentle touch plus a contrast tweak will look better than brightness alone.

Brightness, exposure and contrast

Brightness and exposure are often confused. Exposure is a camera setting — how much light hit the sensor — while brightness is an edit applied afterwards. They feel similar because both make a picture lighter or darker, but brightness applies evenly across the whole image, where a real exposure change affects the shadows and highlights differently.

Brightness also works best with a little contrast. Lifting brightness alone can make a photo look washed out, because the darks and lights move closer together. If your brightened photo looks flat, follow up with the Contrast Adjuster: a small contrast lift restores the separation between light and dark that makes a photo feel three-dimensional.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. The adjustment runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device.

What does 100% brightness mean?

100% is your original image, unchanged. Values above 100% brighten it; values below 100% darken it. At 0% the image is fully black.

Can brightness fix a completely black photo?

No. Brightness scales the detail that is already there. Pixels that are fully black contain no information, so they stay black however far you drag the slider.

Why does my brightened photo look washed out?

Brightening lifts the shadows toward the highlights, which flattens the picture. Add a little contrast afterwards with the Contrast Adjuster to restore depth.

Why can't I download in my browser?

This tool applies its effect with canvas filters, which Safari 17 and older do not support. The preview still works, but downloading needs Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari 18+.

What formats can I download?

Keep the original format, or choose JPEG, WebP or PNG. PNG loses nothing; JPG makes the smallest files for photos.
