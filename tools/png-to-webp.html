Why convert PNG to WebP?

WebP was designed to do what PNG does, in a much smaller file. A PNG converted to WebP is typically 25 to 35 percent smaller at the same visual quality, and for graphics with flat colours the saving can be larger still. Unlike JPG, WebP keeps transparency, so logos, icons and graphics with see-through backgrounds convert without losing anything that matters. That combination — smaller files plus transparency — is why WebP has become the standard image format for modern websites.

Smaller images mean faster pages. A page full of PNG screenshots and graphics can take seconds longer to load than the same page with WebP images, and visitors notice the difference. Search engines do too: page speed is part of how pages are ranked, so converting a site's images to WebP is one of the cheapest performance wins available. Every modern browser — Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari — displays WebP natively, so there is no compatibility worry for web use.

WebP, JPG or PNG — which should you pick?

Pick WebP when the image is going on a website or into an app: it is smaller than PNG, keeps transparency unlike JPG, and every current browser supports it. Pick JPG when the destination is a form, an email or an older system that does not accept WebP — JPG is still the most universally accepted photo format. Keep PNG when the image must be edited further without any loss, or when it will be opened in software that predates WebP.

One honest caveat: WebP compression is lossy at the quality settings this tool uses, so a tiny amount of data is discarded. At the default 92% the loss is not visible in normal viewing — logos stay sharp and screenshots stay readable. If a graphic must be pixel-perfect, PNG remains the lossless choice, and the larger file is the price of that perfection.

Quality and batch tips

The quality slider runs from 10% to 100% and controls how hard WebP compresses. Graphics with sharp edges and text — logos, screenshots, diagrams — deserve a higher setting, around 90% or above, because compression artefacts show up first on hard edges. Photos and smooth gradients compress well at lower settings. Try one image at 92% first, check the size and the look, then convert the rest.

Converting changes the format, not the dimensions: a 2000-pixel-wide PNG becomes a 2000-pixel-wide WebP. Your originals are never changed, so keep them as the archive. Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Conversion runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device, which is why this tool is safe for private photos and screenshots.

Does WebP keep transparency?

Yes. Unlike JPG, WebP supports transparency, so logos and graphics with see-through backgrounds convert without losing it.

Will converting to WebP reduce quality?

WebP compression is lossy, so a tiny amount of data is discarded — but at the default 92% quality the difference is not visible in normal viewing. Logos stay sharp and screenshots stay readable.

Do all browsers support WebP?

Yes — Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari all display WebP natively. Very old browsers and some older apps do not, which is the main reason to keep a JPG or PNG copy for those cases.

Can I convert many PNG files at once?

Yes. Add as many PNG files as you like, convert them together, and download the results one by one or all together in a single ZIP file.

Will the WebP keep the same dimensions?

Yes. Converting changes the format, not the size of the picture. A 2000-pixel-wide PNG becomes a 2000-pixel-wide WebP. To change the dimensions, use the Resize Image tool.

What quality setting should I use for WebP?

The 92% default is a good balance for most images — small files with no visible loss. Go lower (70 to 80%) for thumbnails and background images where file size matters most, and higher (95% or above) for hero images and logos where fine detail counts.

Why convert PNG to WebP for a website?

Because smaller images load faster. A WebP is typically 25 to 35 percent smaller than the same image as a PNG at the same visual quality, so a page full of WebP images loads noticeably quicker — better for visitors on slow connections and for your site's loading speed.

Is the WebP lossy or lossless?

This converter makes lossy WebP with a quality slider, like JPG: a tiny amount of data is discarded to make the file smaller. At the default 92% quality the difference is not visible in normal viewing, and logos stay sharp — but the file is not a pixel-perfect copy of the PNG.
