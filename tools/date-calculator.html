How to use the Date Calculator

  1. For a duration, pick the start and end dates — the exact span appears instantly.
  2. For a deadline, switch to Add / subtract days, enter the number of days, and read the resulting date and weekday.

Deadlines, countdowns and 'how long until…'

Date arithmetic looks trivial until months of different lengths and the occasional 29 February get involved — which is exactly when the answer matters: a visa that expires 90 days after entry, a contract notice period, a project countdown, a baby's due-date maths, or the simple question of how many days you have been alive. Counting squares on a calendar is slow and error-prone; this calculator does the real calendar maths, leap years and century rules included.

The result is given three ways because people think about time three ways: the raw total of days (what contracts use), weeks and leftover days (what planners use), and the years-months-days breakdown (how everyone states an age). The add/subtract mode answers the mirror-image question — not "how long between" but "when exactly" — and names the weekday, because a deadline that lands on a Sunday is a different deadline.

Frequently asked questions

Does the count include both the first and last day?

The result is the exact difference: from 1 January to 2 January is 1 day. This matches how contracts, ages and countdowns are normally counted. If you need inclusive counting (both endpoints), simply add one day.

Are leap years handled?

Yes. The calculation uses the real calendar, so a span covering 29 February counts that extra day, and century rules (1900 not a leap year, 2000 a leap year) are applied correctly.

What is the "years, months, days" breakdown?

The same span expressed the way people say ages: whole calendar years first, then whole months, then the leftover days. It always adds back up to the exact total days shown.

Can I find a deadline like "90 days from today"?

Yes — use the Add / subtract mode: pick today (or any start date), enter 90 days, and the resulting date and weekday are shown.
