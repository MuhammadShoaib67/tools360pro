How to use the Random Group Generator

  1. Paste the names, one per line — from a class list, a spreadsheet column, or typed by hand.
  2. Choose how to split them: a fixed number of groups, or a fixed group size.
  3. Press Generate groups. Use Reshuffle for a fresh split, Project mode to show the groups on a screen, or Copy to paste them into a document.

Fair groups, without the awkward picking

Picking groups by hand is slow and rarely feels fair: the same confident students end up together, someone is always picked last, and the teacher spends ten minutes on what should take ten seconds. A random split removes all of that. Nobody is chosen or left out on purpose, mixed-ability groups happen by chance instead of by design, and the class gets on with the work instead of negotiating who sits with whom.

The two modes cover the two ways teachers actually plan. "Number of groups" fits the lesson — four discussion groups, six lab stations, three debate teams — and the generator works out the sizes. "Group size" fits the activity — pairs for peer review, threes for group work, fives for a project — and the generator works out how many groups that makes. Either way the sizes stay balanced, so no group is left with one person doing the work of three. For a plain shuffled list with no grouping, List Randomizer shuffles any list and picks random winners.

How the shuffle stays fair

The generator uses the Fisher-Yates shuffle, the standard algorithm for an unbiased shuffle, driven by your browser's cryptographic random number generator — the same source of randomness used for security features. Every possible ordering of the names is equally likely, so no student is more likely to land in any particular group. After the shuffle, names are dealt one by one into the groups, like dealing cards, which is what keeps the sizes balanced: a class of 23 split into 5 groups gives groups of 5, 5, 5, 4 and 4, never a group of 8 and a group of 2.

Privacy matters here more than with most tools, because a class list is children's names. The list is stored only in your own browser, in localStorage, so it is still there when you come back tomorrow — and it is never sent to any server, because there is no server involved at all. Press "Clear saved list" and the names are gone from the browser completely. Project mode is designed for the classroom display: large cards, high contrast, readable from the back row, so students can find their group without crowding around the teacher's desk.

Frequently asked questions

How are the groups shuffled?

With the Fisher-Yates shuffle, the standard unbiased shuffling algorithm, driven by your browser's cryptographic random number generator. Every ordering of the names is equally likely — no name is favoured.

Are the groups balanced?

Yes. After shuffling, names are dealt one by one into the groups, like dealing cards, so group sizes differ by at most one person. A class of 23 split into 5 groups gives groups of 5, 5, 5, 4 and 4.

Do the names leave my browser?

No. The names are stored only in your own browser (localStorage) so the list is still there when you come back. Nothing is uploaded to any server, and clearing the saved list removes them completely.

What is project mode?

A full-screen view with large group cards, made for showing on a projector or whiteboard so the whole class can find their group from their seats. Press Escape or Close to return to the normal view.

How many names can I add?

There is no fixed limit in the tool. The list is stored in your browser, so very long lists of thousands of names depend on your browser's storage, but any normal class, team or workshop list works fine.
