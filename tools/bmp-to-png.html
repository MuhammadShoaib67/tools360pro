Why convert BMP to PNG?

BMP is one of the oldest image formats still in use, and it stores every pixel without any compression at all. That makes BMP files enormous: a single photo can be tens of megabytes, where the same picture as a PNG is a few megabytes and as a JPG is a few hundred kilobytes. BMP files are awkward to email, slow to upload and wasteful to store — and most websites will not accept them.

PNG is the natural replacement. Like BMP it is lossless, so every pixel survives the conversion exactly — but PNG compresses the data, typically shrinking a BMP by 50 to 70 percent. The result opens in every image app, uploads anywhere and takes a fraction of the space. There is no reason to keep a BMP once a PNG exists.

Nothing is lost in the conversion

Both BMP and PNG are lossless formats, which means this conversion is exact: every pixel in the BMP appears unchanged in the PNG. No detail is discarded, no colours shift, no quality setting is needed. The only thing that changes is how the pixels are stored — compressed instead of raw — and that is where the size saving comes from.

That makes BMP to PNG one of the safest conversions there is. If the BMP looks good, the PNG will look identical. The only files that can surprise you are very old or unusual BMP variants — some use colour palettes or bit depths that modern software handles differently — but ordinary BMPs from Windows, scanners and cameras convert cleanly.

Where BMP files still show up

BMP survives in a few corners: scans from older scanners, screenshots from old Windows software, images exported by some industrial and medical devices, and files passed around in offices that have used the same workflow for decades. If you have received a BMP, it is usually because the sender's software produced it by default, not because BMP was a deliberate choice.

Converting changes the format, not the dimensions: a 3000-pixel-wide BMP becomes a 3000-pixel-wide PNG. Your originals are never changed, so keep them if you need the exact original file. For a whole folder of BMP scans or screenshots, drop them all in at once and download everything as a ZIP.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Conversion runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device, which is why this tool is safe for private scans and screenshots.

Will converting to PNG lose any quality?

No. Both BMP and PNG are lossless, so every pixel survives the conversion exactly. The PNG looks identical to the BMP — it is simply stored more efficiently.

How much smaller will the PNG be?

Typically 50 to 70 percent smaller. A 30 MB BMP often becomes a 10 MB PNG. The exact saving depends on the image — flat graphics compress more than busy photos.

Can I convert many BMP files at once?

Yes. Add as many BMP files as you like, convert them together, and download the results one by one or all together in a single ZIP file.

Will the PNG keep the same dimensions?

Yes. Converting changes the format, not the size of the picture. A 3000-pixel-wide BMP becomes a 3000-pixel-wide PNG. To change the dimensions, use the Resize Image tool.

Why are BMP files so large?

BMP stores every pixel without any compression. PNG stores the same pixels with lossless compression, which is why the same picture takes a fraction of the space as a PNG.
