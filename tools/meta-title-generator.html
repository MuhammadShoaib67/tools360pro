What makes a title work

A good meta title does two jobs at once: it tells the search engine what the page is about, and it persuades a person scanning ten blue links to click yours. The keyword belongs near the start, because both readers and search engines weigh the first words most. After the keyword, the title should promise something specific: a complete guide, a step-by-step method, a comparison, a set of tips. Vague titles get skipped; specific titles get clicked.

Honesty matters as much as craft. A title that promises ten tips must deliver ten tips, and a title that calls a page the complete guide should not lead to three short paragraphs. Google rewrites titles it finds misleading or stuffed with keywords, and readers who feel tricked bounce straight back to the results, which is a signal no page wants.

Title length: characters vs pixels

Titles are truncated by width, not by character count: Google cuts a title off at about 600 pixels and shows an ellipsis. That is why the familiar 60-character rule is only approximate — a title full of wide letters like W and M runs out of room sooner than one full of narrow letters like i and l. This tool measures each suggestion in pixels, using a 20px Arial canvas in your browser, which matches the size Google uses for result titles.

Aim for under 600 pixels and, as a rough guide, under 60 characters. If your best title is slightly over, trim a filler word rather than cutting the keyword: remove the, a complete, or a repeated word before you touch the part people search for. Brand names at the end are the first thing to drop when space is tight.

Templates, not magic

The suggestions on this page come from proven title templates, not from AI: a how-to frame, a complete-guide frame, a comparison frame, a tips frame, and a few others that have worked across thousands of pages. Templates give a title its structure; the keyword and the specifics of your page give it its meaning. Treat every suggestion as a starting point and edit it until it describes your page exactly.

One more habit is worth building: write the title after the page, not before it. A title written from the finished content matches what the page actually delivers, which is what Google checks when it decides whether to show your title or rewrite it. If you change the page later, change the title in the same edit.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a meta title be?

Aim for under 60 characters and under 600 pixels wide. Google truncates longer titles with an ellipsis, which hides the end of your message from searchers.

Should the keyword go first?

Usually yes. Readers scan titles from the left, and search engines weigh the opening words most, so the keyword earns its place at the start unless the title reads unnaturally that way.

Are these titles written by AI?

No. They come from proven templates filled with your keyword. Edit the result until it describes your page exactly: templates give structure, your content gives meaning.

Why does Google sometimes rewrite my title?

Google rewrites titles it finds too long, stuffed with keywords, or mismatched with the page content. A short, honest, keyword-led title is usually shown as written.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. This tool runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Nothing you type is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere.
