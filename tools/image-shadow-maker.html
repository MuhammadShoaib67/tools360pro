What a drop shadow actually does

A drop shadow is a blurred, offset copy of your image's shape, drawn underneath it. Our eyes read that offset as depth: the picture looks as if it is floating slightly above the surface. It is the same trick used in product photos, app icons and presentation slides — a small shadow makes a flat image feel like an object.

The shadow follows the image's opaque shape, not its rectangle. On a logo with a transparent background, the shadow hugs the letters or the icon. On a full rectangular photo, there is no shape to follow, so the shadow appears only around the edges — a subtle lift rather than a dramatic effect. That is why this tool shines with cut-outs: remove the background first with the Background Remover, then add the shadow here.

Offset, blur and opacity — the three settings that matter

Offset is the direction of the light. A shadow offset down and to the right suggests light from the upper left, which is what our eyes expect — it is how sunlight and ceiling lights usually fall. Small offsets of 8 to 15 pixels look natural; large offsets look like the object is floating high above the surface, which suits playful designs but looks odd on a product photo.

Blur is the softness. A sharp shadow with no blur looks like a hard cut-out — fine for a bold graphic style, wrong for realism. A blur of 15 to 30 pixels gives the soft edge of a real shadow. Opacity is the strength: real shadows are rarely black. Dark grey at 30% to 50% opacity reads as a genuine shadow; 100% black looks like a sticker. When in doubt, go softer and lighter than you think — subtle shadows look professional, heavy ones look dated.

Transparency, padding and file size

The shadow needs room: this tool adds transparent padding around your image so the shadow is never cut off. The amount of padding grows with the blur and offset you choose. Download as PNG to keep that transparency — a JPG cannot be transparent, so the padding becomes white and the shadow sits on a white background instead of floating.

Transparent PNGs are larger than JPGs, and a big soft shadow adds to the size because the padding area is no longer empty. For a web page, that trade is usually worth it: a shadowed logo on a transparent background sits cleanly on any page colour. If the file feels too large, the Compress Image tool shrinks it without touching the shadow. Your original file is never changed — the download is always a new file.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. The shadow is drawn on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device.

Why does my photo barely show a shadow?

The shadow follows the image's opaque shape. A full photo is an opaque rectangle, so the shadow only appears around its edges. Shadows show best on logos and cut-out images with transparent backgrounds — remove the background first with the Background Remover.

Why is the downloaded image bigger than my original?

The tool adds transparent padding around the image so the shadow has room and is never cut off. The padding grows with the blur and offset you choose.

Should I download as PNG or JPG?

PNG, to keep the transparent padding — the shadow then floats on any background. JPG cannot be transparent, so the padding becomes white.

What settings look most natural?

A small offset down and right (around 10–15 pixels), a blur of 15–30 pixels, and a dark shadow at 30–50% opacity. Real shadows are soft and grey, not hard and black.

Can I add a shadow to text?

Yes, if the text is part of an image — a logo or a screenshot, for example. To add text with a shadow to a photo, use the Add Text to Image tool, which draws readable text with its own subtle outline.

What images work best with a drop shadow?

Transparent-background logos and cut-outs: the shadow follows the opaque shape, so a round logo gets a round shadow. On a full opaque photo the shadow only shows at the edges, because the whole frame is opaque.

Can I change the shadow colour?

Yes — the colour, offset, blur and opacity are all adjustable. A soft grey shadow suits most images; a coloured shadow can match a brand or a background.

What format is the result?

A transparent PNG, so the shadow and the image sit on a transparent background and can be placed on any page, slide or design without a white box around them.
