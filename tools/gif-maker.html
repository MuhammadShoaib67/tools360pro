How to use the GIF Maker

  1. Drop two or more images onto the box above, or click to browse. Each one becomes one frame.
  2. Drag the frames in the list to set the playback order — the top frame plays first.
  3. Set the frame delay (500 ms by default), the output width (480 px by default) and whether the GIF loops forever or plays once.
  4. Press Create GIF. Watch the preview, then download the .gif file. Your originals stay untouched.

How to make a GIF that looks good

The delay sets the mood: 100–200 ms per frame feels snappy and works for short reactions and demos, the 500 ms default suits a small slideshow, and 1000 ms or more reads as a slow step-by-step sequence. The width is a trade between size and sharpness — 480 px is a good default for chats and social posts, while 800 px keeps more detail for a larger embed at the cost of a bigger file.

Use frames that are the same size and orientation for the smoothest result: every frame is scaled to fit the first frame's shape, so a mix of portrait and landscape photos leaves white bars on some frames. Fewer frames also means a smaller file — a GIF stores every frame as a full picture, so 10 frames at 500 ms is usually livelier (and much lighter) than 50 frames doing the same job. If the result is too big, lower the width or drop a few middle frames and encode again.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. The GIF is encoded on a canvas in your browser. Nothing leaves your device.

Which image formats can I use as frames?

JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP, AVIF and SVG. Each frame is drawn onto a canvas at the width you choose — 480 pixels by default and 800 at most — keeping its aspect ratio.

Why do transparent areas turn white in my GIF?

Every frame is drawn onto a white background before encoding, so transparent parts of PNG or WebP images become white. That keeps the GIF small and playable in every app.
