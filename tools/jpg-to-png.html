Why convert JPG to PNG?

Every time a JPG is opened, edited and saved again, it is compressed again — and each round throws away a little more detail. That is fine for sharing a photo once, but it is a poor way to keep a file you plan to edit. Converting to PNG stops the cycle: PNG is lossless, so the picture you have now is preserved exactly, and any further editing starts from a clean copy instead of a copy that has already been compressed several times.

PNG is also the format many print shops, design tools and archives prefer, because it stores every pixel exactly as it is. If a photo is going into a design, a print job or a long-term archive, PNG is the safer container. Some application forms and older systems ask for PNG too. Converting is quick, and since the original JPG is never changed, there is nothing to lose by keeping both versions.

Converting to PNG does not add quality back

One honest point: converting a JPG to PNG cannot restore detail that JPG compression already removed. If a photo was saved at low quality and looks soft or blocky, the PNG will look exactly the same — only larger. Conversion preserves what is there; it does not improve it. Anyone promising that a converter will sharpen or enhance a JPG is selling something this kind of tool cannot do.

What conversion does give you is a stable file. From the PNG onwards, every edit and save keeps every pixel intact, which matters when a photo will be cropped, retouched or resized several times. Think of it as freezing the photo at its current quality instead of letting each save chip a little more away. If the JPG already looks good, the PNG will look identical — and it will stay that way.

When PNG is the wrong choice

PNG files are much larger than JPGs — often three to five times larger for a photo. That makes PNG a poor choice for sharing, uploading or putting on a website, where the extra size only slows things down. If the destination is a chat, an email or a web page, keep the JPG or convert to WebP instead, which is smaller than both.

Converting changes the format, not the dimensions: a 4000-pixel-wide JPG becomes a 4000-pixel-wide PNG. Your originals are never changed, so keep them. Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Conversion runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device, which is why this tool is safe for private photos.

Will converting to PNG improve the quality?

No. Conversion cannot restore detail that JPG compression already removed. It preserves the photo exactly as it is and stops further quality loss from repeated saves.

Why is the PNG file so much bigger?

PNG is lossless — it stores every pixel exactly, with no data thrown away. JPG achieves its small size by discarding detail. The larger file is the price of keeping every pixel intact.

Can I convert many JPG files at once?

Yes. Add as many JPG files as you like, convert them together, and download the results one by one or all together in a single ZIP file.

Will the PNG keep the same dimensions?

Yes. Converting changes the format, not the size of the picture. A 4000-pixel-wide JPG becomes a 4000-pixel-wide PNG. To change the dimensions, use the Resize Image tool.

Does the PNG gain transparency?

No. JPG has no transparency, so there is nothing to keep. The PNG will have a solid background, exactly like the JPG it came from.
