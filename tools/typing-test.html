What WPM actually measures

A "word" in typing tests is not a word from the dictionary — it is 5 characters, by convention. That is what makes WPM comparable between tests: a passage full of short words and a passage full of long ones both reduce to characters divided by 5, divided by the minutes spent. Net WPM counts only the characters you typed correctly, so it is the number that matters; gross WPM counts everything, mistakes included, and flatters fast but sloppy typing.

Accuracy is the other half of the story, and consistency is the part most tests ignore. Two typists can both score 50 WPM, one steady and one in bursts with long pauses — the steady one finishes real work faster, because pauses are where thinking, correcting and re-reading hide. The per-second graph shows your rhythm: a flat line means steady typing, spikes and dips mean bursts. A 2018 study (Dhakal et al.) measured an average of about 52 words per minute on a physical keyboard and about 36 on a phone, so if you score above 60 you are already faster than most people.

How to get faster (without practising wrong)

The fastest way to raise your WPM is not to type faster — it is to make fewer mistakes. Every error costs the time to notice it, delete it and retype, which is why accuracy and speed rise together in good typists and why chasing speed alone usually backfires. Practise at a pace where your accuracy stays above 95%, and let the speed follow: it rises on its own as the finger movements become automatic.

Short, regular practice beats long sessions. A 1-minute test a day builds the habit without tiring your hands, and the personal bests saved in this browser show whether you are actually improving or just having a good day. If you type on a phone, expect a lower number — the 36 WPM phone average exists because small screens and thumbs are slower than a full keyboard, not because phone typists are worse. Net WPM, gross WPM and the graph: what your result means

A WPM test online free usually reports one number, but this one reports two: gross WPM counts everything you typed, mistakes included, while net WPM counts only correct characters — a word is 5 characters, by convention — so the gap between them shows how much accuracy is costing you. Accuracy and CPM, characters per minute, sit alongside, and the per-second graph shows where you sped up and where you stumbled, second by second.

The test is passage-based: words appear exactly as shown, and you type them as they come — no random letters, no made-up words. One minute is the standard quick check, and longer tests give a steadier number because a single stumble matters less. Frequently asked questions

How is WPM calculated?

A “word” in typing tests is 5 characters, by convention. Net WPM is your correctly typed characters divided by 5, divided by the minutes spent. Gross WPM uses all characters you typed, right or wrong.

What is the difference between net and gross WPM?

Gross WPM counts everything you typed, including mistakes. Net WPM counts only correct characters, so it is the honest number: fast typing full of errors scores high on gross WPM and low on net WPM.

What is a good typing speed?

A 2018 study (Dhakal et al.) measured an average of about 52 words per minute on a physical keyboard and about 36 words per minute on a phone. Above 60 WPM is faster than most people; accuracy matters as much as speed, because errors cost more time than they save.

Why does the timer start on my first keystroke?

So the time you spend reading the words first does not count against you. The test measures typing, not reading — the clock starts the moment you type the first character.

Are my results stored anywhere?

Only in your own browser: your personal best for each mode is saved in localStorage so you can track your progress. Nothing is sent to any server.

How long should a typing test be?

One minute is the standard quick check and the most common choice. Longer tests — 3 to 5 minutes — give a steadier result, because a single stumble matters less over more words. Pick any length from 15 seconds to 5 minutes in time mode, or 10 to 100 words in words mode.

What is CPM in a typing test?

Characters per minute — the raw number of characters you typed in a minute. WPM divides that by five, the standard word length, so CPM and WPM always tell the same story in different units.

Does the test use real words or random letters?

Real words, in passages: the words appear exactly as shown and you type them as they come. That makes the result a fair measure of everyday typing — the kind you do in emails and documents — rather than a test of random keystrokes.
