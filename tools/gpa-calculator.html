Why one heavy course outweighs two light ones

GPA is a weighted average, and the weight is credit hours: an A in a 4-credit course lifts you four times as much as an A in a 1-credit lab, and a weak grade in a heavy course is equally hard to hide. That single fact drives most GPA strategy — protect the heavy courses first, because recovering from a bad one takes several perfect light ones.

This calculator uses the standard 4.0 scale found across US colleges and many international universities, with plus/minus steps. Use it two ways: backwards, to know exactly where you stand before results day panic sets in; and forwards, entering hoped-for grades on unfinished courses to see what a target GPA actually demands — often less perfection, applied in the right courses, than students fear.

Frequently asked questions

How is GPA calculated?

Each grade becomes grade points (A = 4.0, B = 3.0, and so on). Multiply each course's points by its credit hours, add them all up, and divide by the total credit hours — so a 4-credit course moves your GPA more than a 1-credit one.

Which grade scale does this use?

The standard US 4.0 scale with plus/minus steps: A 4.0, A− 3.7, B+ 3.3, B 3.0, B− 2.7, C+ 2.3, C 2.0, C− 1.7, D+ 1.3, D 1.0, F 0.0. Some schools weight A+ as 4.0 too — here A+ counts 4.0.

My school uses percentages. Can I still use it?

Yes — convert each percentage to the nearest letter first (roughly: 93–100 A, 90–92 A−, 87–89 B+, 83–86 B, 80–82 B−, 77–79 C+, 73–76 C, 70–72 C−, 67–69 D+, 65–66 D, below 65 F), or check your school's own table, which is the one that counts.

Can I plan a target GPA with this?

Yes — enter the courses you have finished, then add the remaining courses with the grades you hope to get. The live GPA shows exactly where that plan lands.
