The mid-market rate, explained

Every currency has two prices at any moment: the price at which banks buy it and the price at which they sell it. The mid-market rate is the midpoint between those two — the rate banks and currency markets use when they trade with each other. It is the fairest single number for a conversion, which is why this converter uses it as its reference for every pair.

The rates come from ExchangeRate-API's free endpoint, refreshed daily, with the US dollar as the base currency. Pairs that do not involve the dollar are worked out through it in two steps — first into dollars, then into the target currency. The time shown under the result is the last-updated time reported by the API itself, so you always know how fresh the figure is.

Why the bank's rate is never the same

A bank, a card network or a money changer has to make money on the exchange, so each one adds its own margin to the mid-market rate — and sometimes a flat fee on top. That is why the amount that finally lands in an account is usually a little less than the converter shows, and why the rate at an airport kiosk is often the worst of all. The converter's figure is the honest yardstick to compare those offers against.

If you are offline, the converter does not stop working: it falls back to the rates from its last successful fetch, stored in your browser, and shows you when they were saved. For travel budgeting, checking an invoice in another currency, or deciding whether a transfer quote is fair, a day-old mid-market rate is still a far better guide than guessing.

Frequently asked questions

Where do the exchange rates come from?

Rates are fetched from open.er-api.com, the free endpoint provided by ExchangeRate-API, with US dollars as the base currency. The “last updated” time shown under the result is the time reported by that API itself.

Why might the rate I get at a bank or money changer differ?

This converter uses mid-market style reference rates. Banks, card networks and money changers add their own margins and fees, so the amount you actually receive or pay will usually differ slightly.

What happens if I am offline?

The last successfully fetched rates are saved in your browser. If a new fetch fails and saved rates exist, the converter keeps working with those cached rates and tells you when they were saved.

How is a conversion between two non-USD currencies calculated?

Both rates are quoted against the US dollar, so the tool divides your amount by the “from” rate and multiplies by the “to” rate: amount ÷ rate(from) × rate(to).

Is my amount uploaded or stored?

Your amount is never sent anywhere — it is only used in calculations on this page. Only the public exchange-rate data is downloaded, and a copy of the rates (not your inputs) is cached in your browser for offline use.

How do I convert USD to PKR?

Select USD as the 'from' currency and PKR as the 'to' currency, enter the amount, and the converter shows the result at the live exchange rate from ExchangeRate-API. The rate updates each time you convert, so it reflects the current market rate.

How do I convert euros to dollars?

Pick EUR as the 'from' currency and USD as the 'to' currency, enter the amount in euros, and the result appears at the live rate. Swap the pair to convert dollars back to euros.

How often do the exchange rates update?

The rates are fetched live from ExchangeRate-API every time you convert, so each conversion uses a fresh rate. Exchange rates move through the day, so converting again later may give a slightly different result.
