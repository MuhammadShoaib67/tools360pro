Why convert WebP to JPG?

WebP is a web format, and the web is where it belongs — but images often need to travel further than a browser. Application forms, older photo editors, email clients, printers and plenty of office software still accept JPG and nothing else. A WebP saved from a website and then uploaded to a form is a common reason for a rejected submission. Converting to JPG removes the problem entirely, because JPG opens on every device and every app built in the last thirty years.

JPG is also the safer format to send to another person. You cannot be sure what their phone or computer runs, but you can be sure it opens JPG. For anything that leaves the web — a form, an email, a print, a chat with someone on an old device — JPG is the format that simply works.

What happens to transparency?

JPG cannot store transparency, so any transparent areas in your WebP are placed on a white background during conversion. For photos this makes no difference at all, because they have no transparent pixels. For logos and graphics with see-through backgrounds, the white fill changes how the image looks on a coloured page — the logo will sit inside a white box instead of blending in.

If transparency matters, convert to PNG instead, which keeps it. This converter is the right tool when the destination needs a JPG — a form, an email, an older app — and the picture does not rely on a transparent background. When in doubt, convert one file first and look at the result before converting the whole batch.

Quality and batch tips

The quality slider runs from 10% to 100% and controls how hard JPG compresses. The default of 92% is a sensible middle ground for most photos: the result looks identical to the original in normal viewing. Lower settings shrink the file further but soften fine detail — small text, hair and foliage are the first things to show it.

Converting changes the format, not the dimensions: a 2000-pixel-wide WebP becomes a 2000-pixel-wide JPG. Your originals are never changed, so keep them — the WebP is the smaller file and worth keeping for web use. For a whole folder of downloaded images, drop them all in at once and download everything as a ZIP.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Conversion runs on a canvas in your browser. Your images never leave your device, which is why this tool is safe for private photos.

Will converting to JPG reduce quality?

JPG is lossy, so some data is discarded — but at the default 92% quality the difference is not visible in normal viewing. Only very fine detail may soften slightly.

What happens to transparent parts of my WebP?

JPG cannot store transparency, so transparent areas are placed on a white background. If you need transparency, convert to PNG instead.

Can I convert many WebP files at once?

Yes. Add as many WebP files as you like, convert them together, and download the results one by one or all together in a single ZIP file.

Will the JPG keep the same dimensions?

Yes. Converting changes the format, not the size of the picture. A 2000-pixel-wide WebP becomes a 2000-pixel-wide JPG. To change the dimensions, use the Resize Image tool.

Why do some apps still not accept WebP?

WebP is newer than JPG and PNG, and older software was built before it existed. Many forms and apps only list JPG and PNG as accepted formats, which is exactly the problem this converter solves.
