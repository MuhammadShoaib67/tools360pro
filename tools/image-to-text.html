How to use the Image to Text Converter

  1. Drop an image onto the box above, or click to browse.
  2. Choose the language of the text in the image.
  3. Press Extract text and wait a few seconds while the OCR reads your image.
  4. Copy the text, or download it as a .txt file.

Getting the best results from OCR

OCR works best on clear, printed text: a straight-on photo or scan, evenly lit, with the words in sharp focus. If you are photographing a page, fill the frame with the text, avoid shadows and glare, and keep the page flat. Choosing the correct language matters just as much — the engine reads far more accurately when it knows which script and dictionary to expect, which is why Urdu, Hindi and Arabic are separate choices rather than one setting.

Common uses include turning a scanned letter into an editable document, copying text from a screenshot or a photo of a noticeboard, digitising old printed records, and grabbing the words from a meme or poster so you can quote or translate them. Because everything runs on your device, even sensitive documents — ID cards, bank letters, medical reports — never leave your hands.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. The OCR engine (Tesseract) runs entirely in your browser. Your image never leaves your device — only the OCR engine and the language data you choose are downloaded, the first time you use them.

Which languages are supported?

English, Urdu, Hindi, Arabic, French, Spanish and German. Pick the language of the text in your image before extracting — the right language makes a big difference to accuracy.

How accurate is the result?

Clear printed text from a sharp photo or scan is usually read very accurately. Blurry photos, low light, decorative fonts and handwriting are harder — handwriting in particular often gives poor results.

Can I extract text from a PDF?

Not directly — this tool reads images. Convert the PDF pages to images first with the PDF to Image tool, then run each image through here.

Why is the first extraction slow?

The first time, your browser downloads the OCR engine and the language data (a few MB). They are cached on your device afterwards, so later extractions start quickly.

What image formats can I use?

JPG, PNG, WebP and BMP images all work. A straight, well-lit photo of the page gives much better results than a tilted or shadowed one.
