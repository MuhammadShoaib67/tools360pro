Why convert SVG to PNG?

SVG is a vector format: it describes shapes with code, so it scales to any size without losing sharpness. That makes it perfect for logos and icons on the web — but many places still cannot use it. Chats, documents, emails, social media uploads and plenty of apps accept only pixel images like PNG and JPG. An SVG sent to someone on their phone may not even preview.

Converting to PNG turns the vector into an ordinary image that opens everywhere. The PNG is rendered at the SVG's own size — its declared width and height, or its viewBox dimensions — so a logo designed at 512 pixels comes out as a 512-pixel PNG. Transparency is kept, so a logo with a see-through background stays see-through. For a logo needed at a specific size, the Resize Image tool can adjust the PNG afterwards.

Size, transparency and the 4096-pixel cap

The PNG is rendered at the size the SVG declares. If the SVG has width and height attributes, those are used; otherwise the viewBox dimensions are used. SVGs with no size information at all are rendered at a sensible default. Very large SVGs are capped at 4096 pixels on the longest side, keeping the proportions, so the conversion stays fast and the file stays manageable.

Transparency is preserved: areas of the SVG that are see-through stay see-through in the PNG. If you need a solid background instead — for example a white background behind a logo — open the PNG in any editor and add one, or place the PNG on a coloured background where it will be used.

What this tool cannot do — an honest note

Browsers refuse to load external resources from a local SVG file, for security. If your SVG references images, fonts or stylesheets by URL or by file path, those parts will be missing from the PNG — the shapes will render, but linked images and custom fonts will not. The fix is to embed everything in the SVG first: convert text to paths and embed images as data, which most vector editors can do on export.

Animations are not preserved either: the PNG shows the SVG's first frame, as a still image. And very complex filters or effects may render slightly differently from one browser to another, because each browser has its own SVG engine. For ordinary logos, icons and illustrations — the files this tool is made for — the result matches what you see in the browser.

Frequently asked questions

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. Conversion runs on a canvas in your browser. Your files never leave your device, which is why this tool is safe for private logos and artwork.

What size will the PNG be?

The PNG is rendered at the SVG's own size — its width and height attributes, or its viewBox dimensions. Very large SVGs are capped at 4096 pixels on the longest side, keeping the proportions.

Does the PNG keep transparency?

Yes. Transparent areas of the SVG stay transparent in the PNG, so logos and icons keep their see-through backgrounds.

Why is part of my SVG missing from the PNG?

Browsers cannot load external resources from a local SVG file. If the SVG references images or fonts by URL or file path, those parts will be missing. Embed them in the SVG first — convert text to paths and embed images as data.

Can I convert many SVG files at once?

Yes. Add as many SVG files as you like, convert them together, and download the results one by one or all together in a single ZIP file.

Are animations preserved?

No. A PNG is a still image, so the conversion captures the SVG's first frame. Animated SVGs convert to a static picture.
