How to use the Wheel Spinner

  1. Type your items into the box, one per line — names, prizes, options, or anything you want to choose between. Four names are already loaded so you can try it right away.
  2. Click Spin the wheel. The wheel turns several times and slows down, then the pointer lands on the winner.
  3. Read the winner below the wheel. Tick Remove winner after spin if you want each spin to pick someone new — the winner is taken off the list automatically.
  4. Use Shuffle items to reorder the wheel, or Clear to start over with a fresh list.

How the wheel picks a winner

Each spin combines several full turns with a random extra rotation, then slows down with an easing curve so it feels like a real wheel losing momentum. The final resting angle is random — no one can predict it, and the pointer lands on whichever segment happens to be at the top when the wheel stops.

Every item on the wheel gets an equal-sized segment, so every item has an equal chance of winning. It does not matter where an item sits on the wheel or what order the items are in: the random rotation makes each segment equally likely to end up under the pointer.

Fairness: is the wheel random

The spin uses your browser's built-in random number generator to choose the extra rotation, which is the same source of randomness used for shuffling and games across the web. It is not a casino-grade random source, and this tool is not for gambling — but for giveaways, classroom picks and team decisions, it is as fair as a physical wheel.

One thing a digital wheel does better than a physical one: it cannot be nudged. A real wheel can be spun harder or softer to favour a segment; this wheel's final angle is chosen by the random number generator before the animation even starts, and the animation simply plays out that result.

Ways to use a spinning wheel

Giveaways are the classic use: put the entrants' names on the wheel, spin it live, and everyone can see the winner is chosen fairly. Teachers use it to pick students for questions, choose groups, or decide the order of presentations. Teams use it to pick who presents first, who takes the next support shift, or where to go for lunch.

It also works as a decision maker. Put your options on the wheel — the restaurants, the movies, the weekend plans — and let it choose. If the result disappoints you, that feeling is useful information: it tells you what you actually wanted, which is often the real answer.

Frequently asked questions

Is the wheel spinner fair?

Yes — every item gets an equal-sized segment and the final rotation is random, so each item has the same chance of winning. The wheel is fair for giveaways, classroom picks and team decisions.

How many items can I put on the wheel?

Between 2 and 50 items. Fewer than 2 cannot be spun, and more than 50 makes the segments too small to read. For very large lists, use the List Randomizer to shuffle them first.

Can I remove a winner so they cannot win twice?

Yes — tick Remove winner after spin and the winner is taken off the list automatically after each spin, so the next spin picks someone new.

Does the wheel remember my items?

No. Your list lives only in the text box on this page. Reloading the page resets it to the four sample names, so copy your list somewhere safe if you need it again.

Is anything I enter uploaded or stored?

No. The wheel runs entirely in your browser. Your list of names is never sent or stored anywhere.
