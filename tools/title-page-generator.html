One page, five different rules

A title page looks simple — a title, a name, a date — but every citation style places those pieces differently, and markers notice. APA 7 puts the title bold and centred in the upper half of the page, with the author, affiliation, course, instructor and date below it, and a page number in the top-right corner. Chicago puts the title about a third of the way down and the author details near the bottom, with no page number at all. MLA 9 officially has no separate title page: the name, instructor, course and date go in the top-left corner of the first page of the essay — though many instructors still ask for a title page, which is why this generator builds that format too.

The professional APA version is the most different of all. It drops the course details entirely and adds a running head — a shortened title in capital letters at the top of every page — plus an author note at the bottom of the title page. IEEE, meanwhile, does not use a separate title page either: the title block sits at the top of the first page of the paper, with the author names and affiliations directly underneath. The details that cost marks

Two small details catch more students than any other. The first is title case: in APA, Chicago and Harvard titles, major words are capitalised and short words — articles, short prepositions and short conjunctions — stay lowercase, unless they are the first or last word of the title. "The Effect of Sleep on Exam Performance" is right; "The Effect Of Sleep On Exam Performance" is not. The title-case check on this page spots the difference and suggests the corrected version.

The second is the running head. In a professional APA paper it is a shortened version of your title, in capital letters, and it must fit in 50 characters including spaces and punctuation — a limit that surprises almost everyone the first time. If your title is long, shorten it to its key words: "The Effect of Sleep on Exam Performance" becomes "SLEEP AND EXAM PERFORMANCE" (26 characters). The counter under the running-head field counts as you type and warns you past 50. Dates are simpler: write the date your instructor expects, in the format they expect — and if they did not say, the full date ("15 November 2026") is the safest choice.

Frequently asked questions

Which style should I choose?

Your instructor or syllabus decides. As a rough guide: APA 7 is common in psychology, education and the social sciences; MLA 9 in literature and the humanities; Chicago in history; IEEE in engineering; Harvard is widely used in the UK and Australia.

What is the difference between APA student and professional title pages?

The student version shows your course number and name, instructor and due date, and has no running head. The professional version has a running head and an author note instead, and no course details.

Does MLA 9 have a title page?

No — MLA 9 officially puts your name, instructor, course and date in the top-left corner of the first page of the essay, with the title centred below. Some instructors still ask for a separate title page, so this generator builds that format too.

What is a running head?

A shortened version of your paper's title, in capital letters, shown at the top of every page of a professional APA paper. It must be no more than 50 characters long, including spaces and punctuation.

Can I edit the downloaded Word file?
