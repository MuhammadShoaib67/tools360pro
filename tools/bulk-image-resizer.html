Why resize in bulk

Images rarely travel alone. A listing needs a dozen product photos, a blog post needs a set of illustrations, a photo album needs a hundred pictures shrunk for sharing. Resizing them one at a time is the kind of repetitive work that eats an afternoon — and the settings drift from file to file, so the results come out inconsistent.

Bulk resizing applies one setting to every file, so the whole set comes out uniform: the same width, the same quality, the same format. That consistency is what makes a product catalogue look professional and a photo gallery load evenly. It also saves the most precious resource involved — your time.

Percentage vs target width

Percentage is the right choice when your images are different sizes and you want them all smaller by the same amount: 50% halves a 4000-pixel photo to 2000 and a 2000-pixel photo to 1000. The set keeps its variety, just smaller — useful for archiving a mixed album.

Target width is the right choice when the destination wants one size: a listing that needs 1600-pixel images, a blog that displays at 800 pixels, a form with a maximum width. Every image comes out at exactly that width, with its height following its own aspect ratio — nothing is ever stretched. If the destination also wants a specific height, resize by width here, then use the Crop Image tool on the few that need it.

Formats, quality and the ZIP

Keeping the original format is usually right: JPGs stay JPGs, PNGs stay PNGs. But bulk resizing is also a good moment to standardise — converting a mixed set to JPG makes the whole batch small and uniform, which is what most uploads want. The quality slider applies to JPG and WebP output; 85% is a good default that keeps photos looking sharp while cutting the file size substantially.

The results arrive as a single ZIP file, with each image named after its original plus “-resized”. If two files would collide, a number is added automatically. Unzip anywhere — every operating system opens ZIP files without extra software. Your originals are never changed: the ZIP contains new files, and the files on your device stay exactly as they were.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Every image is resized on a canvas in your browser, and the ZIP is built on your device. Nothing is uploaded.

How many images can I resize at once?

There is no fixed limit, but very large batches use a lot of memory — each image is held in the browser while it is processed. For hundreds of very large photos, work in batches of 30–50 for the smoothest result.

Will my images be stretched?

No. Height always follows the aspect ratio — a percentage scales both sides equally, and a target width computes the height from the image's own shape. Images are never stretched or squashed.

What if one image cannot be read?

It is marked “could not be read” in the list and skipped in the ZIP. The rest are processed normally. The usual cause is a damaged file, or an AVIF or BMP image in a browser without support for it.

Can I remove one image from the list?

Yes — press the ✕ next to any file to remove it before downloading the ZIP.

What formats can the resized images be?

Keep each original format, or convert the whole batch to JPEG, WebP or PNG. JPG makes the smallest files for photos; PNG keeps transparency and loses nothing.
